About Lives of Scripture

Lives of Scripture is a reference profiling every person named in the Bible — patriarchs, judges, kings, prophets, apostles, and everyone else Scripture names, from Adam to the closing chapters of Revelation. It exists to help worship leaders, Bible teachers, and families answer "who's who" in Scripture, see how everyone connects by genealogy and relationship, and see what each person's story reveals about God's redemptive plan.

This is a sibling site to Lives of Faith, which covers historical Christians from church history onward. Lives of Scripture covers the biblical text itself.

Two kinds of entries

Most of the roughly 3,000 named individuals in Scripture appear in a single genealogy verse with no narrative to tell. This site distinguishes two tiers:

  • Full entries — for people with enough documented narrative in Scripture to support a real story. These include a life story, family story, an illustration, and genealogy/relationship connections.
  • Name-only entries — everyone else: a name, its alternate spellings, the verses where they appear, and their place in the genealogy graph. These exist so the connections between people stay complete, even where Scripture gives no story to tell.

Name Spelling

The New American Standard Bible (NASB) is this site's default for how a person's name is spelled. You can also view names as they appear in the King James Version (KJV) — useful because the KJV often spells the same person's name differently in the Old and New Testaments (Elijah/Elias, Isaiah/Esaias, Noah/Noe). No verse text is quoted or stored anywhere on this site, in any translation — references are given as chapter and verse only (e.g. "Genesis 12:1–3"), which you can look up in whatever translation you prefer.

Sources

  • The biblical text itself — the source for all narrative content and factual claims about a person.
  • Easton's Bible Dictionary, Smith's Bible Dictionary, and the International Standard Bible Encyclopedia (ISBE) — all public domain — for historical and cultural background supplementing the text.
  • Wikipedia — background and context only, never as the source of a narrative claim the biblical text itself should be supplying.
  • Genealogy and family-relationship data is seeded from BibleData by Brady Stephenson, used under a CC BY 4.0 license.

What this site doesn't do

  • It doesn't sanitize Scripture's own heroes. David's adultery and murder, Solomon's idolatry, Noah's drunkenness, Peter's denial, Jonah's rebellion — these are the text, not scandals to soften or omit.
  • It doesn't state post-biblical legend as fact. Later Jewish or church tradition about a person is only ever presented as tradition, clearly labeled, never as something Scripture itself records.
  • It doesn't claim historical accuracy for portrait illustrations — there is no contemporary-portrait record for any biblical figure. Illustrations are simple, AI-generated line art, clearly labeled as such, not claims of likeness.
