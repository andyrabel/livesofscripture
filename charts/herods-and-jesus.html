The Herods, John the Baptist & Jesus

Six rulers of the Herod family appear in the New Testament, spanning nearly a century and a half — and it is easy to lose track of which one is which. This chart lays their reigns against the lifetimes of John the Baptist and Jesus, with each Herod's Scripture references shown beside the name. Numbered markers place eleven Gospel and Acts events on the timeline; a faint line drops from each one to the Herod it involved, and the same numbers are listed in full below the chart. Bars and markers are clickable; hover or focus a bar for exact dates.

Herod ruler (reign) John the Baptist (life) Jesus (life) Numbered Gospel & Acts event (drops to the Herod involved)

New Testament dates rest on Roman-era cross-references and are far firmer than Old Testament chronology, but two points are genuinely disputed and marked “c.” throughout: the death of Herod the Great (most place it in 4 BC; some argue 1 BC) and the date of the crucifixion (c. AD 30, though a significant number of evangelical scholars hold AD 33). The chart follows the more common 4 BC / AD 30 framework; shifting to the alternative moves John's and Jesus's bars a few years later but changes none of the relationships the chart shows.

Gospel & Acts events involving a Herod

  1. c. 6 BC An angel tells Zacharias that his son John will be born — dated “in the days of Herod, king of Judea.”Herod the Great · Luke 1:5-13
  2. c. 5 BC Jesus is born in Bethlehem “in the days of Herod the king.”Herod the Great · Matthew 2:1
  3. c. 5 BC Wise men from the east ask Herod where the newborn king of the Jews is, then find and worship the child.Herod the Great · Matthew 2:1-12
  4. c. 4 BC Herod orders every boy in Bethlehem under two years old killed; an angel warns Joseph, who takes the family to Egypt.Herod the Great · Matthew 2:13-18
  5. c. 3 BC After Herod's death Joseph brings the family back, but settles in Nazareth on hearing Archelaus now rules Judea.Herod Archelaus · Matthew 2:19-23
  6. c. AD 28 John the Baptist begins preaching in the wilderness, dated by the rule of Antipas in Galilee and Philip in Iturea.Herod Antipas · Luke 3:1-3
  7. c. AD 29 Antipas arrests John for condemning his marriage to Herodias, his brother's wife.Herod Antipas · Luke 3:19-20
  8. c. AD 30 At Herodias's prompting, Antipas has John beheaded after her daughter dances at his birthday feast.Herod Antipas · Mark 6:21-29
  9. c. AD 30 Pilate sends Jesus to Antipas, who questions him, gets no answer, treats him with contempt, and sends him back.Herod Antipas · Luke 23:6-12
  10. AD 44 Agrippa I kills the apostle James, imprisons Peter, and is struck down at Caesarea after accepting the crowd's worship.Herod Agrippa I · Acts 12:1-23
  11. c. AD 59 Paul makes his defense before Agrippa II and Bernice at Caesarea; the king says Paul could have been set free.Herod Agrippa II · Acts 25:23-26:32
