Hosea (M)

Name means “Salvation” — Hebrew
Prophesied to Israel, c. 753 BC–715 BC

First named in Hosea 1:1-2

OT Divided Monarchy

Hosea ministered to the northern kingdom of Israel during its final, spiritually corrupt decades, and his own marriage became the central message of his book: God commanded him to marry Gomer, a woman who would prove unfaithful, as a living picture of Israel's persistent unfaithfulness despite His covenant love (Hosea 1:2-3). Their children were given prophetic names reflecting Israel's coming judgment — Jezreel, for bloodshed to be repaid; Lo-ruhamah, "not pitied"; and Lo-ammi, "not my people" (Hosea 1:4-9). Yet the book turns on remarkable grace: God commanded Hosea to go again and love his wife, buying her back from slavery even after her repeated unfaithfulness, exactly as God kept pursuing an unfaithful Israel (Hosea 3:1-3). Hosea's poetry captures both God's grief over Israel's idolatry — "they sacrifice... but I have no delight in them" (Hosea 8:13) — and His unbreakable tenderness — "How can I give you up, Ephraim?... My heart recoils within me" (Hosea 11:8). Hosea's message, "I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice," was quoted by Jesus against religious hardness of heart, and his line about God calling His son out of Egypt is applied by Matthew to the child Jesus (Hosea 6:6; 11:1; Matthew 2:15; 9:13).

Hosea was a prophet whose own marriage became a living picture of God's message. God asked Hosea to marry a woman named Gomer, even though God knew she wouldn't stay faithful to him — this was meant to show how Israel kept turning away from God even though He loved them faithfully. Even after Gomer left, God told Hosea to go find her, forgive her, and bring her back home again, paying to free her. This showed Israel — and shows us — that God keeps loving and welcoming people back, even after they've made big mistakes and wandered away. Hosea's most famous words are that God wants our hearts to truly love Him, not just for us to go through religious motions. His story is really a story about God's patient, forgiving love.

Thought for Today

Hosea’s story teaches us about prophecy; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Hosea 1:1-2; Romans 9:25
