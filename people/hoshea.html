Hoshea (M)

Name means “Salvation” — Hebrew
Reigned over Israel, c. 732 BC–722 BC

First named in 2 Kings 15:30

OT Divided Monarchy

Hoshea son of Elah seized Israel's throne through assassination, conspiring against King Pekah, striking him down, and becoming king in his place (2 Kings 15:30). He reigned nine years in Samaria as Israel's last king, doing evil in the LORD's sight -- though, Scripture notes almost as a footnote, "not as the kings of Israel who were before him" (17:1-2). Hoshea became a vassal of Assyria's Shalmaneser, paying tribute to avoid invasion, but then gambled on rebellion: he sent secret envoys to "So, king of Egypt," seeking an alliance, and stopped paying Assyria's tribute (17:3-4). Shalmaneser discovered the conspiracy, seized Hoshea, and bound him in prison, then marched on Israel itself, besieging Samaria for three years (17:4-5). In Hoshea's ninth year, Samaria fell; Assyria deported the northern kingdom's population, resettling foreign peoples in their place -- the end of the ten northern tribes as an independent nation (17:6). The chapter immediately follows this with a long theological verdict: Israel's exile came "because the sons of Israel had sinned against the LORD their God" through generations of idolatry, ignoring prophet after prophet's warning (17:7-23). Hoshea's brief, unheroic reign -- born in violence, ended in captivity -- closes the book on the northern kingdom exactly as its prophets, including Hosea, had long warned would happen (Hosea 10:15).

Hoshea was the last king of Israel, the northern kingdom made up of ten of Israel's twelve tribes. He became king in a violent way -- by killing the previous king, Pekah. The Bible says Hoshea did what was evil in God's sight, though not as badly as some earlier kings of Israel had. For a while Hoshea paid tribute money to the powerful king of Assyria to keep peace, but then he secretly tried to get help from Egypt instead and stopped paying. When the king of Assyria found out, he was furious. Assyria's army surrounded Israel's capital city, Samaria, for three years, and finally captured it. Israel's people were taken from their land into exile, and the northern kingdom came to an end -- an ending God's prophets had long warned was coming.

Thought for Today

Hoshea's rebellion and Israel's long unfaithfulness ended the northern kingdom in exile, just as the prophets warned. Jesus is the true Shepherd-King who gathers His scattered people home.

References: 2 Kings 15:30; 2 Kings 17:1-6; 2 Kings 18:1-10; Hosea 10:15
