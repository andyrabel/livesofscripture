Ahasuerus (M)

Name means “Prince; head; chief” — Persian

First named in Ezra 4:6

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Ahasuerus reigned as king over Persia's vast empire, "from India to Cush," ruling from the citadel of Susa (Esther 1:1). After Queen Vashti refused to appear at his banquet before his officials, Ahasuerus deposed her on his advisors' counsel and later selected Esther -- a Jewish orphan raised by her cousin Mordecai, who had concealed her ethnicity -- as his new queen through an empire-wide search (Esther 2:1-17). When Haman, the king's highest official, was enraged that Mordecai would not bow to him, he manipulated Ahasuerus into approving an irrevocable decree to annihilate the Jewish people throughout the empire, offering a huge sum for the privilege (3:1-11) -- the king approved it without apparently grasping its full weight or Esther's own identity. At great personal risk, Esther approached the king uninvited, exposed Haman's plot at a banquet, and revealed she herself was Jewish (7:1-6). Ahasuerus's fury turned on Haman, who was hanged on the very gallows he had built for Mordecai (7:9-10). The king then permitted the Jews to defend themselves against the decree's still-standing effect and elevated Mordecai to Haman's former position (8:1-17). Ahasuerus comes across throughout Esther as a powerful but impulsive and easily swayed ruler, whose decisions were repeatedly shaped by the people around him -- for both harm and eventual good.

Ahasuerus was a powerful king who ruled over the huge Persian Empire from his palace in the city of Susa. Early in his reign, he removed Queen Vashti from her position because she refused one of his commands. Later, after a search across the kingdom, he chose a young Jewish woman named Esther to be his new queen, not realizing she was Jewish. One of the king's officials, a proud man named Haman, tricked Ahasuerus into signing a terrible decree to destroy all the Jewish people in the empire. When Esther bravely told the king the truth, that she was Jewish and that Haman's plan would destroy her people too, Ahasuerus was furious with Haman and had him punished instead. The king then let the Jewish people defend themselves, and Esther's cousin Mordecai was honored in Haman's place.

Thought for Today

Ahasuerus’s story teaches us about Persian empire; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
