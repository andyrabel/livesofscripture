Nicolas (M)

First named in Acts 6:5

NT Apostolic

Nicolas was one of the seven men Acts 6 names as chosen by the Jerusalem church to administer the daily distribution of food after Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked (Acts 6:1-6). The apostles asked the congregation to select seven men "of good reputation, full of the Spirit and of wisdom," and the church chose Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolas; the apostles prayed and laid hands on them (6:3-6). Scripture adds one detail found for none of the other six: Nicolas is called "a proselyte of Antioch" (6:5) -- meaning he was a Gentile who had first converted to Judaism before coming to faith in Christ, making him a notable early example of Gentile inclusion in the church even before Paul's missionary work formally opened that door. Later church tradition, from the second century onward, associated a "Nicolas" with the Nicolaitans, a group whose practices Christ condemns in Revelation 2:6 and 2:15 -- but this identification with the Nicolas of Acts 6 is not stated in Scripture and remains disputed among interpreters, some ancient and modern alike defending his character while later legend accuses him of moral compromise. Scripture itself records nothing beyond his selection as one of the seven trusted servants.

Nicolas was one of seven men chosen by the early church in Jerusalem to make sure widows were being fairly cared for and given food each day. The apostles wanted to focus on teaching and prayer, so they asked the church to pick trustworthy, Spirit-filled men for this job, and Nicolas was one of them. The Bible tells us one extra detail about him: he came from the city of Antioch and had originally converted to Judaism before believing in Jesus. The apostles prayed for Nicolas and the other six and placed their hands on him, setting him apart to serve the church.

Thought for Today

Nicolas, a Gentile convert to Judaism, was trusted to serve the church before Paul's mission formally reached the Gentiles. Jesus was already gathering outsiders into His family.
