Adoram (M)

Also called: Hadoram
Name means “Their beauty; their power” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Samuel 20:24

OT Divided Monarchy

Adoram (also called Hadoram) served as the official in charge of forced labor first under King David (2 Samuel 20:24) and continued in that role into the reign of Solomon's son Rehoboam. When Rehoboam became king, the northern tribes gathered at Shechem and asked him to lighten the heavy labor and taxation Solomon had imposed. Rejecting the counsel of his father's older advisors, Rehoboam followed younger counselors and answered harshly, threatening even heavier burdens (1 Kings 12:1-15). In the fury that followed, Israel rejected the house of David outright, crying "To your tents, O Israel!" Rehoboam then made the disastrous choice to send Adoram -- the very symbol of the forced labor the people hated -- to address the rebelling crowd. They stoned him to death on the spot, and Rehoboam himself barely escaped, fleeing to Jerusalem in his chariot (12:18). Adoram's death marks the violent, immediate trigger of the kingdom's permanent split into Israel and Judah. His decades of loyal administrative service under two or three kings ended as collateral damage in Rehoboam's political failure -- a reminder of how one leader's pride and poor judgment can cost others their lives.

Adoram, also called Hadoram, was in charge of organizing work crews for King David and later for King Rehoboam, Solomon's son. When Rehoboam became king, the people of Israel were tired of hard work and heavy taxes, and they asked him to make things easier. Rehoboam refused and spoke harshly to them instead. He sent Adoram to talk with the angry crowd, but the people were so furious that they attacked and killed him with stones. Rehoboam had to rush away in his chariot. This sad event was part of the kingdom of Israel splitting into two separate kingdoms.

Thought for Today

Adoram’s story teaches us about labor administration; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Samuel 20:24; 1 Kings 12:18; 2 Chronicles 10:18
