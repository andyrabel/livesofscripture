Jeduthun (M)

First named in 1 Chronicles 9:16

OT United Monarchy

When David brought the ark of the covenant to Jerusalem and organized worship there, he appointed Jeduthun, along with Asaph and Heman, over the service of song (1 Chronicles 16:41-42; 25:1). Jeduthun's sons were set apart "to prophesy with the lyre in giving thanks and praising the LORD" (25:1, 3), and his family became one of the twenty-four courses of Levitical musicians who ministered in rotation at the sanctuary (25:1-6; 9:16). Three psalms carry his name in their titles -- Psalms 39, 62, and 77 -- traditionally understood as psalms performed "according to Jeduthun," possibly indicating a musical style or choir associated with his line. Jeduthun's family's ministry outlasted his own lifetime by centuries: his descendants sang at the dedication of Solomon's temple (2 Chronicles 5:12), took part in Hezekiah's reconsecration of the temple (29:14), and served again at Josiah's great Passover celebration, where Jeduthun himself is remembered by the striking title "the king's seer" (35:15) -- language elsewhere reserved for prophets who spoke on the king's behalf. Jeduthun's story is less a single dramatic episode than a lifelong, generational vocation: leading God's people in sung worship that Scripture treats as itself a form of prophetic proclamation, faithfully carried on by his family long after him.

Jeduthun was one of three men King David chose to lead the music and worship at God's house in Jerusalem, along with Asaph and Heman. Jeduthun and his sons played instruments and sang, and the Bible even says they "prophesied" with their lyres -- using music to praise God and proclaim His truth. Three psalms in the book of Psalms mention Jeduthun by name in their titles. Long after Jeduthun's own lifetime, his family kept serving as musicians for generations, including during the temple celebrations under later kings like Hezekiah and Josiah, when Jeduthun is remembered with the special title "the king's seer."

Thought for Today

Jeduthun led God's people in sung worship that Scripture calls a form of prophecy. Jesus is the one our worship has always pointed toward, in song or word.
