Deborah (F)

Name means “Word; thing; a bee” — Hebrew

First named in Judges 4:1-24

OT Judges

Deborah, a prophetess, was already judging Israel -- settling disputes beneath a palm tree in the hill country of Ephraim -- when Israel had been oppressed for twenty years by Jabin, king of Canaan, and his army commander Sisera, who had 900 iron chariots (Judges 4:1-5). Deborah summoned Barak and relayed God's specific command: gather ten thousand men at Mount Tabor, and God would draw Sisera's army to the Kishon river and give Israel the victory (4:6-7). Barak agreed only on the condition that Deborah go with him, to which she consented, though she told him plainly that the honor of the victory would then go to a woman instead of to him (4:8-9) -- a prophecy fulfilled not by Deborah but by Jael, who killed the fleeing Sisera in her tent (4:17-22). Israel's forces routed Sisera's army completely at Deborah's word (4:14-16). Afterward, Deborah and Barak together sang an extended victory song, one of the oldest poetic texts in Scripture, recounting God's intervention, praising Jael's act, and closing with a prayer that all God's enemies would perish while those who love Him would 'be like the rising of the sun in its might' (Judges 5:1-31). Deborah's leadership as judge and prophetess, in a period otherwise marked by repeated cycles of Israel's unfaithfulness, stands as a clear instance of God raising up capable, faithful leadership wherever He chose.

Deborah was a prophetess and a judge, which meant she was one of the leaders God raised up to guide Israel and settle disputes. She lived during a time when Israel was being oppressed by a Canaanite king with a powerful army. Deborah called for a man named Barak to lead Israel's soldiers into battle against that army, promising him that God would give Israel the victory. When Barak asked Deborah to come with him, she agreed, and God did give Israel a great victory that day. Afterward, Deborah and Barak sang a song together praising God for what He had done. Deborah's story shows a wise, courageous leader who trusted God completely.

Thought for Today

Deborah trusted God's word completely and led Israel to a victory only God could win. Jesus leads us to a victory over sin we could never win ourselves.

References: Judges 4:1-24; Judges 5:1-31
