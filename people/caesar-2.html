Caesar (M)

Name means “Title of Roman emperors; etymology disputed, traditionally linked to Latin for "hairy" or "cut"” — Latin

First named in Acts 25:8-25

NT Apostolic

When the apostle Paul, arrested in Jerusalem, faced a plot by Jewish leaders who wanted him tried in Jerusalem so they could ambush and kill him along the way, he exercised his right as a Roman citizen and appealed his case directly to the emperor: "I am standing before Caesar's tribunal, where I ought to be tried... I appeal to Caesar" (Acts 25:8-11). Once made, the appeal was binding -- Governor Festus declared, "You have appealed to Caesar, to Caesar you shall go" (25:12), and later summarized the situation to King Agrippa, still uncertain what charge to send with him (25:25-27; 26:32). The appeal set in motion Paul's long voyage to Rome, including a violent storm and shipwreck, with Paul reassuring his fellow travelers that an angel had told him he must stand before Caesar (27:24). Once in Rome, Paul lived under guard for two years awaiting his hearing, during which he continued preaching and teaching about Jesus openly (28:16-31). Writing from that Roman imprisonment, Paul later sent greetings from believers, "especially those of Caesar's household" (Philippians 4:22) -- evidence that the gospel had reached into the emperor's own staff. Caesar himself never appears as a character, but his court is the destination that shapes the entire second half of Acts.

After Paul was arrested in Jerusalem on false charges, Jewish leaders wanted him handed over so they could kill him along the way. As a Roman citizen, Paul had a special right: he could appeal directly to Caesar, the emperor in Rome, to have his case heard there instead. Paul used that right, saying, "I appeal to Caesar!" The Roman governor agreed he had to be sent. This decision meant Paul was put on a ship and, after a shipwreck and many adventures, finally arrived in Rome to await trial. There, even under guard, Paul kept teaching people about Jesus, including some who worked in Caesar's own household.

Thought for Today

Caesar’s story teaches us about Roman law; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Acts 25:8-25; Acts 26:32; Acts 27:24; Acts 28:19; Philippians 4:22
