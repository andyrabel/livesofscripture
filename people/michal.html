Michal (F)

Name means “Who is perfect?” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Samuel 14:49

OT United Monarchy

Michal, Saul's younger daughter, loved David, and Saul gave her to him in marriage, hoping the arrangement -- and a dangerous bride price of Philistine foreskins -- would get David killed (1 Samuel 18:20-27). When Saul later sent men to kill David in his own house, Michal helped him escape through a window and placed a household idol in his bed to buy time, then lied to her father's men to protect her husband's life (19:11-17). While David was a fugitive, Saul gave Michal to another man, Paltiel, who wept as she was taken from him (25:44). Once David was established as king, he demanded Michal's return as a condition of peace negotiations with Saul's remaining house, and she was taken from Paltiel and brought back to David (2 Samuel 3:13-16). When the ark of the covenant was brought into Jerusalem and David danced before it with unrestrained joy, Michal watched from a window and "despised him in her heart," later mocking him sharply for exposing himself "as one of the foolish ones" might (6:16, 20). David rebuked her sharply in return, reminding her it was before the LORD that he had celebrated. Scripture closes her story with a stark, quiet line: "Michal the daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death" (6:23) -- a marriage that began in love ending in permanent estrangement.

Michal was King Saul's daughter, and she loved David and became his wife. When Saul grew jealous and tried to have David killed, Michal helped David escape out a window, even tricking her father's men with a decoy in the bed to buy David time. But Saul later gave Michal to another man while David was on the run. Years later, once David had become king, he required that Michal be brought back to him. When David danced joyfully before the ark of the covenant as it entered Jerusalem, Michal watched from a window and despised him for it in her heart, feeling he had acted undignified for a king. The two grew apart after that, and Michal never had children of her own, closing out her story on a sad note.

Thought for Today

Michal’s story teaches us about love turned bitter; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 1 Samuel 14:49; 1 Samuel 18:20-28; 1 Samuel 19:11-17; 1 Samuel 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:13-14; 2 Samuel 6:16-23; 1 Chronicles 15:29
