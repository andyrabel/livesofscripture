Abimelech (M)

Name means “Father of the king” — Hebrew

First named in Judges 8:31

OT Judges

Abimelech was Gideon's son by a concubine from Shechem (Judges 8:31). After Gideon's death, he persuaded Shechem's leaders to back him as king, then used their silver to hire reckless men and murdered sixty-nine of his seventy brothers on one stone, all but the youngest, Jotham, who escaped and hid (9:1-5). Jotham publicly denounced Shechem with a parable of trees choosing a worthless bramble as king, warning that fire would consume both Abimelech and Shechem if their alliance was not made in good faith (9:7-20). God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and Shechem's leaders, and the city rebelled under Gaal; Abimelech crushed the revolt, destroyed Shechem, sowed it with salt, and burned a thousand people alive who had barricaded themselves in the temple of El-berith (9:22-49). Pressing his campaign to Thebez, he besieged a fortified tower there, but as he approached to burn its door, a woman dropped an upper millstone on his head and crushed his skull. Mortally wounded, he ordered his armor-bearer to run him through so it would not be said a woman killed him, and he died (9:50-54). Scripture states plainly that both his crime against his brothers and Shechem's treachery were repaid by God exactly as Jotham had cursed (9:56-57).

Abimelech was one of Gideon's sons. After Gideon died, Abimelech wanted to be king, even though God had never set up a king in Israel that way. He convinced the leaders of Shechem to support him, and he did a terrible thing to remove any rivals for power. Only one brother, Jotham, escaped, and Jotham warned the people that trusting Abimelech would end badly. Sure enough, Abimelech's rule fell apart within a few years, ending in a battle where he was fatally wounded. His story is one of the Bible's clearest warnings about grabbing power through violence instead of trusting God's timing.

Thought for Today

Abimelech’s story warns us about ambition and violence; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Judges 8:31; Judges 9:1-56; Judges 10:1
