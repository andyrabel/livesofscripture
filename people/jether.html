Jether (M)

First named in Judges 8:20

OT Judges

After Gideon's small army routed the Midianites and captured their two kings, Zebah and Zalmunna, at Karkor, Gideon brought the captives back and told his firstborn son, Jether, "Rise, kill them." Scripture does not hide what happened next: "the youth did not draw his sword, for he was afraid, because he was still a youth" (Judges 8:20). Zebah and Zalmunna, watching, told Gideon plainly, "Rise up yourself and kill us; for as the man is, so is his strength" -- an implicit judgment that only a grown warrior's hand was fit for the task. Gideon then killed them himself, along with taking the crescent ornaments from their camels' necks (8:21). Jether's single appearance is a small, honest detail inside a much larger account of his father's victory -- Scripture does not smooth over the fact that a young man froze in a moment meant to prove his readiness. Unlike so many other biblical "firstborn son" notices that carry only a name, Jether's brief scene captures a real, relatable human moment: the gap between what a father hopes his son is ready for, and what fear actually allows in the moment itself.

After defeating the Midianite army, Gideon captured two enemy kings, Zebah and Zalmunna. Gideon told his oldest son, Jether, to kill them himself, so that the deed would be done by someone from his own family instead of a stranger. But Jether was still just a young man, and he was too afraid to draw his sword. Zebah and Zalmunna themselves told Gideon that a grown man's strength was needed for the task. So Gideon killed the kings himself. Jether's fear is a small, honest moment inside a much bigger story about how following God still takes real courage.

Thought for Today

Jether froze in fear when told to kill the captured kings. Jesus felt real fear in Gethsemane too, yet did not draw back from what awaited him.
