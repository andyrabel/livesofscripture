Gaius (M)

NT Apostolic

Gaius is named by Paul in his letter to the Romans, written from Corinth, as 'my host and host of the whole church' (Romans 16:23) -- language suggesting Gaius had a home large enough, and a generosity great enough, to provide a regular gathering place for the entire Corinthian congregation, not merely for Paul personally. This same generosity is reflected in 1 Corinthians, where Paul, addressing concerns about factionalism tied to who had baptized whom, recalls, 'I thank God that I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius' (1 Corinthians 1:14) -- meaning Gaius was one of the earliest and most notable converts in the Corinthian church, personally baptized by Paul himself. A Gaius is also mentioned in Acts as one of Paul's traveling companions from Macedonia who was seized by the crowd during the riot incited by Demetrius the silversmith in Ephesus, dragged into the theater along with Aristarchus while the mob shouted in confusion for about two hours (Acts 19:29, 32-34); many identify this as the same well-traveled and evidently well-resourced believer. Whether one man or more than one shared this common name across these accounts, Gaius stands in Scripture as a model of a believer whose home, generosity, and personal risk were placed fully at the service of the wider church and of Paul's own ministry.

Gaius was a believer in Corinth known for his generous hospitality -- Paul describes him as host not just to himself but to the whole church there, meaning Gaius likely opened his home for the believers in Corinth to gather and worship together. Paul mentions that he personally baptized Gaius, one of just a few people he baptized directly in that city. Gaius may also be the same traveling companion of Paul's who was swept up in the dangerous riot in Ephesus. Gaius's story is a warm example of using one's home and resources generously to serve the whole church, not just individual guests.

References: Acts 19:29; Romans 16:23; 1 Corinthians 1:14
