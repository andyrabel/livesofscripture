Lazarus

NT Gospels

The brother of Martha and Mary of Bethany, a close friend of Jesus, whose death and four-day entombment Jesus reversed by raising him back to life, the last and greatest of His public signs before His own death.

For younger readers

Lazarus lived in the village of Bethany with his two sisters, Martha and Mary, and the family was close friends with Jesus. When Lazarus became seriously ill, his sisters sent word to Jesus, expecting Him to come quickly and heal him. Instead, Jesus waited two extra days before traveling there, and by the time He arrived, Lazarus had already died and been in the tomb four days. Both sisters told Jesus that if He had come sooner, Lazarus would not have died. Jesus wept at the tomb, deeply moved by their grief, even though He already knew what He was about to do. He ordered the stone rolled away, called out, "Lazarus, come forth!" and Lazarus walked out of the tomb alive, still wrapped in his grave clothes. This astonishing miracle showed Jesus's power over death itself and pointed forward to His own resurrection.

Life Story

Lazarus of Bethany, brother of Martha and Mary, was someone Jesus loved deeply and personally (John 11:3, 5, 36). When Lazarus fell seriously ill, his sisters sent word to Jesus, expecting Him to come at once, but Jesus deliberately stayed two more days where He was, telling His disciples the illness was "for the glory of God" (11:1-6). By the time Jesus arrived in Bethany, Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days -- long enough, by Jewish custom of the time, that no doubt remained he was truly dead (11:17, 39). Both Martha and Mary independently told Jesus the same thing: "if You had been here, my brother would not have died" -- grief mixed with confused faith (11:21, 32). Jesus, though He already intended to raise Lazarus, was "deeply moved" and "wept" at the tomb, sharing fully in the family's sorrow even while about to end it (11:33-35). He had the stone rolled away over Martha's objection about the smell, prayed aloud to the Father, and cried out, "Lazarus, come forth!" -- and the dead man walked out, still bound in his grave wrappings (11:38-44). The miracle, the last and greatest of Jesus's public signs in John's Gospel, so alarmed the religious leaders that it precipitated their final plot to kill Jesus, and later Lazarus too, since his life was drawing many to believe (11:45-53; 12:9-11). Lazarus's raising previews the resurrection Jesus would accomplish only days later.

First named in Luke 16:20-25
