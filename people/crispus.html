Crispus (M)

Name means “Curled” — Latin

First named in Acts 18:8

NT Apostolic

Crispus served as the ruler of the synagogue in Corinth, a position of real religious authority and respect within the Jewish community there, when Paul arrived and began reasoning in the synagogue every Sabbath, trying to persuade both Jews and Greeks (Acts 18:1-4). Luke records that Crispus 'believed in the Lord with his whole household,' and that 'many of the Corinthians when they heard were believing and being baptized' -- Crispus's conversion evidently carried significant weight and encouraged others toward faith as well (18:8). This came at a moment of real opposition, since some in the synagogue were resisting and blaspheming Paul's message, making Crispus's public conversion as the synagogue's own leader a striking and costly public stand for the gospel in that setting (18:6-8). Later, writing to the Corinthian church amid concerns about factional loyalty to whoever had baptized a given believer, Paul specifically recalled, 'I thank God that I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius,' explaining that Christ had sent him to preach the gospel rather than to baptize, so that no one could claim to have been baptized specifically in Paul's own name (1 Corinthians 1:14-17). Crispus's inclusion in this short, specific list confirms his conversion was well known and significant enough in the Corinthian church's memory that Paul could reference it years later as an established fact requiring no further explanation.

Crispus was the leader of the synagogue in the city of Corinth. When the apostle Paul came and taught there, Crispus believed in the Lord Jesus, and remarkably, his entire household believed along with him. Many other Corinthians who heard Paul's teaching believed and were baptized too. Paul later mentioned that he personally baptized Crispus, one of just a few people he baptized directly in Corinth, since Paul's main calling was to preach the gospel rather than to do the baptizing himself. Crispus's conversion, as a respected synagogue leader, was a significant and encouraging moment for the young church in Corinth.

Thought for Today

Crispus, the synagogue leader, believed in Jesus with his whole household despite fierce opposition around him. May we stand for Jesus too, even when it costs us.

References: Acts 18:8; 1 Corinthians 1:14
