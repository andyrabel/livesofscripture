Asher (M)

First named in Genesis 30:13

OT Patriarchal

Asher was the second son born to Zilpah, Leah's maidservant, during the years Jacob's wives competed to give him children (Genesis 30:12-13). At his birth Leah declared, "Happy am I! For women will call me happy," and named him Asher -- "happy" or "blessed" -- reflecting her own vindication in a household marked by rivalry between her and her sister Rachel. Asher is listed among the sons who went down to Egypt with Jacob (Genesis 46:17) and among the twelve tribal ancestors counted in Israel's wilderness census (Numbers 1:13; 26:44-47). Near his death, Jacob pronounced blessings over each son; of Asher he said, "his food shall be rich, and he will yield royal dainties" (Genesis 49:20), foreseeing that his descendants' territory would be especially fertile. That territory, allotted after the conquest along the fertile coastal region near Phoenicia (Joshua 19:24-31), fulfilled the blessing -- among the most agriculturally rich land given to any tribe, though also one that struggled to fully drive out its Canaanite neighbors (Judges 1:31-32). Scripture records no individual deeds of Asher himself beyond his birth and his father's blessing; his story belongs to the larger account of how rivalry, sorrow, and unexpected joy among Jacob's wives produced the twelve tribes God would build into a nation.

Asher was one of Jacob's twelve sons -- his mother was Zilpah, the servant of Jacob's wife Leah. When Asher was born, Leah was so happy that she said, "How happy I am! Other women will call me happy too," so she named her son Asher, which means "happy" in Hebrew. Asher grew up to be the father of one of the twelve tribes of Israel. Near the end of his life, Jacob blessed each of his sons, and he told Asher that his land would produce rich, delicious food -- food fit for a king's table. Asher's descendants later settled in a fertile part of the land of Canaan, just as Jacob had promised.
