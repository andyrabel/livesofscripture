Gershom (M)

Name means “A stranger here” — Hebrew

First named in Exodus 2:22

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Gershom was the firstborn son of Moses and Zipporah, born during the years Moses spent in Midian after fleeing Egypt for killing an Egyptian overseer (Exodus 2:11-15, 21-22). Scripture records that Moses himself chose the name and explained why: "he named him Gershom, for he said, I have been a sojourner in a foreign land" (2:22) -- a Hebrew wordplay (ger, "sojourner" or "foreigner") that turned his son's name into a permanent marker of Moses's exile, displacement, and dependence on God's protection among strangers. The naming is repeated almost verbatim in Exodus 18:3, when Moses's father-in-law Jethro brings Zipporah and her two sons -- Gershom and his younger brother Eliezer -- out to Moses in the wilderness after the exodus from Egypt, restating the same explanation for Gershom's name. Scripture records no independent words or deeds of Gershom himself; he appears only within the account of his birth and his father's household. Yet his name preserves something deeply personal about Moses's own story -- a reminder, carried in his son's identity, that the man God would use to lead Israel out of slavery had himself once been a homeless fugitive, entirely dependent on God's hidden care during the very years that shaped him for the calling still to come.

Gershom was the first son of Moses and his wife Zipporah, born while Moses was living far from home in the land of Midian. Moses had fled there after running away from Pharaoh, so for years he lived as a stranger in someone else's country. When Gershom was born, Moses gave him a name that means something like "a foreigner there," as a way of remembering that God had kept him safe even while he lived far from his own people. Later, after God called Moses to lead Israel out of Egypt, Gershom and his mother and brother were brought back to Moses in the wilderness by his father-in-law Jethro.

References: Exodus 2:22; Exodus 18:3
