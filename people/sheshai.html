Sheshai (M)

Name means “Six; mercy; flax” — Hebrew

First named in Numbers 13:22

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Sheshai was one of three sons of Anak -- along with Ahiman and Talmai -- living in Hebron when Moses sent twelve spies to scout the land of Canaan (Numbers 13:22). The spies' report of what they had seen, including "the descendants of Anak," terrified the ten who gave the majority report: "There we saw the Nephilim... and we became like grasshoppers in our own sight, and so we were in their sight" (13:33). That fear, despite Joshua and Caleb's minority report urging Israel to trust God and take the land, led directly to Israel's forty years of wandering as judgment for unbelief (14:1-35). Decades later, when Israel finally entered Canaan under Joshua, Caleb -- then eighty-five years old -- asked for Hebron as his inheritance, specifically because the Anakim lived there and he trusted God's earlier promise that he would drive them out (Joshua 14:6-14). Joshua 15:14 records the fulfillment: "Caleb drove out from there the three sons of Anak: Sheshai and Ahiman and Talmai, the children of Anak." Sheshai's story spans two generations of Israel's history -- first as the reason a whole nation refused to trust God, and finally as proof that the promise Caleb held onto for decades was not wasted.

Sheshai was one of three brothers, sons of a man named Anak, who lived in the city of Hebron. Anak's family were known for being unusually tall and strong, sometimes called giants. When Moses sent twelve spies to explore the land of Canaan, they saw Sheshai and his brothers living there, and their size frightened most of the spies so much that they were afraid to enter the land at all. Only two spies, Joshua and Caleb, trusted that God would help Israel defeat them. Years later, Caleb kept that trust: as an old man, he personally drove Sheshai and his brothers out of Hebron and took the land for his own family.

Thought for Today

Sheshai once terrified Israel into forty years of unbelief, but Caleb's decades-long trust in God's promise finally overcame him. Jesus has already defeated the giants that terrify us.

References: Numbers 13:22; Joshua 15:14
