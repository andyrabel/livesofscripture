Judas of Galilee (M)

First named in Acts 5:37

NT Gospels

Judas of Galilee appears only within a speech by Gamaliel, a respected Pharisee and teacher of the law, addressing the Sanhedrin as it debated what to do with the apostles after their miraculous release from prison (Acts 5:17-40). Urging caution, Gamaliel recalls two recent failed uprisings as evidence that movements not established by God collapse on their own: first Theudas, then "Judas of Galilee," who "rose up in the days of the census and drew away some people after him; he too perished, and all those who followed him were scattered" (5:37). The census in view is most likely the Roman registration under Quirinius around A.D. 6, which provoked armed resistance from Jews who saw submission to Roman taxation as disloyalty to God alone as king -- a revolt the Jewish historian Josephus also records, describing Judas as a leading instigator whose movement is sometimes linked to the roots of the later Zealot party. Gamaliel's point was that if the apostles' movement was merely human, like Judas's, it would likewise fail; but if it was from God, the council would find itself fighting against God. Judas's brief, unflattering appearance functions as a foil: a would-be deliverer whose rebellion ended in death and scattered followers, set against the apostles' gospel, which the rest of Acts shows could not be stopped.

Judas of Galilee is mentioned only once in the Bible, in a speech given by a wise Jewish teacher named Gamaliel. When the Jewish council was deciding whether to punish Jesus' apostles for preaching, Gamaliel reminded them of earlier men who had started movements that eventually failed on their own. One of these was Judas of Galilee, who led a large group of followers during a Roman census but was killed, and his followers scattered afterward. Gamaliel used this example to argue that if the apostles' message wasn't truly from God, it would fail the same way -- but if it was from God, fighting against it would be fighting against God Himself.

Thought for Today

Judas of Galilee led a rebellion that collapsed and scattered the moment he died. Jesus's kingdom, unlike every failed uprising, could not be stopped even by death.
