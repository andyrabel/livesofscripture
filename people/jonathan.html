Jonathan (M)

Name means “Given of God” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Samuel 13-14

OT United Monarchy

Jonathan's courage is established before David appears: with only his armor-bearer, he climbed a cliff to attack a Philistine garrison, reasoning 'the LORD can save by many or by few,' and God turned it into a rout of the army (1 Samuel 14:1-15). After David killed Goliath, Jonathan's soul was 'knit to the soul of David'; he made a covenant with him and gave David his own robe, armor, sword, bow, and belt — surrendering, symbolically, the throne he stood to inherit as Saul's own son (1 Samuel 18:1-4). As Saul's jealousy of David turned murderous, Jonathan spoke well of David to his father, then, convinced Saul was set on killing David, warned him through a prearranged signal and sent him away weeping, renewing a covenant of kindness to Jonathan's descendants — Saul was so enraged he hurled a spear at his own son (1 Samuel 19-20). Their last meeting came while David was a fugitive: Jonathan visited him in hiding and acknowledged David, not himself, would be king, encouraging him to find strength in God (1 Samuel 23:16-18). Jonathan died alongside his father and two brothers in Israel's defeat at Mount Gilboa (1 Samuel 31:2). David's lament, 'How the mighty have fallen,' singles out Jonathan by name, mourning a friendship 'more wonderful than the love of women' (2 Samuel 1:17-27). Jonathan's young son Mephibosheth, crippled in a fall, was later shown kindness by David for his father's sake and given a place at the king's table (2 Samuel 9).

Jonathan was the brave son of King Saul, and he became David's very best friend. Even though Jonathan was next in line to be king himself, he loved David so much that he supported God's choice of David as the future king instead of holding onto that position for himself. When Saul became jealous and tried to hurt David, Jonathan repeatedly protected his friend, even though it made his own father furious with him. Sadly, Jonathan died in battle alongside his father. Afterward, David kept his promise of friendship by kindly taking care of Jonathan's son for the rest of his life. Jonathan's story is one of the Bible's best examples of true, loyal friendship — putting someone else's good ahead of his own claim to power.

Thought for Today

Jonathan stripped off his own royal robe and armor and gave them to David, surrendering the very throne he stood to inherit for the sake of covenant love. Jesus gave up far more than a robe for us, laying aside His glory to clothe us in His own righteousness instead.

References: 1 Samuel 13-14; 1 Samuel 18:1-4; 1 Samuel 19-20; 1 Samuel 23:16-18; 1 Samuel 31; 2 Samuel 1:17-27
