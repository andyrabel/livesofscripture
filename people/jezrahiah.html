Jezrahiah (M)

Name means “The Lord arises; brightness of the Lord” — Hebrew

First named in Nehemiah 12:42

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Jezrahiah is named once, in the account of the dedication of Jerusalem's rebuilt wall under Nehemiah (Nehemiah 12:27-43). After years of danger, opposition, and hard labor to close the breaches in the city's defenses (Nehemiah 4, 6), Nehemiah gathered the Levites from all their settlements to celebrate "with gladness, with thanksgiving and singing, with cymbals, harps and lyres" (12:27). Two great choirs were formed and processed along the top of the wall in opposite directions, each trailed by leaders and priests with trumpets, until both companies converged at the temple (12:31-40). Amid the roll call of singers named in this celebration -- Maaseiah, Shemaiah, Eleazar, and others -- Scripture notes that "the singers sang, with Jezrahiah their overseer" (12:42), identifying him as the one who led and directed this enormous choir. The celebration culminated in sacrifices and "great joy" so loud it "was heard from far away" (12:43). Nothing else is recorded about Jezrahiah -- no genealogy, no other episode -- but his single appearance ties him to one of the most joyful public moments in all of Nehemiah's account: the day Jerusalem's walls, and its worship, were both restored.

After Nehemiah and the people finished the hard work of rebuilding Jerusalem's broken-down walls, they held a huge celebration to say thank you to God. Two large choirs of singers marched around the top of the wall in opposite directions, praising God with instruments and loud singing until they met at the temple. The Bible tells us the singers were led by a man named Jezrahiah. It doesn't tell us much else about him, but his short mention reminds us that God cares about worship being led well -- someone had the job of directing all that joyful noise back to God.

Thought for Today

Jezrahiah led the singers on the day Jerusalem's rebuilt walls and worship were restored with great joy. Jesus is the one our worship has always been leading toward.
