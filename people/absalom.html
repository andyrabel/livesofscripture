Absalom (M)

Name means “Father of peace” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Samuel 3:3

OT United Monarchy

Absalom, David's third son, was renowned for his striking appearance, especially his hair (2 Samuel 14:25-26). When his brother Amnon raped their sister Tamar and David, though angry, failed to punish him, Absalom waited two years in silence before having Amnon killed at a feast and fleeing into exile (13:1-38). After years apart, David allowed him back to Jerusalem but at first refused to see him face to face; Absalom used the estrangement to begin cultivating public support, standing at the city gate intercepting people with grievances and telling them, "would that I were made judge in the land!" (14:33; 15:1-6). After four years of this, Absalom launched an open rebellion, forcing David to flee Jerusalem in humiliation (15:7-14). David, even in flight, ordered his commanders to "deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom" (18:5). In the decisive battle in the forest of Ephraim, Absalom's hair or head caught fast in the branches of a great oak as his mule rode on beneath him, leaving him suspended and helpless; Joab, disregarding David's order, thrust three spears through his heart (18:9-15). When David heard the news, he wept uncontrollably, crying "O my son Absalom... would I had died instead of you" (18:33) -- one of Scripture's rawest expressions of a father's grief over a rebellious, beloved child.

Absalom was one of King David's sons, known for his striking good looks and his thick hair. When his half-brother Amnon wronged his sister Tamar terribly and David failed to punish him, Absalom waited two years and then had Amnon killed in revenge, fleeing into exile afterward. Eventually he was allowed back into Jerusalem, but instead of being grateful, Absalom spent years quietly winning over the hearts of the people and building support against his own father. He eventually launched a full rebellion, forcing David to flee the city. In the battle that followed, Absalom's hair caught in a tree branch, and Joab killed him there despite David's order to spare him. When David heard the news, he was overcome with grief, crying out for his son -- showing that even after everything, a father's love for his child never disappeared.

Thought for Today

Absalom’s story warns us about rebellion; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
