First named in 1 Samuel 8:1-5

Joel (M)

OT United Monarchy

Joel was the firstborn son of the prophet Samuel, and when Samuel grew old, he appointed Joel and his younger brother Abijah as judges over Israel, stationing them at Beersheba (1 Samuel 8:1-2). Unlike their father, whose lifelong integrity Samuel could later openly defend before the entire nation without contradiction (12:3-5), Joel and Abijah 'did not walk in his ways, but turned aside after dishonest gain and took bribes and perverted justice' (8:3) -- a direct, damning contrast within Samuel's own household. The elders of Israel, citing specifically Samuel's old age and 'your sons do not walk in your ways,' used Joel's corruption as their justification for demanding a king to govern them 'like all the nations' (8:4-5) -- a request Samuel took personally and initially resisted, though God told him the people were rejecting God's own kingship, not merely Samuel's sons (8:6-9). Joel is listed later among Samuel's Levitical descendants in the genealogical records of 1 Chronicles (6:28, 33), but Scripture records no further deeds or reform on his part. His corrupt judgeship thus stands as the direct historical hinge between the era of judges and the beginning of Israel's monarchy -- a sobering case of how one generation's unfaithfulness in leadership reshaped the entire nation's future government.

Joel was the prophet Samuel's oldest son. When Samuel grew old, he appointed Joel and his brother Abijah as judges over Israel. But unlike their father, Joel and his brother did not walk in Samuel's honest ways -- they took bribes and twisted justice for their own gain. Because of this, the elders of Israel lost confidence in Samuel's sons as leaders and asked for a king to rule over them instead, which is how Israel's monarchy began under Saul. Joel's story is a sobering reminder that a godly parent's faithfulness doesn't automatically pass down to their children, and that poor leadership can have huge consequences for a whole nation.

References: 1 Samuel 8:1-5; 1 Chronicles 6:28; 1 Chronicles 6:33
