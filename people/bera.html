Bera

OT Patriarchal

King of Sodom defeated in the war of the four kings against five (Genesis 14), who later met Abram after Abram rescued Lot and offered him the recovered goods, an offer Abram refused.

For younger readers

Bera was the king of the city of Sodom a long time before God destroyed that city. When four powerful kings attacked Sodom and four other cities, Bera and his armies went out to fight them but lost, and Bera had to run away. The invading kings captured people and goods, including Abram's nephew Lot, who lived in Sodom. Abram chased the invaders down, rescued Lot, and won back everything that had been taken. When Bera came out to meet Abram afterward, he offered to let Abram keep all the goods. But Abram refused, saying he didn't want anyone to say Bera had made him rich -- he trusted God to do that instead.

Life Story

Bera was king of Sodom when a coalition of four eastern kings led by Chedorlaomer invaded the Jordan valley to put down a rebellion by five vassal kings, Bera among them (Genesis 14:1-4). Bera and his allies marched out to battle in the Valley of Siddim but were routed; Scripture notes the valley was full of tar pits, into which the fleeing kings and armies fell, while survivors escaped to the hills (14:8-10). The invaders plundered Sodom and Gomorrah and carried off captives, including Abram's nephew Lot, who lived there (14:11-12). When Abram heard, he pursued the raiders with 318 trained men, defeated them, and recovered the captives and goods (14:13-16). Bera, "king of Sodom," then went out to meet Abram at the King's Valley after Abram's victory (14:17). He offered Abram a striking deal: "Give the people to me and take the goods for yourself" (14:21). Abram refused even a thread or sandal strap, so that Bera could never say, "I have made Abram rich" (14:22-23) -- Abram wanted his blessing traced to God alone, not to the king of a city already notorious for wickedness (13:13). Bera's brief appearance sets up a deliberate contrast between a defeated pagan king's offer and Abram's refusal to be beholden to him.
