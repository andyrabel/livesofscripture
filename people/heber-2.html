Heber (M)

First named in Judges 4:11-21

OT Judges

Heber the Kenite was part of a family descended from Moses's father-in-law's people who broke from the rest of the Kenites and pitched his tent "as far away as the oak in Zaanannim, which is near Kedesh" (Judges 4:11). Scripture notes one more crucial detail about him: "there was peace between Jabin the king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite" (4:17) -- an alliance with the very Canaanite king whose army general, Sisera, Israel's judge Deborah and commander Barak had just routed in battle (4:12-16). That peace treaty is why Sisera, fleeing on foot, ran specifically to Heber's camp rather than anywhere else, trusting it as safe refuge (4:17). It was Jael, Heber's wife, not Heber himself, who then acted decisively against Sisera; Heber is not mentioned as present. Deborah's victory song afterward blesses Jael "above women in the tent," identifying her once more as "the wife of Heber the Kenite" (5:24). Heber himself takes no recorded action in the climax of the story; his entire narrative role is the strategic, morally ambiguous peace he maintained with an enemy of Israel -- a peace his own household's decisive act then overturned.

Heber was a Kenite, part of a family related to Moses's father-in-law, who left his relatives and set up his tents near a place called Kedesh. Unlike Israel, Heber had made peace with Jabin, a Canaanite king who was fighting against God's people. Because of this, when the Canaanite army commander Sisera lost a huge battle to Israel's judge Deborah and general Barak, he ran for safety to Heber's camp, trusting the peace between Heber and Jabin. But it was Heber's wife, Jael, who acted -- not Heber himself. The story of what she did to Sisera is told elsewhere. Heber's part in the story is the peace treaty that made his tent seem like a safe hiding place, right up until it wasn't.

References: Judges 4:11-21; Judges 5:24
