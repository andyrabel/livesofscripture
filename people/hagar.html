Hagar (F)

First named in Genesis 16

OT Patriarchal

Hagar was Sarai's Egyptian servant, given to Abram as a wife so the barren Sarai could obtain a family through her — a solution Scripture frames as a lack of trust in God's promise (Genesis 16:1-3). Once pregnant, Hagar looked on her mistress with contempt, and Sarai treated her so harshly that Hagar fled (Genesis 16:4-6). At a spring, the angel of the LORD found her — Scripture's first angelic appearance — telling her to return to Sarai, while promising her son would father a multitude (Genesis 16:7-12). Hagar responded by naming God 'El Roi,' the God who sees — Scripture's first instance of a person naming God (Genesis 16:13-14). She returned and bore Ishmael. After Isaac's weaning, Sarah demanded Hagar and her son be cast out; Abraham was distressed, but God told him to listen to Sarah, again promising Ishmael would become a great nation (Genesis 21:8-13). Sent away with only bread and water, Hagar wandered until the water ran out; unable to watch her son die, she wept at a distance until God heard his cry and opened her eyes to a well she had not seen (Genesis 21:14-19). Ishmael grew up in Paran, became an archer, and Hagar arranged an Egyptian wife for him (Genesis 21:20-21). Paul later uses Hagar and Sarah allegorically to contrast slavery under law with freedom under promise — a theological application, not a claim about Hagar's own standing before God, whom Genesis shows caring for her directly, by name, twice (Galatians 4:21-31).

Hagar was a servant in Sarah and Abraham's household, and things got difficult for her more than once through no fault of her own. When she was treated harshly and ran away into the wilderness, God didn't ignore her — an angel found her there and made her a promise, and she gave God a special name: 'the God who sees me.' Later, Hagar and her son Ishmael were sent away from Abraham's household, and when they ran out of water in the wilderness, God heard her son crying and showed Hagar a well of water she hadn't noticed before. Hagar's story is a beautiful reminder that God notices and cares for people even when others treat them unfairly, and that He hears our cries in our hardest moments.

References: Genesis 16; Genesis 21:8-21; Galatians 4:21-31
