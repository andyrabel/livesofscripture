Apollos (M)

Name means “One who destroys; destroyer” — Greek

First named in Acts 18:24

NT Apostolic

Apollos, a Jew from Alexandria, is introduced as "an eloquent man, who was mighty in the Scriptures," instructed in the way of the Lord and speaking boldly in the synagogue at Ephesus, "being fervent in spirit" -- yet knowing "only the baptism of John," an incomplete grasp of the gospel that predated the full picture of Jesus's death, resurrection, and the gift of the Spirit (Acts 18:24-25). Priscilla and Aquila heard him and took him aside privately to explain "the way of God more accurately," and Apollos received their correction without recorded resistance or wounded pride (18:26). He then traveled to Achaia, where believers wrote letters commending him, and "greatly helped those who had believed through grace," powerfully refuting Jewish opponents in public debate by demonstrating from the Scriptures that Jesus was the Christ (18:27-28). His effective ministry in Corinth, however, led to an unhealthy factionalism Paul later had to confront directly: some Corinthian believers began identifying themselves as "of Apollos" against others who claimed to be "of Paul," turning gifted teachers into rival camps (1 Corinthians 1:12; 3:4-6). Paul's response reframed both men correctly: "I planted, Apollos watered, but God was causing the growth... he who plants and he who waters are one" (3:6-8) -- teaching that ministry gifts, however impressive, exist to serve the same Lord rather than to build competing loyalties. Paul later mentions urging Apollos to visit Corinth again, suggesting the two remained on good personal terms despite the factions built in their names (16:12).

Apollos was a Jewish man from Alexandria in Egypt, known for being an eloquent speaker and having deep knowledge of the Scriptures. He taught boldly and accurately about Jesus, but his understanding was incomplete -- he only knew about the baptism of John the Baptist rather than the fuller Christian gospel. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him teaching in the synagogue in Ephesus, they took him aside privately and explained the way of God to him more completely. Apollos was humble enough to receive their correction well, and he went on to become a powerful and effective preacher, especially helping believers in Corinth. Interestingly, some believers in Corinth later began arguing about which teacher, Paul or Apollos, they preferred to follow, and Paul had to address this kind of unhealthy division directly in his letters, even though Apollos and Paul themselves were not in competition.

Thought for Today

Apollos’s story teaches us about eloquence used for God; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Acts 18:24; Acts 19:1; 1 Corinthians 1:12; 1 Corinthians 3:4-22; 1 Corinthians 4:6; 1 Corinthians 16:12; Titus 3:13
