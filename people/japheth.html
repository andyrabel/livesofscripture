Japheth (M)

Name means “May he enlarge; enlargement” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 5:32

OT Primeval History

Japheth was one of Noah's three sons, born before the flood (Genesis 5:32), and one of the eight people God preserved aboard the ark through the waters that destroyed the rest of humanity (Genesis 7:13). After the flood, Noah planted a vineyard, made wine, and became drunk, lying uncovered in his tent -- his youngest son Ham saw his father's nakedness and told his brothers, but Shem and Japheth "took a garment and laid it upon both their shoulders and walked backward and covered the nakedness of their father; and their faces were turned away, so that they did not see their father's nakedness" (Genesis 9:20-23). For this act of honor, Noah's later oracle blessed Japheth: "God will enlarge Japheth, and he will dwell in the tents of Shem; and let Canaan be his servant" (9:27) -- a blessing tying Japheth's future to Shem's rather than to the descendants of Ham's son Canaan, whom Noah cursed for the same episode. Genesis 10's Table of Nations lists seven sons of Japheth -- Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech, and Tiras -- as ancestors of nations spreading north and west of the ancient Near East, commonly associated with peoples of Asia Minor, the Aegean, and beyond, tracing how humanity repopulated the earth after the flood through Noah's three sons.

Japheth was one of Noah's three sons, along with Shem and Ham. When God sent the great flood, Japheth and his wife were among the eight people saved aboard the ark. After the flood, when Noah became drunk and lay uncovered in his tent, Japheth and his brother Shem did something respectful: they walked in backward, carrying a garment between them, so they could cover their father without looking at his shame. Noah later blessed Japheth. The book of Genesis lists Japheth as the ancestor of many nations and peoples who spread out across the earth after the flood, including the ancestors of the Greeks.

Thought for Today

Japheth honored his drunken father instead of exposing his shame, walking backward with a covering garment. Jesus covers our shame rather than exposing it.

References: Genesis 5:32; Genesis 6:10; Genesis 7:13; Genesis 9:18-27; Genesis 10:1-21; 1 Chronicles 1:4-5
