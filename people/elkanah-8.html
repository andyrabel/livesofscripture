Elkanah (M)

Name means “God the zealous; the zeal of God” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Chronicles 28:7

OT Divided Monarchy

Elkanah held the position "next to the king" in the court of King Ahaz of Judah -- likely his chief minister or highest-ranking courtier, second in authority only to Ahaz himself. He is named only once, as a casualty in one of Judah's darkest military defeats. Because Ahaz "had made Judah act with abandon and had been very unfaithful to the LORD" (2 Chronicles 28:19), God allowed King Pekah of Israel to strike Judah severely, killing 120,000 in a single day (28:5-6). In that catastrophe, an Ephraimite warrior named Zichri killed three men from Ahaz's inner circle by name: Maaseiah, one of the king's own sons; Azrikam, the ruler of the royal household; and Elkanah, "next to the king" (28:7). Israel's army went on to carry off 200,000 women and children as plunder, until the prophet Oded confronted the victorious army -- warning that their own guilt before God was no less than Judah's -- and Ephraimite chiefs intervened to clothe, feed, and return the captives instead (28:9-15). Elkanah's brief, grim appearance, named among the highest-ranking casualties of the disaster, underscores how completely Ahaz's covenant unfaithfulness cost his kingdom, striking down even the men standing closest to the throne.

During the reign of King Ahaz, Judah turned away from God, and God allowed the kingdom of Israel to win a terrible victory over Judah in battle. Elkanah held one of the highest positions in King Ahaz's court, second only to the king himself. In that same battle, a mighty warrior from Israel named Zichri killed Elkanah, along with two other important men from the palace: Maaseiah, one of the king's own sons, and Azrikam, who ran the royal household. Their deaths were part of the heavy losses Judah suffered because the nation had stopped trusting God. Elkanah's brief mention in Scripture is a sober reminder of how seriously God takes a nation's unfaithfulness.
