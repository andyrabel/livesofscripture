Onesiphorus (M)

Name means “Who brings profit” — Greek

First named in 2 Timothy 1:16-18

NT Apostolic

Onesiphorus is remembered by Paul in his second letter to Timothy with unusual warmth and specificity: 'the Lord grant mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains; but when he was in Rome, he eagerly searched for me and found me' (2 Timothy 1:16-17). The detail that Onesiphorus 'eagerly searched' for Paul suggests real effort and some risk -- finding an imprisoned man in a large city like Rome was not simple, and openly associating with a Roman prisoner held for his faith carried genuine social and possibly legal risk at a time when many companions might reasonably have kept their distance. Paul contrasts this directly with being ashamed of his chains, implying Onesiphorus's loyalty stood out precisely because it was not universal among those who knew Paul. Paul also recalls that Onesiphorus had served him extensively back in Ephesus as well, a service Timothy himself 'well knows' firsthand (1:18). Paul's closing greetings in the same letter address 'the household of Onesiphorus' rather than Onesiphorus by name (4:19), a phrasing some read as suggesting Onesiphorus had since died, prompting Paul instead to commend mercy on the family he left behind. Whether living or already gone by the time of Paul's writing, Onesiphorus's remembered loyalty -- refreshing, unashamed, and diligently sought out -- stands as one of the New Testament's clearest small portraits of costly, faithful friendship toward someone suffering for Christ.

Onesiphorus was a believer from Ephesus who cared deeply about Paul, even after Paul had been arrested and imprisoned in Rome. Unlike others who may have avoided Paul out of fear or embarrassment once he was in chains, Onesiphorus was not ashamed of Paul's imprisonment at all -- he actively searched the city of Rome until he found Paul and brought him comfort and refreshment. Paul remembered this loyal, diligent kindness and prayed that God would show mercy to Onesiphorus's whole household. Onesiphorus's story is a beautiful example of loyal friendship that doesn't back away when someone is going through a hard and even shameful-seeming season.

Thought for Today

Onesiphorus searched Rome until he found Paul in prison, unashamed of his chains. May we be just as unashamed to stand with those who suffer for Jesus.

References: 2 Timothy 1:16-18; 2 Timothy 4:19
