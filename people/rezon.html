Rezon (M)

First named in 1 Kings 11:23

OT United Monarchy

Rezon son of Eliada had served Hadadezer, king of Zobah, until David's armies crushed Zobah's forces (2 Samuel 8:3-8). Rather than submit, Rezon fled, gathered a band of raiders around himself, and eventually seized Damascus, establishing himself as king over Aram (1 Kings 11:23-24). Scripture is explicit about why this mattered: "God raised up an adversary to [Solomon], Rezon the son of Eliada" (11:23) -- one of two foreign adversaries named, alongside Hadad the Edomite, as direct consequences of Solomon's unfaithfulness, after "his heart was turned away from the LORD" by his many foreign wives and their gods (11:1-4, 9). Rezon "was an adversary of Israel all the days of Solomon," ruling Aram and holding a lasting grudge against Israel (11:25). His dynasty in Damascus would trouble Israel for generations afterward, down through Ben-hadad and Hazael. Rezon's rise is a stark illustration that God is sovereign even over Israel's enemies, raising up trouble as discipline for a king who had every reason to remain faithful and did not.

Rezon had served under Hadadezer, the king of Zobah, until King David defeated Zobah's army. Rather than give up, Rezon gathered a group of men and became their leader, eventually taking control of the city of Damascus and making himself king over Aram (Syria). The Bible tells us that God raised Rezon up as trouble for King Solomon, because Solomon had turned away from wholehearted devotion to God by worshiping other gods. All through Solomon's reign, Rezon opposed Israel. His story is a reminder that when God's people turn away from Him, He can use even an enemy to get their attention.

Thought for Today

Rezon rose as trouble for Solomon because Solomon's heart had turned from the LORD. May we stay close to Jesus rather than drift like Solomon did.
