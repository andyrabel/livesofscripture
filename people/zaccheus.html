Zaccheus (M)

First named in Luke 19:2-8

NT Gospels

Zaccheus was a chief tax collector in Jericho, a position that made him wealthy but also despised, since Jewish tax collectors worked for Rome and routinely enriched themselves by overcharging their own people (Luke 19:1-2). Short in stature and unable to see Jesus over the crowd, Zaccheus ran ahead and climbed a sycamore tree along the road (19:3-4). Jesus stopped beneath it, looked up, and called him by name, telling him He intended to stay at his house that day -- an act of public acceptance toward a man everyone else scorned (19:5). Zaccheus "hurried and came down and received Him gladly," while the crowd grumbled that Jesus "had gone to be the guest of a man who is a sinner" (19:6-7). Zaccheus's response marks one of Scripture's clearest pictures of repentance evidenced by concrete action: standing before Jesus, he pledged to give half his possessions to the poor and to repay fourfold anyone he had defrauded -- far exceeding the law's usual restitution requirement of adding only a fifth (19:8; compare Leviticus 6:5). Jesus responded, "today salvation has come to this house, because he, too, is a son of Abraham," and summarized His mission in words that apply to Zaccheus directly: "the Son of Man has come to seek and to save that which was lost" (19:9-10). Zaccheus's story stands as a vivid demonstration that genuine encounter with Jesus produces real, costly, joyful change.

Zaccheus was a chief tax collector, which meant he was wealthy but also widely disliked, since tax collectors often cheated people out of extra money for themselves. When Jesus came through Jericho, Zaccheus wanted to see Him, but he was too short to see over the crowd, so he climbed up into a sycamore tree to get a better view. Jesus stopped right under the tree, looked up, and told Zaccheus He wanted to stay at his house that day. The crowd grumbled that Jesus would spend time with someone like him, but Zaccheus's heart changed immediately -- he promised to give half his belongings to the poor and to pay back four times as much to anyone he had cheated. Jesus declared that salvation had come to his house that day.
