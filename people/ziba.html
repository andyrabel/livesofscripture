Ziba (M)

Name means “Army; fight; strength” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Samuel 9:2-12

OT United Monarchy

Ziba had been a servant in Saul's household with fifteen sons and twenty servants of his own (2 Samuel 9:10). When David sought out any survivor of Saul's line to "show the kindness of God," Ziba directed him to Mephibosheth, and David placed Ziba in charge of farming Mephibosheth's restored land and provisions (9:1-12). Years later, as David fled Jerusalem during Absalom's revolt, Ziba met him with donkeys, bread, raisins, summer fruit, and wine -- welcome provision for a king in flight -- and told David that Mephibosheth had stayed behind, hoping Israel would restore Saul's kingdom to him (16:1-4). David believed him on the spot and gave Ziba everything that had belonged to Mephibosheth. When David returned victorious, however, Mephibosheth insisted Ziba had slandered him, deceiving him and leaving him behind despite his loyalty (19:24-30). Scripture records both claims without settling which man told the truth; David, unable or unwilling to judge the matter further, simply divides the land between them (19:29). Ziba's story stands as one of Scripture's honest, unresolved cases -- a reminder that not every account in the biblical narrative comes with the truth clearly adjudicated for the reader, and that political loyalty in a time of crisis is not always what it appears.

Ziba had worked for King Saul's family, and when David wanted to show kindness to Mephibosheth, Jonathan's son, David put Ziba in charge of farming Mephibosheth's land and caring for his household. Later, when David's own son Absalom rebelled and David had to flee Jerusalem, Ziba met David on the road with donkeys, bread, and wine to help him -- and told David that Mephibosheth had stayed behind, hoping for the kingdom back. David believed him and gave him Mephibosheth's property. But when David returned safely after the revolt, Mephibosheth said Ziba's story wasn't true. The Bible never tells us for certain who was being honest, leaving Ziba's true loyalty an open question.

Thought for Today

Ziba’s story teaches us about ambiguous loyalty; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Samuel 9:2-12; 2 Samuel 16:1-4; 2 Samuel 19:17-29
