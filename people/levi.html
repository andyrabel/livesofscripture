Levi (M)

First named in Genesis 29:34

OT Patriarchal

Levi was Jacob and Leah's third son (Genesis 29:34), born into a family already strained by rivalry between Leah and Rachel. His defining act comes in Genesis 34: after their sister Dinah was raped by Shechem, son of Hamor, Levi and his brother Simeon responded not with negotiation but deception and slaughter. They agreed to let Shechem's men be circumcised as a condition of peace, then, "while they were in pain," attacked the city, killed every male, and plundered it (34:25-29) -- an act Jacob condemned even as it happened: "You have brought trouble on me... my men being few in number, they will gather together against me and attack me" (34:30). Levi's violence was not forgotten. In Jacob's dying blessing, Levi and Simeon are grouped together and rebuked, not praised: "their swords are implements of violence... cursed be their anger, for it is fierce... I will disperse them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel" (49:5-7) -- a judgment later transformed into vocation, as the tribe of Levi was given no territorial inheritance but was scattered among Israel's other tribes to serve at the tabernacle (Numbers 18; Joshua 21). Levi himself did not live to see it, but his descendants -- through Moses, Aaron, and generations of priests -- became the tribe entrusted with Israel's worship, an outcome Scripture never credits to Levi's own character, only to God's ongoing purpose despite it.

Levi was the third son of Jacob and Leah, and the ancestor of the whole tribe of Levi. When his sister Dinah was mistreated by a man named Shechem, Levi and his brother Simeon were furious. They tricked the men of Shechem's city and attacked them, which upset their father Jacob very much -- he said their anger had brought trouble on the family. Later, when Jacob blessed his sons before he died, he spoke seriously about Levi and Simeon's violence and anger. But God had a plan for Levi's family anyway: many years later, the tribe of Levi was chosen to serve God in a special way, caring for the tabernacle and, through Levi's descendant Aaron, serving as Israel's priests.

Thought for Today

Levi's violence at Shechem drew a curse, yet God turned his line into priests at the tabernacle. Jesus, our greater priest, shows God redeems even a cursed past.
