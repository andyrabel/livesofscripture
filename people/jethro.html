Jethro (M)

Also called: Reuel

First named in Exodus 2:16-22

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Jethro, a priest of Midian, took in Moses after Moses fled Egypt as a fugitive, giving him work as a shepherd and his daughter Zipporah as a wife (Exodus 2:16-21). It was while tending Jethro's flock near Horeb that Moses encountered the burning bush and God's call to deliver Israel (3:1). Years later, after the exodus, Jethro brought Zipporah and Moses' two sons out to meet Moses in the wilderness, rejoicing at hearing all God had done for Israel and offering a sacrifice and praise to the LORD (18:1-12) -- a striking response from a Midianite priest outside Israel's covenant. The next day, Jethro observed Moses sitting alone from morning to evening judging every dispute the people brought him, and told him plainly, 'What you are doing is not good... you will surely wear out, both yourself and these people' (18:14-18). His counsel was concrete: appoint capable, God-fearing, honest men as leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens, so only the hardest cases would reach Moses himself (18:19-23). Moses did exactly as Jethro advised, and the text notes the arrangement worked (18:24-26). Jethro's brief but pivotal appearance shows God's provision for Moses coming, at two different critical moments, through the household and wisdom of an outsider to Israel.

Jethro was a priest in the land of Midian who welcomed Moses in when Moses had fled Egypt with nowhere to go. Jethro gave Moses a place to stay, work as a shepherd, and eventually his daughter Zipporah to marry. Years later, after Moses had led Israel out of Egypt, Jethro visited Moses in the wilderness and noticed that Moses was exhausted from trying to settle every single dispute among the people all by himself. Jethro gave Moses wise advice: choose trustworthy leaders to help share the load. Moses listened, and it made leading God's people far more manageable. Jethro's story shows how God can use wise counsel from an outsider to help His own leaders.

Thought for Today

Jethro’s story teaches us about wise counsel; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Exodus 2:16-22; Exodus 3:1; Exodus 18:1-27
