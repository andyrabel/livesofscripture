Abijah (M)

Name means “The Lord is my father” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Kings 14:1

OT Divided Monarchy

Abijah was the son of Jeroboam, the first king of the northern kingdom of Israel, who had set up golden calves at Bethel and Dan and led Israel into idolatry (1 Kings 12:28-33). When Abijah fell seriously ill, Jeroboam told his wife to disguise herself and go to the prophet Ahijah at Shiloh, who had once prophesied Jeroboam's rise to kingship, to learn what would happen to the boy (14:1-4). God told the blind, aging Ahijah who was coming despite the disguise. Ahijah pronounced sweeping judgment on Jeroboam's house for its idolatry -- everyone connected to it would be cut off and die shamefully, eaten by dogs or birds (14:7-11). But of Abijah specifically, Ahijah said something starkly different: he alone of Jeroboam's household would be buried and properly mourned, "because in him there is found something good toward the LORD, the God of Israel, in the house of Jeroboam" (14:13). Ahijah told the mother that the boy would die the moment she stepped back into the city, and it happened exactly as spoken; all Israel mourned him (14:12, 14:17-18). Abijah never did anything recorded beyond falling sick and dying, yet Scripture singles him out as the one bright exception inside a doomed, idolatrous house.

Abijah was the young son of King Jeroboam, Israel's first king, who had led the nation into idol worship. When Abijah became very sick, Jeroboam sent his wife in disguise to ask the prophet Ahijah what would happen. God let Ahijah know who she really was, and he gave her hard news: because of Jeroboam's sin, trouble was coming for his family. But God said something different about Abijah himself -- that he would die and be honestly mourned by his people, because God had found something good in his heart, unlike the rest of his father's household. Abijah's short story shows that God sees each person's heart individually, even inside a family that has turned away from Him.

Thought for Today

Alone in Jeroboam's idolatrous house, something good toward the LORD was found in Abijah. In Jesus, God finds a goodness in us that we could never earn ourselves.
