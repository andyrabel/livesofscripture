Sihon (M)

Name means “Rooting out; conclusion” — Hebrew (Amorite origin)

First named in Numbers 21:21-34

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Sihon was king of the Amorites, ruling from Heshbon over territory east of the Jordan that he had earlier taken from Moab (Numbers 21:26). When Israel, traveling toward Canaan, sent messengers asking to pass peacefully along the King's Highway, promising not to turn aside into field or vineyard, Sihon refused and instead mobilized his entire army to attack Israel at Jahaz (Numbers 21:21-23; Deuteronomy 2:26-32). Moses recounts that the LORD hardened Sihon's spirit and made his heart obstinate in order to hand him over to Israel (Deuteronomy 2:30) -- Israel struck down Sihon, his sons, and all his people, and took possession of his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok (Numbers 21:24-25; Deuteronomy 2:33-36). That territory was later allotted to the tribes of Reuben and Gad (Joshua 13:10, 21). Sihon's defeat became one of the most frequently recalled victories in Israel's collective memory -- cited by Moses, Jephthah, the Psalms, and Nehemiah alike as proof of God's power and faithfulness in giving His people the land He had promised (Psalm 135:11; 136:19; Nehemiah 9:22). Though Sihon himself acted in hostility and refusal, Scripture presents his fall as a demonstration that no earthly king could stand between Israel and the inheritance the LORD had sworn to give them.

Sihon was the king of the Amorites, ruling from his capital city of Heshbon east of the Jordan River. When the Israelites, on their way to the Promised Land, politely asked to pass peacefully through his territory, Sihon refused and gathered his army to attack them instead. Israel fought back, defeated Sihon's army, and took over his entire kingdom. This land later became home to the tribes of Reuben and Gad. Sihon's defeat became one of Israel's most-remembered victories -- later biblical writers looked back on what God did to Sihon as proof of His power to give His people the land He promised them.

Thought for Today

Sihon refused Israel peaceful passage and was defeated, giving Israel the inheritance God had promised. Jesus secures for us an inheritance no earthly power can refuse or take.
