Kish (M)

Name means “Hard; difficult; straw; for age” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Samuel 9:1-3

OT United Monarchy

Kish was a Benjamite of standing -- Scripture calls him "a mighty man of valor" (1 Samuel 9:1) -- and the father of Saul, Israel's first king. His one recorded action drives one of Scripture's more surprising providences: when Kish's donkeys wandered off, he sent Saul, along with a servant, to search for them (9:3). The search ranged over several regions without success, until the servant suggested consulting a seer in a nearby town -- Samuel, whom God had already told to expect "a man from the land of Benjamin" the very next day, the man he was to anoint as Israel's first king (9:15-17). An ordinary errand about missing livestock became the occasion of Saul's anointing. Kish is also named repeatedly afterward as a genealogical marker of Saul's identity -- "the son of Kish" becomes practically Saul's other name (1 Samuel 10:11; 14:51; Acts 13:21) -- and when Saul and Jonathan finally died at the Philistines' hands, their bones were laid to rest "in the grave of Kish his father" (2 Samuel 21:14), closing the story where it began. Kish himself never seeks a throne for his household; God works through an unremarkable domestic errand to set in motion Israel's entire monarchy.

Kish was a respected man from the tribe of Benjamin, described in the Bible as a "mighty man of valor." His son was Saul, who would become Israel's first king. One day some of Kish's donkeys wandered off and got lost. Kish sent Saul and a servant out to search for them. The search led them farther than expected, and along the way Saul met the prophet Samuel -- the very meeting God used to reveal that Saul would become Israel's first king. What began as an ordinary errand about lost donkeys turned into the start of Saul's whole story. Years later, Saul was buried in the same family tomb as his father, Kish.

Thought for Today

Kish sent Saul to search for lost donkeys, and that ordinary errand led to Saul's anointing as king. God works through small, unnoticed moments toward His plan too.

References: 1 Samuel 9:1-3; 1 Samuel 10:11-21; 1 Samuel 14:51; 2 Samuel 21:14; 1 Chronicles 8:33; 1 Chronicles 9:39; 1 Chronicles 12:1; 1 Chronicles 26:28; Acts 13:21
