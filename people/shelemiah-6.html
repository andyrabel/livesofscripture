Shelemiah (M)

First named in Jeremiah 36:26

OT Divided Monarchy

Shelemiah son of Abdeel served in the royal court of Judah under both King Jehoiakim and King Zedekiah. When Jehoiakim heard Jeremiah's scroll of prophecies read aloud -- warnings that Babylon would destroy Judah -- he cut the scroll apart column by column and burned it in a brazier, unmoved by the warning it contained (Jeremiah 36:20-25). Enraged, the king commanded Shelemiah, along with Jerahmeel the king's son and Seraiah son of Azriel, to seize Jeremiah and his scribe Baruch. But "the LORD hid them" (36:26), and the arrest failed; God simply had Jeremiah dictate the scroll again, with additional words of judgment against Jehoiakim added (36:27-32). Years later, during the Babylonian siege under King Zedekiah, Shelemiah's son Jehucal was sent by the king to ask Jeremiah to pray for the city (37:3), and later still joined other officials pressing for Jeremiah's death (38:1-4). Shelemiah himself never speaks in the text -- he simply obeys a king's order to silence God's prophet, and fails, because God's word cannot be stopped by burning a scroll or arresting a messenger. His brief appearance is a small, specific instance of the resistance Jeremiah faced throughout his ministry from the very court he was called to warn.

Shelemiah worked in the royal court during two different kings' reigns in Judah. When King Jehoiakim burned a scroll containing God's words that Jeremiah had dictated, the furious king ordered Shelemiah and two other officials to arrest Jeremiah and his helper Baruch. But God had already hidden them, so they could not be found. Years later, under a different king named Zedekiah, Shelemiah's own son Jehucal was sent to ask Jeremiah to pray for Jerusalem, which was under attack. Shelemiah's story shows how some people in power tried to silence God's true messenger, but God kept His prophet safe to keep speaking the truth.

Thought for Today

Shelemiah obeyed a king's order to arrest Jeremiah, but the LORD hid the prophet instead. No power on earth can silence God's word or stop His plan.

References: Jeremiah 36:26; Jeremiah 37:3
