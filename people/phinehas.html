Phinehas (M)

Name means “Bold aspect; face of trust or protection” — Hebrew

First named in Exodus 6:25

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Phinehas, grandson of Aaron, is defined by a single decisive act at Baal-peor, where Israelite men were engaging in sexual immorality with Moabite and Midianite women and being drawn into worshiping their gods, bringing a plague on the nation (Numbers 25:1-9). When an Israelite man brazenly brought a Midianite woman into the camp in full view of Moses and the weeping congregation, Phinehas took a spear and killed them both in the act, and the plague, which had already killed twenty-four thousand, immediately stopped (Numbers 25:6-9). God commended Phinehas's zeal for God's honor and granted him and his descendants "a covenant of peace" and "a covenant of a perpetual priesthood," a rare direct divine commendation of a decisive act as covenant loyalty rather than personal vengeance (Numbers 25:10-13). Phinehas later led an Israelite campaign against Midian in judgment for the Baal-peor incident, and still later played a wiser, more diplomatic role, traveling to confront the Transjordan tribes over an altar they had built, only to be satisfied it was meant for worship, not rebellion, averting a civil war (Numbers 31:6; Joshua 22:13-32). Phinehas eventually succeeded his father Eleazar as high priest, and Scripture later notes he was still serving in that role generations afterward (Judges 20:27-28).

Phinehas was Aaron's grandson, and he's remembered for one bold moment when he acted quickly to stop Israel from being led further into serious sin and its terrible consequences. God said Phinehas had acted out of zeal for what was right, and promised that the priesthood would continue through his family. Later in life, Phinehas showed wisdom too — when tribes on the other side of the Jordan River built an altar that worried the rest of Israel, Phinehas went to talk with them first instead of assuming the worst, and helped avoid a big conflict between the tribes. Phinehas's story shows both the importance of taking a stand for what's right and the value of seeking to understand others before jumping to conclusions.

Thought for Today

Phinehas’s story teaches us about zeal for God; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
