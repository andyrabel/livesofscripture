Demetrius (M)

First named in Acts 19:24-38

NT Apostolic

Demetrius was a silversmith in Ephesus who made a substantial living crafting silver shrines of the goddess Artemis, whose great temple there was one of the wonders of the ancient world (Acts 19:24). As Paul's preaching in Ephesus led many to turn from idols, arguing that gods made by human hands are not gods at all, Demetrius saw both his trade and Artemis's reputation threatened (19:25-27). He gathered the silversmiths and other craftsmen and gave a calculated speech, framing the danger not just as lost income but as an insult to "the great goddess Artemis" herself (19:27). Enraged, the workers began shouting, and the uproar spread through the city; a crowd dragged Paul's traveling companions Gaius and Aristarchus into the theater, and for two hours the confused mob chanted, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" (19:28-34). Paul wanted to appear before the crowd himself, but disciples and even some Asiarch officials, his friends, kept him from the danger (19:30-31). The city clerk finally quieted the crowd, warning that they risked being charged with rioting since there was no legitimate legal complaint, and dispersed the assembly (19:35-41). Demetrius's episode shows the gospel provoking opposition rooted not in argument but in threatened profit and pride -- a pattern the early church repeatedly faced.

Demetrius was a silversmith in the city of Ephesus who made his living crafting little silver shrines of the goddess Artemis, which people bought to worship her. When the apostle Paul preached that gods made by human hands are not really gods at all, many people believed him and stopped buying the shrines. Demetrius grew worried about losing money and gathered the other silversmiths and craftsmen together. He stirred them up until a huge, confused crowd filled the city's theater, shouting for two hours, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" The city officials finally calmed everyone down and sent them home. Demetrius's story shows how the gospel can upset people's business and comfort, not just their beliefs.

Thought for Today

Demetrius stirred up a riot because Paul's gospel threatened his profit from making idols. The gospel of Jesus still confronts whatever we love more than Him.
