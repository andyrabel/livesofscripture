Jezebel (F)

First named in 1 Kings 16:31

OT Divided Monarchy

Jezebel, a Phoenician princess, married Ahab, king of Israel, and used her influence to institutionalize Baal worship across the kingdom, supporting 450 prophets of Baal and 400 of Asherah at her own table (1 Kings 16:31; 18:19). She actively hunted down the LORD's prophets, forcing Obadiah to hide a hundred of them in caves to save their lives (18:4, 13). After Elijah's decisive victory over her prophets at Mount Carmel, Jezebel sent a death threat that drove Elijah into the wilderness in despair (19:1-4). Her most notorious act came when Ahab sulked over Naboth's refusal to sell his vineyard: Jezebel arranged false witnesses to accuse Naboth of blasphemy, had him stoned to death, and handed the vineyard to Ahab, prompting Elijah's direct pronouncement that dogs would eat Jezebel's flesh by the wall of Jezreel (21:1-16, 23). Years later, when Jehu came to purge Ahab's dynasty, Jezebel painted her eyes and dressed her hair to face him down defiantly from a window, but Jehu had her thrown down; her body was trampled by horses and eaten by dogs before it could be buried, leaving nothing but her skull, feet, and hands, exactly as Elijah had prophesied (2 Kings 9:30-37). Jezebel's name became lasting biblical shorthand for idolatry, persecution of the faithful, and ruthless abuse of royal power (Revelation 2:20).

Jezebel was a princess from Phoenicia who married King Ahab of Israel. She was determined to spread the worship of the false god Baal throughout Israel and worked hard to have God's true prophets killed, forcing many of them into hiding. When a man named Naboth refused to sell King Ahab his family's vineyard, Jezebel arranged false accusations against him so he would be killed and Ahab could simply take the land. The prophet Elijah confronted Ahab and Jezebel directly about their wickedness and warned that serious judgment was coming. Years later, exactly as Elijah had prophesied, Jezebel met a sudden and violent end. Her name has become a byword throughout history for wickedness and the corrupting use of power and influence.

Thought for Today

Jezebel’s story warns us about idolatry; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 1 Kings 16:31; 1 Kings 18:4-19; 1 Kings 19:1-2; 1 Kings 21:5-25; 2 Kings 9:7-37
