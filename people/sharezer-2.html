Sharezer (M)

First named in Zechariah 7:2

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Sharezer appears in Zechariah 7:2, in the fourth year of King Darius, roughly two years into the rebuilding of the second Temple. The town of Bethel sent Sharezer, along with Regem-melech and their men, to the priests at the house of the LORD in Jerusalem and to the prophets, to ask a specific question that had weighed on the returned exiles for decades: "Shall I weep in the fifth month and abstain, as I have done these many years?" -- referring to the fast commemorating the Temple's destruction by Babylon (7:3; compare 2 Kings 25:8-9). The identity of this delegation is not entirely clear in the Hebrew -- some read "Bethel-sharezer" as a single Babylonian-style personal name rather than "Bethel" sending "Sharezer," and Regem-melech's exact role is likewise uncertain. Whatever the precise makeup of the delegation, their inquiry becomes the occasion for one of Zechariah's longest oracles: God's answer, spanning chapters 7 and 8, redirects the question entirely -- reminding the people that the fasts of the exile were never really about fasting for God's sake, and that what He required all along was "true justice, kindness and compassion," not oppressing the widow, orphan, stranger, or poor (7:5-10), before promising that these very fasts will one day turn to joy and feasting (8:19).

Sharezer lived in the years after God's people returned from exile in Babylon, when the Temple in Jerusalem was being rebuilt. For many years during the exile, the Israelites had kept special fast days to remember the sad things that had happened to Jerusalem. Now that they were home again, Sharezer and another man named Regem-melech were sent to ask the priests and the prophet Zechariah an important question: should the people still fast and mourn, now that things were getting better? Their question led to a wonderful answer from God through Zechariah -- one that reminded the people that God cares more about justice, kindness, and truth in how they treat each other than about outward religious rituals.

Thought for Today

Sharezer's question about fasting led to God's answer that true worship means justice and kindness, not empty ritual. Jesus taught the very same truth to His own generation.
