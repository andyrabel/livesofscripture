First named in 2 Timothy 1:5

Lois (F)

NT Apostolic

Lois is named once in Scripture, in Paul's second letter to her grandson Timothy: "I am mindful of the sincere faith within you, which first dwelt in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice, and I am sure that it is in you as well" (2 Timothy 1:5). No episode or action of hers is recorded -- only Paul's confident testimony that genuine faith took root in her first, before being handed down through her daughter Eunice to her grandson Timothy. Acts identifies Timothy's father as a Greek, not a believer (Acts 16:1, 3), which makes the household's faith formation a matter credited to its women -- Lois foremost. Paul elsewhere reminds Timothy that he had known "the sacred writings" from childhood (2 Timothy 3:15), formation that traces most naturally back through Eunice to Lois as the family's first believer named in the text. Lois's entire biblical portrait is this single line, yet Paul considered her faith significant enough to name specifically, to the very grandson she had helped raise into a church leader now entrusted with carrying the gospel forward.

Lois was Timothy's grandmother. The apostle Paul, writing to encourage young Timothy, reminded him that a sincere, genuine faith in God had first been alive in Lois, was passed down to Timothy's mother Eunice, and now lived in Timothy himself. The Bible doesn't tell us any specific story about something Lois did -- we only know this one warm compliment from Paul. But it says a lot: Lois's quiet, real faith in God shaped her family for at least three generations, helping raise a grandson who became one of Paul's most trusted co-workers in spreading the good news about Jesus.
