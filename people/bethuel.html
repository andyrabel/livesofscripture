Bethuel (M)

Name means “Filiation of God” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 22:22-23

OT Patriarchal

Bethuel, son of Nahor and Milcah, lived in Paddan-aram (also called Haran) and was the father of Laban and Rebekah (Genesis 22:20-23; 24:15, 24). When Abraham sent his servant to find a wife for Isaac from his own kindred rather than the Canaanites, the servant prayed for a sign and met Rebekah at the town well, where she watered his camels exactly as he had asked God to confirm (24:1-27). Rebekah brought him to her family, and the servant recounted the entire providential chain of events to Bethuel and Laban (24:28-49). Their response acknowledged what had happened: "The matter comes from the LORD; so we cannot speak to you bad or good. Here is Rebekah before you, take her and go, and let her be the wife of your master's son, as the LORD has spoken" (24:50-51). Bethuel's role in the negotiation is brief -- Laban is the more active voice throughout the chapter -- but Bethuel is named as the one giving consent alongside his son, and the family ultimately left the final choice to Rebekah herself, who agreed to go (24:57-58). Bethuel is later remembered simply as the ancestral link connecting Isaac's line back to Abraham's brother Nahor (25:20; 28:2-5), grounding Israel's patriarchs in a real, specific extended family rather than a legendary one.

Bethuel lived far from Canaan, in a place called Paddan-aram. He was the father of Rebekah and Laban. When Abraham grew old, he sent his trusted servant on a long journey to find a wife for his son Isaac from among his own relatives, rather than from the Canaanites. The servant traveled to Bethuel's hometown and, praying for God's guidance, met Rebekah at a well. Rebekah brought the servant home, and he explained the whole story to her family -- how God had led him straight to her. Bethuel and his son Laban agreed that this was clearly God's doing, and they blessed Rebekah's marriage to Isaac, though the final decision to go was left to Rebekah herself.

Thought for Today

Bethuel’s story teaches us about betrothal; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 22:22-23; Genesis 24:15-50; Genesis 25:20; Genesis 28:2-5
