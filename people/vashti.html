Vashti (F)

Name means “That drinks; thread” — Persian

First named in Esther 1:9-19

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Vashti was queen to King Ahasuerus (Xerxes) of Persia, who ruled from Susa over 127 provinces from India to Cush (Esther 1:1). During a lavish 180-day display of his wealth followed by a seven-day banquet, the king, "merry with wine," commanded his eunuchs to bring Queen Vashti before all his officials wearing her royal crown, so the assembled men could gaze on her beauty (1:2-11). Vashti refused to come (1:12). Enraged, the king consulted his wise men, who warned that her refusal would embolden women throughout the empire to despise their husbands once the story spread, and urged him to depose her formally and decree that she never again come before him, giving her royal position to "another who is more worthy" (1:13-20). The king agreed, sending the decree throughout the empire that every man be master in his own house, and Vashti's banishment set in motion the search for a new queen that would eventually bring Esther to the throne (1:21-22; 2:1-4). Esther 2:1 notes that once his anger cooled, the king remembered Vashti, what she had done, and what had been decreed against her -- a brief glimpse of regret before the narrative moves on to Esther's rise. Vashti's own voice and reasoning are never recorded; Scripture states only her refusal, leaving her motive to the reader.

Vashti was the queen of Persia, married to King Ahasuerus. During a huge feast the king was hosting, he asked Vashti to come and be shown off in front of all his guests, but Vashti said no. This made the king very angry, and his advisors told him to remove Vashti as queen so that other wives in the kingdom wouldn't learn to disobey their husbands too. So the king took away Vashti's position and sent out a new law about husbands and wives across his whole kingdom. Later on, when the king needed a new queen, a young woman named Esther was chosen -- which is how Esther's own story in the Bible begins.

Thought for Today

Vashti said no and lost her throne for it. Jesus knows what it costs to stand for what's right — he paid the highest price of all.

References: Esther 1:9-19; Esther 2:1-17
