Ahaziah (M)

Name means “Seizure; vision of the Lord” — Hebrew
Reigned over Israel, c. 853 BC–852 BC

First named in 1 Kings 22:40-51

OT Divided Monarchy

Ahaziah, son of Ahab and successor to his throne in Israel, "did evil in the sight of the LORD," serving Baal just as his parents had (1 Kings 22:51-53). Early in his short reign, he fell through the lattice of his upper room in Samaria and was severely injured. Rather than seek the LORD, he sent messengers to inquire of Baal-zebub, the god of Ekron, whether he would recover (2 Kings 1:2). The angel of the LORD intercepted Elijah and sent him to meet the messengers with a pointed question and a verdict: "Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal-zebub?... you shall surely die" (1:3-4). When Ahaziah learned it was Elijah who had spoken, he sent a captain and fifty soldiers to seize the prophet; Elijah called down fire from heaven that consumed the company, and a second company sent after them met the same fate (1:9-12). A third captain approached humbly, pleading for his life, and Elijah went with him to deliver the verdict to Ahaziah's face (1:13-15). Ahaziah died exactly as prophesied and, having no son, was succeeded by his brother Jehoram (1:17). A separate account (2 Chronicles 20:35-37) shows Ahaziah's alliance with godly King Jehoshaphat in a shipbuilding venture that God condemned through the prophet Eliezer, and the ships were destroyed before they ever sailed.

Ahaziah became king of Israel after his father Ahab died, and he followed his parents' bad example by worshiping the false god Baal instead of the true God. One day Ahaziah fell through a lattice window in his upstairs room and was badly hurt. Instead of asking the God of Israel whether he would get better, he sent messengers to ask a foreign god named Baal-zebub. The prophet Elijah met the messengers on the way and told them Ahaziah would not recover because he had turned to a false god. Furious, Ahaziah sent soldiers to capture Elijah, but God sent fire from heaven to protect His prophet. Ahaziah died just as Elijah had said, without ever getting a son to become king after him.

Thought for Today

When Ahaziah was hurting, he looked to a false god instead of the real one. When we're hurt or scared, may we come to God first.

References: 1 Kings 22:40-51; 2 Kings 1:2-18; 2 Chronicles 20:35-37
