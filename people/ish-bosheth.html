Ish-bosheth (M)

Also called: Eshbaal
Name means “Man of shame — a later, deliberately unflattering alteration of his earlier name Esh-baal, "man of Baal"” — Hebrew
Reigned over the united kingdom, c. 1010 BC–1008 BC

First named in 2 Samuel 2:8-15

OT United Monarchy

Ish-bosheth, Saul's remaining son, was made king over Israel by Abner following Saul's death at Gilboa, even as David was already reigning over Judah from Hebron, splitting the kingdom for roughly two years of conflict (2 Samuel 2:8-11). Ish-bosheth appears throughout as a weak figure, dependent entirely on Abner's military strength; when he confronted Abner over a relationship with one of Saul's concubines, Abner turned furiously on him and defected to David, stripping Ish-bosheth of his only real support (3:6-11). "He lost courage, and all Israel was disturbed" at the news of Abner's death, since his kingship had rested almost entirely on Abner's power rather than his own (4:1). Two of his own army captains, Baanah and Rechab, then took advantage of his weakness: they entered his house at midday while he napped, stabbed him in the stomach, beheaded him, and carried his head to David expecting reward, framing the murder as an act of loyalty to David's cause (4:5-8). David, however, had them executed for murdering an innocent man in his own bed, and had Ish-bosheth's head buried honorably in Abner's tomb (4:9-12). Ish-bosheth's short, largely powerless reign ended the rival royal line and cleared the way for David to be anointed king over all Israel.

Ish-bosheth was one of King Saul's sons. After Saul and his other sons, including Jonathan, died in battle, Abner, the army commander, made Ish-bosheth king over most of Israel, even though David had already been anointed king over Judah. This meant Israel was divided for a time between two kings, leading to years of conflict. Ish-bosheth didn't have much power of his own -- Abner made most of the real decisions. When Abner later left to join David and was murdered, Ish-bosheth lost his last support, and two of his own officers betrayed and killed him while he was resting. David was angry about this murder too and punished the men responsible, even though Ish-bosheth had been his rival.

Thought for Today

Ish-bosheth’s story teaches us about civil war; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Samuel 2:8-15; 2 Samuel 3:7-15; 2 Samuel 4:1-12; 1 Chronicles 8:33; 1 Chronicles 9:39
