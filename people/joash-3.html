Joash (M)

Also called: Jehoash
Name means “Who despairs or burns” — Hebrew
Reigned over Judah, c. 835 BC–796 BC

First named in 2 Kings 11:2-21

OT Divided Monarchy

Joash (also called Jehoash) survived infancy only because his aunt Jehosheba hid him in the temple when his grandmother Athaliah seized the throne and slaughtered the rest of the royal family (2 Kings 11:1-3). At seven, the priest Jehoiada staged a coordinated coup, crowned Joash king, and had Athaliah put to death (11:4-16). Under Jehoiada's guidance Joash did what was right, organizing priests to collect funds and repair the long-neglected temple (12:1-16; 2 Chronicles 24:1-14). But everything changed after Jehoiada died. Judah's officials persuaded Joash to abandon the temple and worship Asherah poles and idols instead (2 Chronicles 24:17-18). When Jehoiada's own son Zechariah confronted the king with a prophetic rebuke, Joash had him stoned to death in the temple courtyard -- the same courtyard where Zechariah's father had once saved Joash's life -- and Zechariah's dying words were, "May the LORD see and avenge!" (24:20-22). That same year Judah was defeated by a small Aramean raiding force, and Joash was left severely wounded (24:23-25). His own servants conspired against him and killed him in his bed, in reprisal for Zechariah's blood, and he was denied burial with the kings (24:25; 2 Kings 12:20-21). Joash's reign is one of Scripture's starkest studies in how far a life can drift once faithful mentorship is gone.

When Joash was just a baby, his grandmother Athaliah tried to kill every member of the royal family so she could rule Judah herself. But Joash's aunt secretly rescued him and hid him in the temple for six years. When Joash was seven, the priest Jehoiada bravely helped crown him as the rightful king. As a young king guided by Jehoiada, Joash did what was right and organized the repair of God's temple, which had fallen into disrepair. Sadly, after Jehoiada died, Joash stopped listening to wise counsel and let Judah slide back into worshiping idols. His story is a reminder of how much a good mentor matters, and what can happen when someone drifts from following God.

Thought for Today

Joash was rescued as a baby and reigned faithfully while his mentor Jehoiada lived. May we stay close to Jesus, not only through those who nurture our faith.

References: 2 Kings 11:2-21; 2 Kings 12:1-20; 2 Kings 13:1-10; 2 Kings 14:1-23; 1 Chronicles 3:11; 2 Chronicles 22:11; 2 Chronicles 24:1-24; 2 Chronicles 25:23-25
