Obadiah (M)

Name means “Servant of the Lord” — Hebrew

First named in Obadiah 1:1

OT Exile

Obadiah's book is the shortest in the Old Testament — just twenty-one verses — and almost nothing is recorded about the man himself beyond his name, which means "servant of the LORD," a common name shared by several Old Testament figures. His entire message is a judgment against Edom, descendants of Jacob's brother Esau, for standing aside or actively helping when foreigners looted Jerusalem, gloating over their kinsman nation's disaster (Obadiah 1:10-14). Obadiah reminds Edom that pride in their mountain strongholds would not save them: "Though you soar aloft like the eagle... I will bring you down" (Obadiah 1:3-4). Because Edom took joy in Jacob's calamity instead of showing family loyalty, Obadiah declares "as you have done, it shall be done to you" (Obadiah 1:15). The book closes by looking beyond judgment to hope: deliverance would come to Mount Zion, God's people would possess their inheritance again, and ultimately "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (Obadiah 1:17-21) — a brief but firm assurance that human cruelty and pride do not have the final word in God's redemptive plan.

We know almost nothing about Obadiah himself — his very short book is only 21 verses long! His message was about a nation called Edom, whose people were actually distant relatives of Israel (descended from Jacob's brother Esau). When enemies attacked Jerusalem, instead of helping their relatives, the Edomites cheered and even helped themselves to what was left behind. Obadiah warned Edom that being proud and feeling untouchable in their mountain home wouldn't protect them from God's justice, and that they would be treated the same way they had treated others. But Obadiah's short book ends with hope: God promised that His people would be rescued and that in the end, His kingdom would triumph. Obadiah's story reminds us that God notices when people are cruel to others, and that He always has the final word.

Thought for Today

Obadiah’s story teaches us about prophecy; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
