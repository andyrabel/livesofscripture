Adonijah (M)

First named in 2 Samuel 3:4

OT United Monarchy

Adonijah, David's fourth son and, after Absalom's death, the presumed heir by age, declared himself king while David was still alive but old and failing, gathering chariots, horsemen, and the support of Joab and the priest Abiathar for a public coronation feast (1 Kings 1:5-10). Nathan the prophet and Bathsheba moved quickly, reminding David of his earlier promise that Solomon would reign, and David immediately had Solomon anointed king at Gihon, undercutting Adonijah's claim before it could take hold (1:11-40). Hearing the trumpets and celebration for Solomon, Adonijah's guests scattered in fear, and Adonijah himself fled to the altar, grasping its horns for sanctuary until Solomon swore to spare him if he proved himself "a worthy man" (1:49-53). Solomon released him to go home in peace. But after David's death, Adonijah asked Bathsheba to request that Solomon give him Abishag, David's attendant in his final days, as a wife -- a request Solomon read as a renewed and calculated claim to the throne, since taking a former king's concubine carried royal implications in the ancient Near East (2:13-22; compare Absalom's similar act in 2 Samuel 16:22). Solomon had him executed immediately for it, closing out a life defined by grasping prematurely for a throne that was never his to take (1 Kings 2:23-25).

Adonijah was one of King David's older sons, and as David grew old, Adonijah decided to make himself king before his father had actually chosen an heir. He gathered support from some of David's officials and held a celebration declaring himself king. But the prophet Nathan and Bathsheba quickly told David what was happening, and David immediately had Solomon, the son he had promised the throne to, anointed king instead. When Adonijah heard, he was afraid and ran to the altar for protection, but Solomon spared his life on the condition that he behave honorably from then on. Later, though, Adonijah made a request that Solomon saw as another bid for the throne, and Solomon had him executed. His story is a warning about impatience and self-promotion instead of trusting God's timing.

Thought for Today

Adonijah’s story teaches us about premature ambition; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Samuel 3:4; 1 Kings 1:5-51; 1 Kings 2:13-28; 1 Chronicles 3:2
