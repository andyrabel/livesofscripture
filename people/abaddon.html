Abaddon (M)

Also called: Apollyon
NT Apostolic

Abaddon appears once in Scripture, within John's vision of the fifth trumpet in Revelation 9. Smoke rises from a shaft to the abyss, and out of it come locust-like creatures given power to torment, but not kill, those without God's seal for five months (Revelation 9:1-10). John identifies their ruler: "They have as king over them the angel of the abyss; his name in Hebrew is Abaddon, and in the Greek he has the name Apollyon" (9:11). Both names mean "destroyer" -- in the Old Testament, "Abaddon" already appears as a poetic name for the realm of the dead or ruin, paired with Sheol (Job 26:6; Proverbs 15:11). Whether John means a specific fallen angel or is personifying destruction itself as this plague's "king," the vision is one of Revelation's series of limited, temporary judgments during the tribulation, preceding the final judgment and the new creation. Abaddon's brief, unsettling appearance underscores a consistent biblical pattern: even forces of chaos and destruction operate only within boundaries God permits, and no power described in Revelation, however terrifying, escapes God's ultimate sovereignty over how the story ends.

In the book of Revelation, John sees a vision of a plague of strange locusts that come up out of a deep pit to punish people on the earth for a time. These locusts have a king ruling over them, and John tells us his name: in Hebrew he's called Abaddon, and in Greek, Apollyon. Both names mean "destroyer." The Bible doesn't tell us much more about him than this one vision. He's part of a scary picture of God's judgment, but Revelation also makes clear that even the most fearsome powers only act within limits God sets, and God's judgments are always followed by His plan to make all things right in the end.
