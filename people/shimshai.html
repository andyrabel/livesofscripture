Shimshai (M)

Name means “My son” — Hebrew

First named in Ezra 4:8-23

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Shimshai was a Persian government scribe stationed in Samaria who, together with the commander Rehum and other officials, opposed the Jewish rebuilding of Jerusalem's walls during the reign of Artaxerxes. Shimshai co-authored a letter to the king warning that if the "rebellious and evil city" were rebuilt, its inhabitants would refuse to pay tribute and Persian control of the region would suffer (Ezra 4:8-16). The letter worked: Artaxerxes, after having the royal records searched, ordered the work stopped until further notice (4:17-22). Ezra records that once the decree reached Rehum, Shimshai, and their associates, "they went in haste to Jerusalem... and stopped them by force and power," halting construction (4:23). Shimshai's opposition was real and effective in the moment, but it was not the end of the story -- the narrative that follows in Ezra 5-6 shows the temple work resuming and eventually the walls themselves being finished under Nehemiah despite continued hostility from surrounding officials. Shimshai's brief, self-interested appearance in the text stands as one instance of a recurring biblical pattern: earthly powers can delay God's purposes for His people for a season, but Scripture consistently shows those purposes ultimately accomplished.

After some Jewish exiles returned to Jerusalem, they began rebuilding the city walls. Not everyone was happy about this. A government scribe named Shimshai worked with an official named Rehum to write a letter to the Persian king, accusing the Jews of planning to rebel if the walls went up. Their plan worked for a while -- the king ordered the building to stop, and Shimshai and his officials hurried to Jerusalem and forced the workers to stop by force. Later, though, a different king allowed the work to continue and it was finished anyway. Shimshai's story shows that human opposition cannot permanently stop what God has planned.

Thought for Today

Shimshai's letter to the king stopped Jerusalem's rebuilding for a season, but could not stop it forever. No opposition can permanently stop what God has planned in Jesus.
