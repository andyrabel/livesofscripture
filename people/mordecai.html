Mordecai (M)

First named in Ezra 2:2

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Mordecai, a Jewish exile living in Susa, raised his orphaned younger cousin Esther as his own daughter (Esther 2:5-7). When Esther was taken into the king's harem and eventually made queen, Mordecai kept close watch over her, and it was through this connection that he uncovered a plot by two of the king's officers to assassinate Ahasuerus, which he reported through Esther, saving the king's life -- a service recorded in the royal chronicles but left unrewarded at the time (2:19-23). When Haman rose to become the king's highest official, Mordecai alone refused to bow to him, apparently on grounds of religious conviction, provoking Haman's fury into a genocidal plot against all Jews in the empire (3:1-6). Mordecai responded with public mourning and a direct challenge to Esther, warning that her silence would not save her and deliverance would come from elsewhere if she failed to act -- "who knows whether you have not attained royalty for such a time as this?" (4:12-14). After a sleepless night, the king had the neglected record of Mordecai's loyalty read to him, and Haman himself was forced to publicly honor the man he had planned to hang (6:1-11). Haman's plot collapsed; he was executed on the gallows built for Mordecai, and Mordecai was elevated to second in the kingdom, using his authority to secure the Jews' deliverance and institute the feast of Purim (8:1-2, 15-17; 9:20-23; 10:1-3).

Mordecai raised his younger cousin Esther as if she were his own daughter after her parents died. When Esther was chosen to become queen of Persia, Mordecai stayed close to the palace and continued watching out for her. He once discovered a plot to assassinate the king and reported it, saving the king's life. Mordecai also refused to bow down to Haman, a powerful official, which made Haman furious enough that he plotted to destroy not just Mordecai but every Jewish person in the empire. Mordecai encouraged Esther to bravely approach the king to save their people, famously telling her she might have been placed in her position "for such a time as this." In the end, Haman's plot failed completely, and Mordecai was honored and promoted to become second in power in the whole kingdom.

Thought for Today

Mordecai refused to bow to Haman and urged Esther she had come to her position 'for such a time as this.' Jesus calls us to the same courage.

References: Ezra 2:2; Nehemiah 7:7
