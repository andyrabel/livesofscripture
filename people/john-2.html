John (M)

Also called: John Boanerges
Name means “The grace or mercy of the Lord” — Hebrew

First named in Matthew 4:21

NT Gospels

John, son of Zebedee and brother of James, was mending fishing nets with his father when Jesus called the two brothers, and they "immediately left the boat and their father, and followed Him" (Matthew 4:21-22). Jesus nicknamed John and James "Boanerges," Sons of Thunder (Mark 3:17), fitting their zeal -- they once asked Jesus to call fire down on a Samaritan village that rejected Him, and were rebuked for it (Luke 9:54-55). John was part of Jesus's innermost three, alongside Peter and James, present at the raising of Jairus's daughter, the Transfiguration, and Jesus's agony in Gethsemane (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33). He and James also asked to sit at Jesus's right and left in glory, drawing the other disciples' indignation and Jesus's teaching that greatness means serving, not ruling (Mark 10:35-45). After Pentecost, John became a prominent leader alongside Peter -- together they healed a lame beggar at the temple gate, were arrested and questioned by the Sanhedrin, and preached boldly despite threats, with onlookers noting "they had been with Jesus" (Acts 3:1-4:20). Paul later names John among the Jerusalem church's recognized "pillars" (Galatians 2:9). Tradition, though not the texts themselves, credits John as the "disciple whom Jesus loved" in the fourth Gospel, and as author of three epistles and Revelation, received while exiled on Patmos "because of the word of God" (Revelation 1:9).

John was a fisherman who, along with his brother James, left his fishing boat right away when Jesus called him to be a disciple. John became one of Jesus's closest friends among the twelve apostles -- he was there when Jesus was transformed in shining glory on a mountain, and he stayed close to Jesus during His hardest night before the cross. After Jesus rose and returned to heaven, John became an important leader in the early church, helping heal a man who couldn't walk and boldly telling others about Jesus even when it got him in trouble with religious leaders. Later in life, tradition says John wrote a Gospel about Jesus's life, several letters, and the book of Revelation.

Thought for Today

John once asked for fire to fall on people who rejected Jesus. Jesus patiently taught him love instead — may he teach us the same.
