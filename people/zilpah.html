Zilpah (F)

Name means “Distillation from the mouth” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 29:24

OT Patriarchal

Zilpah was given to Leah by her father Laban as a maidservant at Leah's wedding to Jacob (Genesis 29:24). When Leah's childbearing paused after her first four sons, she followed Rachel's earlier example and gave Zilpah to Jacob as a wife so Zilpah's children would count as Leah's own (30:9). Zilpah bore Gad, whose name Leah connected to good fortune, and Asher, whose name Leah tied to being called happy by other women (30:10-13). Both sons became founders of two of the twelve tribes of Israel, later listed among Jacob's household who went down to Egypt (46:18) and again among those Jacob blessed before his death (49:19-20). Scripture records no words of Zilpah's own and no individual scene devoted to her the way it does for Leah, Rachel, or even Bilhah; she appears strictly within the framework of the family's childbearing rivalry between the two sisters. Her inclusion among the mothers of Israel's tribes, alongside Bilhah, underscores a consistent pattern in Genesis: the twelve tribes that would become the nation of Israel, and eventually the people from whom the Messiah would come, were built not only from the free matriarchs but from servant women whose social status gave them little standing of their own, yet whom God wove fully into His covenant family.

Zilpah was a servant given to Leah when Leah married Jacob. When Leah stopped having children for a little while, she gave Zilpah to Jacob so that Zilpah's children would be counted as Leah's own, just as her sister Rachel had done with her servant Bilhah. Zilpah had two sons, Gad and Asher, who each became the father of one of the twelve tribes of Israel. Like Bilhah, Zilpah's story is brief, but it shows that God's plan to build the twelve tribes of Israel included the children of servants as much as the children of Jacob's two wives.

Thought for Today

Zilpah’s story teaches us about surrogacy; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 29:24; Genesis 30:9-13; Genesis 35:26; Genesis 46:18
