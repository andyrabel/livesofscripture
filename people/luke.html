Luke (M)

First named in Colossians 4:14

NT Apostolic

Luke appears only briefly by name in Scripture, always in Paul's letters. Paul calls him "the beloved physician" among those sending greetings from his imprisonment (Colossians 4:14), lists him among his "fellow workers" in his short letter to Philemon (Philemon 1:24), and, writing near the end of his life from a harsher, more isolating imprisonment, says starkly, "only Luke is with me" -- others had deserted him or been sent elsewhere (2 Timothy 4:11). That single line captures Luke's character better than any longer account could: steady presence when it cost something to stay. Church tradition, unbroken since the second century and consistent with the physician's careful, orderly style both books share, identifies this same Luke as the author of the Gospel of Luke and its sequel, Acts of the Apostles -- though neither book names its author internally, and the Gospel's preface (Luke 1:1-4) presents its writer as a careful researcher and compiler of eyewitness testimony rather than an eyewitness himself. If the tradition is correct, Luke wrote more of the New Testament by volume than anyone but Paul, all while remaining almost entirely offstage in his own narrative -- a physician, historian, and friend whose devotion shows itself in careful, self-effacing service rather than a story of his own.

Luke was a doctor who became one of the apostle Paul's closest friends and traveled with him on his missionary journeys. Paul calls him "the beloved physician" in one of his letters, showing how much he cared for him. Near the end of his life, when many of his other friends and helpers had left, Paul wrote that Luke alone stayed with him -- a picture of true, loyal friendship. Church tradition, going back to the earliest Christians, says Luke also wrote two books of the Bible: the Gospel of Luke, which tells the story of Jesus's life, and the book of Acts, which tells how the good news spread after Jesus returned to heaven. Luke's quiet faithfulness reminds us that following Jesus often means simply staying close to those we serve, even when it's hard.

Thought for Today

Luke stayed with Paul when nearly everyone else deserted him near the end. May we remain as steady in serving Jesus even when it costs us companionship.

References: Colossians 4:14; 2 Timothy 4:11; Philemon 1:24
