Jarib (M)

First named in Ezra 8:16

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

As Ezra assembled the exiles who would return with him to Jerusalem, he reviewed the group gathered at the Ahava Canal and found no Levites among them (Ezra 8:15). So he sent for "men of insight" -- among them Jarib -- and dispatched them to Iddo, the leader at a place called Casiphia, with instructions to bring back attendants for the house of God (8:16-17). The mission succeeded: "by the good hand of our God upon us they brought us a man of insight" along with Levites and 220 temple servants, enough to serve in Jerusalem (8:18-20). Jarib is thus one of a small group of named leaders entrusted with securing workers for the temple's service, a task treated with enough seriousness that Ezra credits its success to God's own hand. A man named Jarib appears again later, listed among the priestly family of Jeshua who had married foreign wives and pledged, under Ezra's reforms, to send them away (Ezra 10:18-19) -- Scripture does not state plainly whether this is the same man honored earlier for his role in gathering Levites, or another leader who shared his name.

Before leading a group of exiles back to Jerusalem, Ezra realized that none of the Levites -- the men needed to serve in God's temple -- had joined the group yet. So he sent several trusted, wise leaders, including a man named Jarib, to a place called Casiphia to ask a leader there named Iddo for help finding Levites and temple workers. The mission worked: they returned with Levites and temple servants who joined the journey to Jerusalem. A man named Jarib is also listed later among priests who agreed to correct a mistake by sending away wives they should not have married, though it isn't certain this is the same Jarib.

Thought for Today

Jarib helped gather workers for God's house, work Ezra credited to 'the good hand of our God.' Jesus still gathers workers for his kingdom the same way.

References: Ezra 8:16; Ezra 10:18
