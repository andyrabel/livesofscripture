Baalis (M)

Name means “A rejoicing; a proud lord” — Hebrew

First named in Jeremiah 40:14

OT Exile

After Nebuchadnezzar destroyed Jerusalem in 586 BC, he appointed Gedaliah governor over the poor who remained in the land, and a fragile peace began to take hold at Mizpah (Jeremiah 40:1-12). Baalis, king of the Ammonites across the Jordan, worked to unravel it. Jeremiah 40:14 records that Johanan son of Kareah and the army commanders warned Gedaliah directly: "Are you well aware that Baalis the king of the sons of Ammon has sent Ishmael the son of Nethaniah to take your life?" Baalis's motive is not stated -- perhaps hostility toward Judah, perhaps opportunism against a client governor of Babylon, perhaps resentment that Ishmael, a member of Judah's royal line, had found refuge at his court. Gedaliah refused to believe the report and would not let Johanan act preemptively (40:15-16). The warning proved true: Ishmael assassinated Gedaliah and the Judeans and Chaldean soldiers with him at Mizpah (Jeremiah 41:1-3), and the resulting panic drove the remaining Judean survivors to flee to Egypt against Jeremiah's explicit word from the LORD (Jeremiah 42-43). Baalis never appears again in Scripture, but his one recorded act -- inciting a murder that scattered Judah's last remnant in the land -- shows how quickly outside hostility can compound a people's grief when they are already at their lowest.

After Jerusalem fell and most of its people were taken away to Babylon, a small group was left behind in the land, led by a governor named Gedaliah. Baalis was the king of Ammon, a nearby nation. Baalis secretly sent a man named Ishmael to kill Gedaliah. Some of Gedaliah's officers found out about the plot and warned him, but Gedaliah refused to believe them and would not let anyone stop Ishmael. Sadly, the warning was true, and Gedaliah was killed. Baalis's plot brought even more suffering to a people who had already lost so much, showing how easily trouble can come from those who wish God's people harm.

Thought for Today

Baalis's plot to kill Gedaliah drove Judah's last remnant into fear and disobedience. Even so, God's larger plan to send Jesus through that remnant could never be stopped.
