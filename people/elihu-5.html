Elihu (M)

Name means “He is my God himself” — Hebrew

First named in Job 32:2-6

OT Patriarchal

Elihu son of Barachel the Buzite is introduced late in the book of Job, after Job's three friends -- Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar -- have exhausted their arguments and fallen silent, unable to answer Job's protests of innocence (Job 32:1). Elihu, who has held back out of respect for his elders, says his anger has burned against Job "because he justified himself before God," and against the three friends because "they had not found an answer, although they had condemned Job" (32:2-3). Across four chapters (32-37) he delivers Job's longest single speech, insisting that God is greater than any human accuser, that God speaks through affliction to turn people from pride, and that God's justice and power in creation should silence Job's demand for an explanation (33:14-30; 36:22-33; 37:14-24). Elihu's tone is confident to the point of self-important -- he repeatedly announces how much he has to say -- yet his emphasis on God's transcendence and use of suffering to instruct rather than merely punish anticipates themes God Himself takes up immediately afterward, speaking to Job out of the whirlwind (38:1). Notably, Elihu is never named among those God rebukes at the book's end (42:7-9), unlike the three friends, though Scripture does not explicitly commend him either -- his exact place in the book's argument remains genuinely debated.

Elihu was a young man who had been listening quietly while Job and his three older friends argued about why Job was suffering so much. Elihu grew frustrated -- with Job, for defending himself instead of trusting God, and with the three friends, for failing to really answer Job's hard questions. So Elihu spoke up, even though he was younger than everyone else there. He gave several long speeches reminding everyone how great and just God is, and that God often teaches people through suffering rather than only punishing them for sin. Right after Elihu finished speaking, God Himself began to speak to Job out of a whirlwind.

Thought for Today

Elihu’s story teaches us about God's justice; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Job 32:2-6; Job 34:1; Job 35:1; Job 36:1
