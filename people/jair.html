Jair (M)

First named in Numbers 32:41

OT Judges

Jair the Gileadite judged Israel for twenty-two years after Tola (Judges 10:3). Scripture records one distinguishing detail: he had thirty sons who rode thirty donkeys and possessed thirty towns in the land of Gilead, which were called Havvoth-jair, "the towns of Jair," down to the time of the book's writing (10:4). Owning a donkey to ride, rather than walking, marked a family of some standing in this period, and thirty sons each governing his own town suggests a judge whose household had grown into a small ruling network across the region -- a picture of order, stability, and prosperity rather than crisis. No war or deliverance is attached to his name; like Tola before him, Jair represents one of the quieter interludes of Judges, a reminder that the book's repeated cycle of "apostasy, oppression, deliverance" was not ceaseless chaos, but included real stretches of peace under judges who simply governed well.

Jair judged Israel for twenty-two years from the region of Gilead. The Bible tells us one memorable detail about his family: he had thirty sons, and each one rode his own donkey and was in charge of one of thirty towns, which came to be called the Havvoth-jair, meaning "the towns of Jair." This detail shows that Jair's family was large, respected, and prosperous, and that Israel had a period of stability and peace during his leadership. Like several of the other judges, no dramatic battle is recorded for Jair -- just years of steady, well-ordered leadership.

Thought for Today

Jair’s story teaches us about quiet faithfulness; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
