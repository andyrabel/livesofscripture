Agag (M)

Name means “Roof; upper floor” — Amalekite

First named in 1 Samuel 15:8-33

OT United Monarchy

Agag was king of the Amalekites when Saul was commanded through Samuel to "utterly destroy" Amalek -- man, woman, child, and livestock -- as judgment for Amalek's ancient attack on Israel during the exodus (1 Samuel 15:2-3). Saul defeated Amalek but spared Agag alive and kept the best of the sheep and oxen, claiming later that the animals were meant for sacrifice (15:8-9, 15). Samuel confronted Saul with the famous rebuke, "to obey is better than sacrifice" (15:22), and declared that because Saul had rejected the LORD's word, the LORD had rejected him as king (15:23, 26). Samuel then summoned Agag, who came before him "cheerfully," saying, "Surely the bitterness of death is past" -- evidently believing himself safe (15:32). Samuel answered, "As your sword has made women childless, so shall your mother be childless among women," and he himself killed Agag before the LORD at Gilgal (15:33). Agag's brief appearance carries lasting weight: centuries later, Haman in the book of Esther is identified as "the Agagite" (Esther 3:1), and Jewish tradition has long read Haman's plot against the Jews as this same Amalekite hostility resurfacing -- making Saul's incomplete obedience here a shadow reaching far beyond his own reign.

Agag was the king of the Amalekites, a people group God told King Saul to defeat completely because of their long history of attacking Israel. Saul won the battle but disobeyed God by letting Agag live and keeping the best animals for himself instead of destroying everything as commanded. When the prophet Samuel found out, he was very upset with Saul's disobedience. Samuel then dealt with Agag himself. Saul's choice to only partly obey God turned out to be very serious -- God told Samuel that because of it, Saul would no longer be king. The story teaches that partial obedience is still disobedience.

Thought for Today

Agag believed his death sentence had passed him by, but justice still came due. Jesus alone can say the bitterness of death is behind us, once for all.
