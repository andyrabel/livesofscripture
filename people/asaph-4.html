Asaph (M)

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Asaph appears in a single verse, but at a pivotal moment in Nehemiah's mission. Serving King Artaxerxes as cupbearer in Susa, Nehemiah asked the king for letters granting him safe passage to Judah and materials to rebuild Jerusalem's defenses. Among his requests: "a letter to Asaph the keeper of the king's forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the fortress which is by the temple, for the wall of the city and for the house to which I will go" (Nehemiah 2:8). The king granted every request, and Nehemiah credits the outcome not to Persian favor alone but to God: "the king granted them to me because the good hand of my God was on me." Nothing more is recorded of Asaph himself -- no words, no further appearance -- but his office made him a necessary link in the chain of provision that let the walls of Jerusalem rise again. His timber framed the gates of the temple fortress and the city, physical materials for a project that was, in Nehemiah's own telling, an answer to prayer (Nehemiah 1:4-11). Asaph is a reminder that Scripture's account of God's great works often runs through ordinary officials doing their jobs -- unnamed to history except for one verse, but part of how the walls got built.

When Nehemiah asked the Persian king for permission to go rebuild the walls of Jerusalem, he needed wood as well as travel safety. So Nehemiah asked the king for a letter to a man named Asaph, who took care of the king's forest. The letter told Asaph to give Nehemiah the timber he needed to rebuild the gates of the temple fortress, the city wall, and the house where Nehemiah would live. The king said yes to everything Nehemiah asked, "because the good hand of my God was on me." Asaph's timber became part of the walls that protected God's people in Jerusalem. Even a forest keeper far from Jerusalem had a part to play in God's plan to rebuild the city.
