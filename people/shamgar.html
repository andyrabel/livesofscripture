Shamgar (M)

Name means “Named a stranger; he is here a stranger” — Hebrew

First named in Judges 3:31

OT Judges

Shamgar receives just one verse of narrative: "after him was Shamgar the son of Anath, who struck down six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad; and he also saved Israel" (Judges 3:31). An oxgoad was a long, sharpened wooden pole farmers used to drive cattle while plowing -- not a weapon of war, which makes the scale of the victory Scripture credits him with all the more striking. He is mentioned again only in the Song of Deborah, where "the days of Shamgar the son of Anath" are recalled as a time so dangerous that travelers avoided the main roads (Judges 5:6), suggesting his deliverance came amid ongoing Philistine harassment of the countryside rather than a single decisive campaign. Nothing is recorded of his tribe, his call, or his death, unlike the fuller accounts given to Ehud, Deborah, or Gideon. Shamgar's brief entry in the roll of judges is a reminder that Israel's rescues in this period did not depend on rank, equipment, or a lengthy résumé -- an ordinary farmer with a farming tool, willing to stand and fight, was enough in the hand of the God who "saved Israel" through him.

Shamgar is one of the shortest stories in the whole Bible -- just one verse -- but it's a memorable one. He saved Israel from the Philistines by fighting off six hundred of them using nothing but an ox goad, a long stick farmers used to prod their animals while plowing. Shamgar didn't have fancy weapons or an army; he simply used what he had at hand, trusted God, and won. His story is a reminder that God doesn't need impressive tools to accomplish great things -- He can use ordinary people and ordinary objects to rescue and protect His people.

Thought for Today

Shamgar saved Israel from six hundred Philistines using nothing but a farming tool. God still works great salvation through ordinary means -- even a cross.

References: Judges 3:31; Judges 5:6
