Benjamin (M)

Also called: Ben-oni

First named in Genesis 35:18-24

OT Patriarchal

Benjamin was born near Bethlehem as Jacob's family traveled through Canaan; his mother Rachel, dying in labor, named him Ben-oni ("son of my sorrow"), but Jacob renamed him Benjamin ("son of my right hand"), the only one of Jacob's twelve sons born in the promised land itself (Genesis 35:16-19). As Rachel's second son and Joseph's only full brother, Benjamin became especially precious to Jacob after Joseph was presumed dead, and Jacob refused for years to let him travel to Egypt during the famine (Genesis 42:4, 38). When Joseph, now Egypt's vizier, demanded to see Benjamin, Jacob relented only after Judah personally guaranteed his safety (43:8-14). Joseph wept at seeing his full brother again (43:29-30) and then tested the older brothers by planting his silver cup in Benjamin's sack and threatening to enslave him -- forcing the moment Judah, once willing to sell Joseph, instead offered himself as a substitute slave to spare their father further grief (44:1-34). That offer proved the brothers' repentance and triggered Joseph's tearful revelation of himself (45:1-4). Jacob's final blessing described Benjamin's tribe as "a ravenous wolf" (49:27), and Benjamin's descendants later produced Israel's first king, Saul, Queen Esther's ancestor Kish, and the apostle Paul (Philippians 3:5).

Benjamin was the youngest of Jacob's twelve sons. Sadly, his mother Rachel died giving birth to him, and with her last breath she named him Ben-oni, meaning "son of my sorrow," but Jacob renamed him Benjamin, "son of my right hand," showing his love for the baby. Years later, when a famine forced Benjamin's older brothers to travel to Egypt for grain, they discovered the powerful official they were dealing with was secretly their long-lost brother Joseph. Joseph tested the brothers by keeping Benjamin back, and his brother Judah bravely offered to take Benjamin's place rather than let their father lose him too. That test proved the brothers had truly changed, and led to a joyful family reunion.

Thought for Today

Through Benjamin’s experience of family reconciliation, God teaches us to trust His wisdom and follow Jesus faithfully.
