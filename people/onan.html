Onan (M)

Name means “Strong; vigorous” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 38:4-9

OT Patriarchal

Onan was Judah's second son by a Canaanite woman, Bath-shua. When his older brother Er died childless -- struck down by the LORD for his own wickedness -- Judah instructed Onan to fulfill the ancient custom of levirate marriage: to marry his brother's widow Tamar and raise up offspring in Er's name, so his brother's line and inheritance would not disappear (Genesis 38:6-8). Onan understood exactly what was at stake and refused it deliberately: "whenever he went in to his brother's wife, he wasted his seed on the ground, in order not to give offspring to his brother" (38:9). He accepted the privilege and standing of the marriage while withholding, by his own choice, the one obligation attached to it -- a calculated unwillingness dressed up as compliance. Scripture states plainly that "what he did was displeasing in the sight of the LORD, so He took his life also" (38:10), just as it had Er's. Onan's death left Tamar a childless widow twice over, setting up the story's next turn, in which she took matters into her own hands to secure an heir through Judah himself (38:12-30). Onan's account is one of Scripture's unflinching examples of judgment falling on calculated, selfish disobedience dressed in the appearance of duty.

Onan was the second son of Judah. When his older brother died without having any children, it was the custom for Onan to marry his brother's widow, Tamar, so that his brother's family line would continue -- the child would legally count as his brother's. Onan agreed outwardly but was unwilling to actually follow through, refusing to give his brother an heir. Because of his selfishness and disobedience, God was displeased with Onan, and he died as a result. Onan's story is a hard one, showing that refusing to do what's right, even quietly and privately, still matters to God.

Thought for Today

Onan accepted a duty's privileges while deliberately refusing its cost, and God judged his selfish deceit. Jesus, by contrast, held nothing back, giving Himself fully for us.

References: Genesis 38:4-9; Genesis 46:12; Numbers 26:19; 1 Chronicles 2:3
