Nehemiah

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Cupbearer to King Artaxerxes of Persia who, upon learning of Jerusalem's ruined walls, obtained the king's permission to return and rebuild them; led the project to completion in fifty-two days despite fierce opposition, then served as governor of Judah, leading covenant renewal and reforms against exploitation and compromise with surrounding peoples.

For younger readers

Nehemiah worked as a cupbearer, a trusted servant, for the powerful Persian king Artaxerxes. When he heard that the walls of Jerusalem, his ancestors' home city, were broken down and its gates burned, he was deeply saddened and prayed to God about it. He asked the king for permission to go rebuild the walls, and the king agreed. Once in Jerusalem, Nehemiah organized the people to work together, even though enemies mocked them and threatened to attack. Amazingly, the wall was finished in just fifty-two days! Nehemiah then helped lead the people back to following God's law faithfully, correcting unfair treatment of the poor and helping the community recommit to serving the LORD.

Life Story

Nehemiah served as cupbearer to King Artaxerxes of Persia, a position of considerable trust and access within the royal court (Nehemiah 1:11). When his brother Hanani brought word from Jerusalem that the city's walls remained broken down and its gates burned since the Babylonian destruction generations earlier, Nehemiah wept, mourned, fasted, and prayed for days (1:3-4), confessing both his own sin and the sins of Israel and pleading God's covenant promises. When Artaxerxes noticed his sadness, Nehemiah -- after a moment of prayer -- boldly requested permission to go rebuild Jerusalem, and the king granted it along with letters of safe passage and timber (2:1-8). Once in Jerusalem, Nehemiah inspected the walls by night, then rallied the people with the appeal, 'Come, let us rebuild the wall of Jerusalem so that we will no longer be a reproach' (2:17), organizing families and officials to repair specific sections. The project faced sustained opposition -- mockery and conspiracy from Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem, and threats of armed attack -- which Nehemiah met by posting armed guards while workers built with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other (4:1-18), and by refusing repeated attempts to lure him away from the work (6:1-14). The wall was completed in fifty-two days (6:15). Nehemiah also confronted economic exploitation among his own people, rebuking nobles who were charging interest and seizing the property of poorer Jews during a famine, and setting an example of generosity by refusing the governor's customary allowance himself (5:1-19). As governor, he stood alongside Ezra in leading a great public reading and exposition of the Law that moved the people to weeping and renewed covenant commitment (8:1-12; 9:38-10:39), and later took further reforming action against Sabbath violations and intermarriage with surrounding peoples that threatened Judah's covenant distinctiveness (13:1-31).

First named in Ezra 2:2
