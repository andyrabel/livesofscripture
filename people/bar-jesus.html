Bar-Jesus (M)

Also called: Elymas
Name means “Son of Jesus (Joshua); "the LORD saves"” — Aramaic

First named in Acts 13:6-8

NT Apostolic

Bar-Jesus, also identified as Elymas -- "Elymas" apparently a title or nickname meaning "magician" -- was a Jewish false prophet and sorcerer attached to the court of Sergius Paulus, the Roman proconsul of Cyprus, described as "a man of intelligence" who summoned Paul and Barnabas because he wanted to hear the word of God (Acts 13:6-7). Elymas actively "opposed them, seeking to turn the proconsul away from the faith," recognizing that Paul's message threatened his own influence and position at court (13:8). Paul, "filled with the Holy Spirit," fixed his gaze on him and delivered one of the sharpest personal rebukes recorded anywhere in Acts: "you who are full of all deceit and fraud, you son of the devil, you enemy of all righteousness, will you not cease to make crooked the straight ways of the Lord? Now, behold, the hand of the Lord is upon you, and you will be blind and not see the sun for a time" (13:9-11). Immediately "a mist and a darkness fell upon him," and he had to grope about seeking someone to lead him by the hand (13:11). Sergius Paulus, witnessing the sudden and public judgment, "believed when he saw what had happened, being amazed at the teaching of the Lord" (13:12). The episode is Scripture's clearest instance of temporary physical blindness used as direct, visible judgment against active spiritual opposition to the gospel, and it marks a turning point in Acts where Saul begins consistently being called Paul.

Bar-Jesus, also called Elymas, was a Jewish sorcerer and false prophet who had attached himself to Sergius Paulus, the Roman governor of Cyprus. When Paul and Barnabas came to the island and the governor wanted to hear the word of God from them, Elymas actively opposed them, trying to keep the governor from believing. Paul, filled with the Holy Spirit, looked straight at him and firmly condemned his deceit and opposition to what was right, declaring that he would be struck blind for a time as a direct sign of God's judgment. Immediately, Elymas's sight went dark, and he had to be led around by the hand, searching for someone to guide him. Seeing this powerful display, the governor Sergius Paulus believed, amazed at the teaching about the Lord.

Thought for Today

Elymas tried to turn a Roman official from the faith and was struck blind for it. Nothing can stop the gospel of Jesus from reaching hearts God chooses.
