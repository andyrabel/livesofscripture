Tibni (M)

First named in 1 Kings 16:21-22

OT Divided Monarchy

After Zimri's death by self-immolation ended his seven-day reign, Israel's people split into two factions over the succession: half followed Tibni son of Ginath, and half followed Omri, the army commander the troops had already proclaimed king at Gibbethon (1 Kings 16:21-22). The text notes simply that "the people who followed Omri prevailed over the people who followed Tibni"; Tibni died, and Omri became king over all Israel without contest (16:22). Nothing else is recorded of Tibni -- no reign length, no acts, no religious verdict -- only that his claim to the throne ultimately failed. His brief mention marks one of the several periods of internal civil conflict that plagued the northern kingdom of Israel, a nation whose throne, unlike Judah's Davidic line, changed hands repeatedly through coups, assassinations, and contested successions rather than settled inheritance. Tibni's failed bid is a small but telling data point in that pattern of instability.

After Zimri's very short reign ended in fire, Israel was left divided over who should be king next. Half the people supported a man named Tibni, while the other half supported Omri, the army commander. For a few years, the kingdom was split by this disagreement, with the two sides in conflict. Eventually, Omri's side proved stronger, and Tibni died, leaving Omri as the sole king of Israel. The Bible tells us very little about Tibni himself -- mostly just that he was Omri's rival during this unstable period in Israel's history.
