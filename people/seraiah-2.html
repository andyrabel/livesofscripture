Seraiah (M)

First named in 2 Kings 25:18

OT Exile

Seraiah served as chief priest in Jerusalem when the Babylonian army finally breached the city's walls after a long siege, bringing the kingdom of Judah to its end. Nebuzaradan, the captain of Nebuchadnezzar's guard, seized Seraiah along with Zephaniah the second priest, three keepers of the threshold, a military officer, five royal counselors, a scribe, and sixty other men of standing from the city (2 Kings 25:18-19). All were brought to the king of Babylon at Riblah, where he had them struck down and put to death (25:20-21) -- "so Judah was led away into exile from its land." Seraiah's execution as the city's highest religious leader marks the brutal, definitive close of the monarchy and the beginning of exile. Yet his line did not end there: he was the father of Jehozadak, who was carried into exile, and an ancestor of Ezra the priest and scribe (Ezra 7:1), whose return generations later would help restore worship in Jerusalem.

Seraiah was the chief priest in Jerusalem when the terrible day came that the city fell to the Babylonian army. Along with other important leaders, Seraiah was captured and taken to a place called Riblah, where the king of Babylon had him put to death. It was one of the darkest moments in Israel's history, when the consequences of the nation's long unfaithfulness finally came. Even in this tragedy, God was not finished with Seraiah's family: his descendants, including the priest Ezra, would later return to Jerusalem to help rebuild God's people.

Thought for Today

Seraiah the chief priest was executed at Jerusalem's fall, yet his line continued through his son to Ezra. God preserves a future for His people, fulfilled in Jesus.
