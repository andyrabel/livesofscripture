Iddo

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

The leading man at Casiphia in Babylonia whom Ezra petitioned for Levites and temple servants before leading the second return of exiles to Jerusalem; he supplied the personnel Ezra's caravan lacked.

For younger readers

Before Ezra led a group of Jewish exiles back home to Jerusalem from Babylon, he noticed a problem: none of the Levites, the tribe responsible for helping in temple worship, had joined the group yet. So Ezra sent messengers to a leader named Iddo, who lived in a place called Casiphia, asking him to send Levites and temple workers to join the journey. Iddo responded well -- he sent back capable men, including Levites and temple servants, to travel with Ezra to Jerusalem. Because of Iddo's help, Ezra's caravan had everything it needed to properly serve in God's house once they arrived.

Life Story

Iddo appears in Ezra's account of organizing the second return of exiles from Babylon to Jerusalem. Reviewing the group gathered at the Ahava canal before departure, Ezra found no Levites among them (Ezra 8:15). To remedy this, he sent a delegation of leading men to "Iddo, the leading man at the place Casiphia," along with specific instructions about what to say, asking him to send "attendants for the house of our God" (8:16-17). Iddo responded well: "by the good hand of our God upon us they brought us a man of insight... and Levites," along with 220 temple servants, a class of workers assigned to assist the Levites in temple service (8:18-20). Nothing else is recorded about Iddo -- not his tribe, his exact office, or his life story -- only that he was a recognized leader among the Jewish community remaining in Babylon, with the standing and resources to supply skilled personnel on Ezra's request. His brief, cooperative role ensured that when Ezra's caravan reached Jerusalem, it arrived properly staffed to resume temple worship, a small but necessary link in the larger story of the exiles' return and restoration.
