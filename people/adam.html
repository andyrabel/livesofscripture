Adam (M)

First named in Genesis 2:20

OT Primeval History

Adam is formed by God "from the dust of the ground" and given life by God's own breath (Genesis 2:7), placed in the Garden of Eden to cultivate and keep it (2:15). When none of the animals proves a fitting companion, God causes a deep sleep to fall on Adam and builds Eve from his rib; Adam's exclamation over her ("bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh") is Scripture's first recorded human speech (2:18-24). God's one command -- not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil -- is the boundary Adam is given, and he breaks it: present with Eve when the serpent tempts her, Adam eats the fruit she gives him without recorded protest (3:6), and Romans 5:12-14 lays responsibility for sin entering the world at Adam's feet specifically, even though Eve ate first. Confronted by God, Adam's answer -- blaming both Eve and, implicitly, God himself ("the woman whom You gave to be with me") -- is the first recorded act of human self-justification. Consequences follow: cursed ground, painful labor, death, and exile from Eden (3:17-19, 23-24). Yet even in judgment God clothes Adam and Eve (3:21) and later grants them Seth after Cain kills Abel (4:25). Paul names Adam as the historical head under whom all humanity is bound to sin and death, and, by contrast, as the type of Christ, "the last Adam," whose obedience reverses what Adam's disobedience began (Romans 5:12-19; 1 Corinthians 15:22, 45).

God made the very first man, Adam, by shaping him from the dust of the ground and breathing life into him. God placed Adam in a beautiful garden called Eden and gave him one simple rule: don't eat fruit from one particular tree. God made Eve to be Adam's wife and companion, and the two cared for the garden together. But a sneaky serpent tricked Eve into eating the forbidden fruit, and Adam ate it too, even though he knew better. This first act of disobedience -- often called "the Fall" -- brought trouble, hard work, and death into the world, and Adam and Eve had to leave the garden. Even so, God didn't abandon them: He made clothes for them and stayed with them. The Bible later calls Jesus the "last Adam," because Jesus came to undo, through His perfect obedience, the harm that Adam's disobedience caused.

Thought for Today

Adam’s story teaches us about creation; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
