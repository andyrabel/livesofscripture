Boaz (M)

First named in Ruth 2-4

OT Judges

Boaz first appears greeting his own harvesters with 'The LORD be with you' — his character established before anything else is said of him (Ruth 2:4). Noticing Ruth gleaning among the sheaves, he learns she is the Moabite widow who returned with Naomi, and responds with generosity beyond what the law required: he tells his workers to let extra grain fall for her and instructs the men not to touch or humiliate her (Ruth 2:8-16). When Naomi later sends Ruth to the threshing floor at night to ask him, in the coded language of custom, to act as a close relative, Boaz responds with restraint — he blesses her for not pursuing a younger man, but discloses a nearer kinsman has the first legal right of redemption, resolving to settle the matter properly in daylight (Ruth 3:6-13). At the city gate, Boaz assembles ten elders as witnesses and formally offers the nearer relative the right to redeem Elimelech's land, and with it, the obligation to marry Ruth. The kinsman declines, confirming the transfer by removing his sandal (Ruth 4:1-8). Boaz then redeems the land and takes Ruth as his wife before all the witnesses, and the community blesses the marriage by invoking Israel's own founding-mother stories (Ruth 4:9-12). Their son Obed becomes grandfather to Jesse and great-grandfather to David, and Matthew's genealogy of Jesus names Boaz explicitly in Christ's ancestral line — himself, by Matthew's account, the son of Rahab, the Canaanite woman of Jericho (Ruth 4:13-22; Matthew 1:5).

Boaz was a kind and generous landowner in Bethlehem who noticed Ruth, a poor widow from a foreign country, gathering leftover grain in his fields to survive. Instead of ignoring her, Boaz went out of his way to make sure she was safe and had more than enough to eat. When it came time for Boaz to help Ruth and her mother-in-law Naomi in an even bigger way, he made sure everything was done properly and fairly before marrying Ruth himself. Boaz and Ruth's son eventually became King David's grandfather! Boaz's story shows how everyday kindness and doing the right thing, even when no one requires it of you, can be part of something much bigger than we realize.

Thought for Today

Boaz paid the price to bring Ruth, an outsider with nothing, into his family. That's exactly what Jesus did for us, redeeming us with nothing to offer.

References: Ruth 2-4; Matthew 1:5
