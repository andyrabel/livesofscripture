Elah (M)

Name means “An oak; a curse; perjury” — Hebrew
Reigned over Israel, c. 886 BC–885 BC

First named in 1 Kings 16:6-14

OT Divided Monarchy

Elah became king of Israel after his father Baasha's death, reigning only two years from Tirzah (1 Kings 16:8). Like his father before him, Elah "did evil in the sight of the LORD, walking in the way of Jeroboam" (16:13, implied by the pattern condemned in 16:7). While Elah was drinking himself drunk at the house of Arza, his palace steward, Zimri, commander of half his chariots, struck him down and killed him, then seized the throne and exterminated the rest of Baasha's household, leaving "no male" alive of his line, exactly as the prophet Jehu son of Hanani had earlier declared against Baasha for following Jeroboam's idolatry and destroying Jeroboam's own house in turn (16:9-13; compare 16:1-4, 7). Elah's brief, undistinguished, and drunken end illustrates the volatile instability of the northern kingdom during this period -- dynasty after dynasty rising through violence and falling the same way, each new king's sin compounding the last, precisely as the prophets had warned would happen when Israel's kings abandoned covenant faithfulness for the idolatry Jeroboam had first introduced at the kingdom's founding.

Elah became king of Israel after his father Baasha died, but his reign didn't last long -- only about two years. Elah was not a faithful king, following the same wrong path his father had. One day, while Elah was drinking heavily at the house of one of his officials, a man named Zimri, who commanded part of his chariot forces, assassinated him and took over as king. Elah's short, troubled reign was also the fulfillment of a warning God had given earlier that Baasha's family would not last, because of the sins Baasha himself had committed.

Thought for Today

Elah's short, drunken reign ended in assassination, exactly as God had warned his father's dynasty would fall. Only Jesus is the King whose reign will never end.
