Jesse (M)

First named in Ruth 4:17-22

OT United Monarchy

Jesse is introduced as the grandson of Boaz and Ruth, the point the book of Ruth's closing genealogy builds toward — an unremarkable Bethlehemite farmer who turns out to matter enormously to the biblical story (Ruth 4:17-22). When the prophet Samuel came to Bethlehem to anoint Israel's next king from among Jesse's sons, Jesse paraded seven before Samuel, beginning with the eldest, Eliab, whom Samuel assumed must be the one — until God corrected him: 'man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart.' All seven were rejected (1 Samuel 16:6-10). Jesse had not even thought to call in his youngest son, David, from tending sheep, an omission Scripture records without comment, quietly reinforcing God's point (1 Samuel 16:11). Once sent for, David was anointed on the spot, his brothers looking on (1 Samuel 16:13). Later, Jesse's own errands put David at the center of Israel's history again: he sent David to the battle lines with food for his brothers, the trip during which David met Goliath (1 Samuel 17:12-20). Centuries afterward, Isaiah named Jesse directly in a messianic prophecy — not David, but Jesse, the obscure farmer: 'a shoot will spring from the stem of Jesse... the nations will resort to the root of Jesse' — underscoring that the coming King would arise from God's own choice rather than human greatness (Isaiah 11:1, 10; Romans 15:12). Jesse also had two daughters whose sons became prominent military commanders under David, his own nephews (1 Chronicles 2:13-17).

Jesse was just an ordinary farmer living in Bethlehem, David's father and the grandson of Ruth and Boaz. When the prophet Samuel came looking for Israel's next king among Jesse's sons, Jesse didn't even think to bring in his youngest son David from the fields at first — but David was exactly the one God had chosen! This showed that God doesn't judge people the way people judge each other, by looks or by birth order, but looks at the heart instead. Years later, God even mentioned Jesse by name in a special prophecy about the coming Savior, showing that ordinary, everyday families can be part of God's extraordinary plans. Jesse's story reminds us that God often chooses the people we'd least expect for the biggest purposes.

Thought for Today

Jesse’s story teaches us about ordinary faithfulness; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Ruth 4:17-22; 1 Samuel 16:1-13; 1 Samuel 17:12-20; Isaiah 11:1,10
