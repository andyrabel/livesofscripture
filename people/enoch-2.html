Enoch

OT Primeval History

The seventh from Adam in the Genesis 5 genealogy, who "walked with God" and was taken by God directly without dying, and who Jude 1:14-15 credits with a prophecy of coming judgment.

For younger readers

Enoch lived a long time ago, seven generations after Adam. The Bible tells us something special about him: he "walked with God," meaning he lived very close to God, trusting and obeying Him. Then something amazing happened -- instead of dying like everyone else in his family line, God simply took Enoch away to be with Him. He was one of only two people in the whole Bible (along with the prophet Elijah much later) who never died. The New Testament book of Jude also remembers Enoch as someone who spoke up for God, warning people that God would one day judge all wrongdoing.

Life Story

Enoch appears in the genealogy of Genesis 5, the seventh generation from Adam through Seth's line, son of Jared and father of Methuselah (Genesis 5:18, 21). Where every other name in that chapter's repeating formula ends with "and he died," Enoch's entry breaks the pattern: "Enoch walked with God; and he was not, for God took him" (5:24). Genesis gives no narrative detail beyond this -- no recorded words, no described deeds -- only the striking twice-repeated statement that he "walked with God" (5:22, 24), and the equally striking fact that he did not experience death but was simply taken. This makes Enoch, along with Elijah centuries later (2 Kings 2:11), one of only two figures in Scripture recorded as bypassing death entirely. The New Testament adds a further dimension: Jude 1:14-15 quotes a prophecy attributed to Enoch, warning of the LORD's coming with His holy ones to execute judgment on the ungodly -- a text drawn from the extrabiblical Jewish work known as 1 Enoch, cited by Jude as containing a genuine prophetic word, though the book itself is not part of the biblical canon. Hebrews 11:5 (outside this entry's cited references but part of the same tradition) later commends Enoch by faith. Enoch's entire biblical portrait rests on two facts: a life spent walking with God, and a departure from this world that death itself did not touch.
