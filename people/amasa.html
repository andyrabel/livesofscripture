First named in 2 Samuel 17:25

Amasa (M)

Also called: Amasai
OT United Monarchy

Amasa, David's nephew through his sister Abigail, commanded Absalom's army during the rebellion against David (2 Samuel 17:25; 1 Chronicles 2:16-17). After Absalom's defeat, David, working to reunite the kingdom, extraordinarily offered to make Amasa his own army commander in place of Joab, telling him "you are my bone and my flesh" (2 Samuel 19:13). When Sheba's revolt broke out shortly after, David ordered Amasa to muster Judah's forces, but Amasa was delayed beyond the appointed time, so David sent Abishai and Joab after Sheba instead (20:4-6). Amasa caught up with them at Gibeon; Joab, wearing a sword concealed at his side, greeted Amasa warmly, took hold of his beard as if to kiss him, and struck him fatally in the stomach with the hidden blade, killing him instantly in an act of premeditated murder driven by jealousy over his replacement (20:8-10). Joab's men left Amasa's body in the road, covering it only once it had become an obstacle to the troops marching past (20:11-13), and Joab resumed command as if nothing had happened. David never forgot the crime: on his deathbed he charged Solomon to hold Joab accountable for shedding "the blood of war in peace," naming both Abner and Amasa as innocent men Joab had murdered (1 Kings 2:5-6, 32).

Amasa was related to King David and, during Absalom's rebellion against his father David, Amasa led Absalom's army against David. After the rebellion failed, instead of punishing Amasa, David surprisingly forgave him and even chose to make him the new commander of Israel's army, replacing Joab. Joab was furious about being replaced and deeply jealous of Amasa. When the two men later met while pursuing a different rebel, Joab pretended to greet Amasa warmly, then treacherously killed him. Amasa's story is a sad example of how jealousy and rivalry led to a shocking act of betrayal, even after David had tried to bring reconciliation and peace.

References: 2 Samuel 17:25; 2 Samuel 19:13; 2 Samuel 20:4-12; 1 Kings 2:5-32; 1 Chronicles 2:17; 1 Chronicles 12:18
