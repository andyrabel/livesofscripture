Esther (F)

Also called: Hadassah

First named in Esther 2:7-22

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Esther, also called Hadassah, was a Jewish orphan in the Persian exile, raised by her older cousin Mordecai in the capital city of Susa (Esther 2:7). When King Ahasuerus sought a new queen after deposing Vashti, Esther was brought into the harem and, on Mordecai's instruction, kept her Jewish identity concealed; she found favor with the king and was crowned queen (2:8-17). The crisis of the book comes when Haman, an official enraged at Mordecai's refusal to bow to him, persuades Ahasuerus to issue a decree for the annihilation of all Jews throughout the empire (3:1-15). Mordecai urges Esther to intervene, warning her that her royal position may exist precisely "for such a time as this" and that silence would not spare her own life if the decree stood (4:12-14). Esther's response carries real risk: approaching the king unsummoned was punishable by death unless he extended his scepter. She asks all the Jews of Susa to fast for three days with her, then declares, "if I perish, I perish" (4:15-16). She approaches the king, is received, and over two banquets skillfully exposes Haman's murderous plot against her and her people (5:1-8; 7:1-6). Ahasuerus has Haman executed on the very gallows he had built for Mordecai (7:9-10), and though the original decree cannot be revoked, a second edict allows the Jews to defend themselves, resulting in their deliverance (8:1-17; 9:1-19) -- an event still commemorated in the feast of Purim (9:20-32). Esther's story is notable in Scripture for never naming God directly, yet its plot turns entirely on unlikely, well-timed providences that spare God's covenant people from destruction.

Esther was a young Jewish woman living in Persia, raised by her older cousin Mordecai after her parents died. When the Persian king Ahasuerus needed a new queen, Esther was chosen, though she kept her Jewish background a secret at first. A powerful official named Haman grew to hate the Jewish people and convinced the king to sign an order allowing them to be destroyed. Mordecai urged Esther to speak to the king on her people's behalf, even though approaching him without being summoned could mean death. Esther bravely agreed, saying, "If I perish, I perish," and asked all the Jewish people to fast and pray for her first. She then exposed Haman's evil plan to the king at a banquet. The king had Haman punished, and the Jewish people were saved. Esther's courage at just the right moment protected her whole nation.

Thought for Today

Esther’s story teaches us about courage; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
