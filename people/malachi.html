Malachi (M)

First named in Malachi 1:1

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Almost nothing is known about Malachi personally — his name means "my messenger," and some scholars debate whether it functioned as a title rather than a personal name (Malachi 1:1). Writing after the temple's rebuilding, when the initial enthusiasm of the return from exile had worn into weary routine, Malachi confronts specific failures: priests offering blind, lame, and diseased animals as sacrifices instead of their best (Malachi 1:6-14); men treacherously divorcing the wives of their youth, which God says He hates (Malachi 2:13-16); and a people who had stopped tithing faithfully, whom God challenges directly, "Will man rob God? Yet you are robbing me... Bring the full tithe... and test me now, and see if I will not open the windows of heaven" (Malachi 3:8-10). Underneath these failures lies a deeper problem: the people had begun to doubt God's love and justice, asking "How have you loved us?" (Malachi 1:2; 3:14). Malachi promises a messenger would come to prepare the way before the LORD's own sudden arrival at His temple, refining His people like a metalworker refines silver (Malachi 3:1-3) — words the Gospels apply to John the Baptist preparing the way for Jesus (Matthew 11:10). The book, and the entire Old Testament, closes by promising "Elijah" would come before "the great and awesome day of the LORD" (Malachi 4:5-6).

We don't actually know very much about Malachi as a person — his name even just means "my messenger." He wrote the last book of the Old Testament, at a time when people had grown tired and careless about following God — priests were offering God their leftover, damaged animals instead of their best, and people had stopped giving generously. Malachi challenged them, saying that if they truly trusted God and gave generously, God would pour out blessings on them. He also gave an exciting promise: that a special messenger would come to prepare the way before the LORD arrived — a promise fulfilled by John the Baptist getting people ready for Jesus. Malachi's story reminds us that even when we grow tired of doing what's right, God is still faithful, and still has good things planned ahead.

Thought for Today

Malachi’s story teaches us about prophecy; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
