Issachar (M)

First named in Genesis 30:18

OT Patriarchal

Issachar was Jacob's ninth son, born to Leah. His name and birth are tied to an unusual bargain: Leah's son Reuben found mandrakes in the field, and Rachel traded Jacob's company that night for some of them; when Leah conceived, she said, "God has given me my wages because I gave my maid to my husband," naming him Issachar -- a name associated with "wages" or "reward" (Genesis 30:14-18). He is listed among the sons who journeyed with Jacob to Egypt (46:13) and became the ancestor of one of the twelve tribes, counted and positioned among Israel's wilderness camps (Numbers 1-2). On his deathbed, Jacob gave Issachar a distinctive blessing: "Issachar is a strong donkey, lying down between the sheep pens. When he saw that a resting place was good and that the land was pleasant, he bowed his shoulder to bear burdens, and became a slave at forced labor" (Genesis 49:14-15) -- an image of strength content to trade freedom for security and rest on good land. Scripture records no personal deeds or words of Issachar himself beyond his birth and this blessing, but his tribe later produced men "who understood the times, to know what Israel should do" among David's supporters (1 Chronicles 12:32), and Issachar's name is preserved among the twelve tribes sealed in Revelation's vision (Revelation 7:7).

Issachar was one of Jacob's twelve sons, born to Leah. His birth came about in an unusual way: Leah traded some plants her son Reuben had found for a night with Jacob, and when Issachar was born she said God had rewarded her. Issachar grew up to become the father of one of the twelve tribes of Israel. Near the end of his life, Jacob gave each son a special blessing, and he described Issachar's tribe as strong like a donkey -- willing to work hard and settle down on good land, even if it meant giving up some freedom to do it.

Thought for Today

Issachar's birth came through Leah's longing to be loved by Jacob, yet God still wove her sorrow into Israel's story. God works even through our messy, unwanted circumstances.
