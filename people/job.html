Job (M)

OT Patriarchal

Job was "blameless, upright, fearing God and turning away from evil," a wealthy man of the land of Uz with seven sons and three daughters (Job 1:1-3). In the book's opening scene, Satan appears before the LORD and challenges whether Job's righteousness is genuine or merely bought by blessing; God permits Satan to strip Job of his wealth and children, then his health, but forbids taking his life (1:6-2:6). Job loses everything in a single day and is afflicted with agonizing sores from head to foot, yet responds, "Shall we indeed accept good from God and not accept adversity?" (2:10). His wife tells him to "curse God and die"; he refuses (2:9). Three friends -- Eliphaz, Bildad, and Zophar -- arrive to comfort him, but their long speeches insist his suffering must be deserved, provoking Job into raw, anguished protest: he curses the day of his birth (3:1-3), demands an audience with God to plead his innocence, and refuses to accept his friends' simplistic formula that suffering always equals guilt. A younger man, Elihu, speaks next, defending God's justice without fully answering Job's questions. Finally God Himself answers Job out of a whirlwind (38:1) -- not with an explanation of why Job suffered, but with an overwhelming display of His own wisdom and power in creation, before which Job repents "in dust and ashes" (42:6). God rebukes the three friends for misrepresenting Him, vindicates Job, and restores him with double his former wealth and a new family (42:7-17).

Job was a good man who loved God and had a big family, many animals, and lots of land. Then, in a very hard time, Job lost everything -- his animals, his home, even his ten children -- and became very sick, all at once. Job's three friends came to comfort him, but instead they kept telling him his suffering must be punishment for something bad he'd done. Job didn't understand why all this happened to him, but he never stopped believing in God, even when it was extremely hard. Eventually God spoke to Job out of a mighty storm, reminding him how great and wise God is. In the end, God blessed Job again with a new family and even more than he had before, and Job is remembered in the Bible as an example of patience through terrible suffering.

Thought for Today

Job lost everything and still said, 'shall I accept good from God and not adversity?' Lord Jesus, help me trust You in my hardest days.

God never explained Job's suffering — He just showed up. Jesus, even when I don't get answers, knowing You are with me is enough.
