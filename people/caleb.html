Caleb (M)

Name means “A dog; a crow; a basket” — Hebrew

First named in Numbers 13:6-30

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Caleb was one of the twelve spies Moses sent into Canaan, representing the tribe of Judah (Numbers 13:6). While ten spies returned with a fearful report of giants and fortified cities, Caleb alone with Joshua urged Israel to trust God: "we should by all means go up and take possession of it, for we will surely overcome it" (13:30). The people believed the majority instead and were condemned to forty years of wandering until that generation died; only Caleb and Joshua, because they "followed the LORD fully," were promised entry into the land (14:24, 30-38). Caleb held onto that promise for the next forty-five years. At eighty-five, he came to Joshua and asked for the very hill country the spies had once feared -- Hebron, home of the giant Anakim -- and drove them out himself with God's help (Joshua 14:6-14). He gave his daughter Achsah in marriage to Othniel, who captured Kiriath-sepher, and Achsah boldly asked her father for springs of water to go with her dry southern land, which Caleb granted (Joshua 15:16-19). Caleb's life is Scripture's model of sustained, undivided faith: a man who believed God's promise at forty and was still acting on it, vigorously, at eighty-five.

Caleb was one of twelve spies Moses sent to explore the land God promised to Israel. Ten of the spies came back scared, saying the people there were too strong to defeat. But Caleb and his friend Joshua trusted God and said, "We can certainly do it!" Because the people believed the fearful report instead, Israel had to wander in the desert for many more years before entering the land. Caleb never lost his faith during all that waiting. When he was eighty-five years old, he finally received his promised territory -- the hill country of Hebron -- and asked to fight the powerful giants living there himself, still strong and ready to trust God. Caleb's story teaches that following God "wholeheartedly," even when others doubt, is worth the wait.

Thought for Today

At forty, Caleb believed God's promise about Canaan; at eighty-five, giants and all, he was still acting on it. May our faith in Jesus stretch out that same way, across an entire lifetime, not just one decision made long ago.
