Lamech (M)

Name means “Poor; made low” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 4:18-24

OT Primeval History

Lamech, five generations from Cain, is Genesis 4's last named figure in Cain's line before the narrative shifts to Seth's descendants. He is also the first man Scripture records taking two wives, Adah and Zillah (Genesis 4:19), a departure from God's design in Eden. His sons became ancestors of livestock-herders, musicians, and metalworkers (4:20-22), showing civilization advancing even in a line under judgment. But Lamech is remembered chiefly for a boastful poem he addressed to his wives: he claimed to have killed a young man merely for wounding him, and declared that if Cain would be avenged sevenfold for any harm done to him, Lamech would be avenged seventy-sevenfold (4:23-24). Where God's sevenfold protection over Cain had been an act of restraining mercy toward a murderer, Lamech twisted the same principle into a boast of unrestrained, self-declared vengeance. His poem marks how quickly violence escalated after Cain's first act of bloodshed, and Genesis presents his arrogance as evidence of sin spreading and intensifying across generations rather than fading. Lamech's unchecked pride stands as a dark counterpoint to the patient, redemptive genealogy of Seth that Genesis 5 introduces immediately afterward.

Lamech was a descendant of Cain, and the Bible includes a short poem he made up for his two wives, Adah and Zillah. In it, Lamech boasted that he had killed a young man who merely wounded him, and bragged that he would get back at anyone who hurt him far worse than what God had promised to do for Cain. Lamech's boast shows how sin and violence kept growing worse in Cain's family line as time went on. His story is a warning about pride and revenge, and it makes the rest of the Bible's story of God patiently working to fix a broken world even more meaningful.

Thought for Today

Lamech boasted he would avenge himself seventy-sevenfold for any wrong done to him. Jesus instead told us to forgive seventy-seven times, turning Lamech's boast completely upside down.
