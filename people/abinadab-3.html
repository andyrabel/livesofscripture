Abinadab (M)

Name means “Father of a vow” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Samuel 16:8

OT United Monarchy

Abinadab was Jesse's second son, David's older brother (1 Chronicles 2:13). When Samuel came to Bethlehem to anoint Israel's next king at God's command, Jesse presented his sons one by one, beginning with the eldest, Eliab. Abinadab passed before Samuel next, and like Eliab, was rejected: "the LORD has not chosen him" (1 Samuel 16:8). It was in this moment that God gave Samuel His now-famous instruction: "God sees not as man sees, for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart" (16:7). Abinadab reappears later serving with his eldest brother Eliab and third brother Shammah in Saul's army, encamped against the Philistines in the Valley of Elah during the standoff with Goliath -- the same campaign their youngest brother David visited bringing supplies, and where David killed the giant while Abinadab and the rest of Israel's soldiers had been too afraid to fight (17:13-14, 17:24). Abinadab himself performs no recorded action beyond standing in the ranks; Scripture uses him chiefly as a foil, the passed-over older brother against whom David's unlikely selection and courage are measured.

Abinadab was one of David's older brothers, the second son of Jesse. When the prophet Samuel came to anoint one of Jesse's sons as Israel's next king, Jesse brought Abinadab before him, but God told Samuel that Abinadab was not the one -- God doesn't judge people by how tall or impressive they look, but by their heart. Later, Abinadab served as a soldier in King Saul's army during the standoff with the giant Goliath, right around the time his younger brother David came to the battle lines and ended up facing Goliath himself.

Thought for Today

Abinadab’s story teaches us about God's choice over human appearance; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 1 Samuel 16:8; 1 Samuel 17:13; 1 Chronicles 2:13
