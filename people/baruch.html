Baruch (M)

Name means “Who is blessed” — Hebrew

First named in Nehemiah 3:20

OT Divided Monarchy

Baruch served as Jeremiah's scribe, writing down the prophet's spoken messages onto a scroll at his dictation (Jeremiah 36:4). He read the scroll's warnings aloud publicly in the temple, and again before King Jehoiakim's officials, who were alarmed enough to warn both Baruch and Jeremiah to go into hiding before showing it to the king (36:8-19). Jehoiakim, hearing the scroll read to him, cut off each section with a knife and burned it in a brazier, showing open contempt for God's word and ordering Jeremiah and Baruch's arrest, "but the LORD hid them" (36:21-26). God then had Jeremiah dictate the entire scroll again to Baruch, with additional judgment added because of the king's defiance (36:27-32). Baruch's own moment of personal despair is recorded directly: overwhelmed by the suffering and grief his work brought him, he cried, "Woe to me now! For the LORD has added sorrow to my pain; I am weary with my groaning and have found no rest" (45:1-3). God answered him not with a promise of ease, but with assurance that amid the coming national catastrophe, his life would be preserved as "a prize" wherever he went, since he had faithfully sought nothing great for himself (45:4-5). Baruch remained loyal to Jeremiah to the end, accompanying him even when the remnant of Judah forced them both into exile in Egypt (43:1-7).

Baruch worked closely with the prophet Jeremiah as his scribe, writing down the difficult messages God gave Jeremiah to deliver to Judah. When King Jehoiakim heard Baruch read one of these scrolls aloud, the king angrily cut it apart and burned it in the fire, refusing to listen to its warnings. Baruch and Jeremiah then had to go into hiding, and God had Baruch write everything down again on a new scroll. At one point, Baruch grew discouraged and exhausted by all the suffering surrounding his difficult work, but God sent him a personal message of comfort, promising to protect his life even though hard times were coming for the whole nation. Baruch stayed loyal to Jeremiah through it all, even later fleeing with him to Egypt.

Thought for Today

Baruch faithfully wrote and read Jeremiah's hard words even when it endangered his life. Jesus calls us to that same obedience, even when His word costs us comfort.
