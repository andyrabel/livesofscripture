Benaiah (M)

First named in 2 Samuel 8:18

OT United Monarchy

Benaiah son of Jehoiada, from Kabzeel, commanded David's personal bodyguard, the Cherethites and Pelethites (2 Samuel 8:18; 20:23). Scripture singles him out among David's mighty men for three feats: he struck down two "ariels" (champions) of Moab, went down into a pit on a snowy day and killed a lion, and killed a huge Egyptian, wresting the man's own spear from him and killing him with it (2 Samuel 23:20-22). Though not one of David's elite Three, he was set over David's guard and "had a name among the three mighty men" (23:22-23). When the aging David's son Adonijah tried to seize the throne without David's authorization, Benaiah sided decisively with Solomon, escorting him to be anointed king (1 Kings 1:8-44). After Solomon's accession, the new king used Benaiah to carry out the executions of Joab -- for the blood Joab had shed in peacetime -- and of Shimei, cleansing the kingdom of lingering threats (1 Kings 2:25-46). Solomon then made Benaiah commander of the entire army in Joab's place (1 Kings 2:35; 4:4), and 1 Chronicles 27 lists him over the monthly military divisions. Benaiah's career models unwavering loyalty to God's chosen and anointed king even when loyalty required difficult, costly action.

Benaiah was one of King David's bravest soldiers and the captain of his personal bodyguard. The Bible tells us he did amazing, brave things: he fought off two powerful warriors from Moab, he went down into a pit on a snowy day and killed a lion, and he beat a huge Egyptian warrior using the man's own spear. Because Benaiah was so loyal and brave, David trusted him completely. When David was old and his son Adonijah tried to grab the throne, Benaiah stood firmly with David's choice, Solomon, and helped make sure Solomon became king the right way. Solomon later made Benaiah the commander of Israel's whole army.

Thought for Today

Benaiah’s story teaches us about loyalty to God's anointed king; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
