Sisera (M)

First named in Judges 4:2-22

OT Judges

Sisera commanded the army of Jabin, king of Canaan, whose nine hundred iron chariots had oppressed Israel for twenty years (Judges 4:2-3). When the prophetess Deborah summoned Barak to battle at Mount Tabor, Barak agreed only if Deborah went with him, so Deborah told him the LORD would deliver Sisera into the hand of a woman instead of his (4:6-9). At the Kishon River, the LORD threw Sisera's army into a panic before Israel, and Sisera abandoned his chariot to flee on foot, leaving his entire army to be destroyed (4:14-16). He ran to the tent of Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite, whose clan was at peace with Jabin, and she welcomed him, gave him milk when he asked for water, and covered him while he slept (4:17-19). While Sisera slept exhausted, Jael drove a tent peg through his temple with a hammer, killing him -- fulfilling Deborah's word that the honor of his defeat would belong to a woman (4:21-22). Judges 5's victory song celebrates the moment further, picturing Sisera's mother waiting anxiously at a window for a son who would never return, wondering why his chariot delayed (5:28-30). Sisera's downfall, engineered entirely outside the expected battlefield, became one of Israel's great deliverance stories, later recalled by the prophet Samuel and the psalmist as an example of God defeating Israel's enemies (1 Samuel 12:9; Psalm 83:9).

Sisera was the powerful commander of a huge army with iron chariots that had made life very hard for God's people for twenty years. God's prophet Deborah told a leader named Barak to gather an army and fight Sisera, promising that God would give Israel the victory. God caused Sisera's whole army to panic in the battle, and Sisera ran away on foot to hide. He came to the tent of a woman named Jael, who let him rest and gave him something to drink. But while Sisera was sleeping, Jael did something brave and surprising that ended his life, just as Deborah had said would happen. Sisera's story shows that no army, however strong, can stand against God's plan to rescue His people -- and that God sometimes works through people no one expects.

Thought for Today

Sisera's mighty army and iron chariots were nothing next to God. No power on earth can stand against God's plan to save us.

References: Judges 4:2-22; Judges 5:20-30; 1 Samuel 12:9; Psalms 83:9
