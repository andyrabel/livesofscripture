Asa (M)

Name means “Physician; cure” — Hebrew
Reigned over Judah, c. 911 BC–870 BC

First named in 1 Kings 15:8-33

OT Divided Monarchy

Asa became king of Judah after his father Abijah and reigned forty-one years, longer than any king of Judah before him (1 Kings 15:9-10). Scripture commends him for doing "what was right in the sight of the LORD" -- removing male cult prostitutes, tearing down his fathers' idols, and even deposing his grandmother Maacah as queen mother for an obscene Asherah image she had made, which Asa cut down and burned (1 Kings 15:11-13; 2 Chronicles 14:2-5). When a vast Cushite army invaded, Asa prayed openly for help -- "LORD, there is no one besides You... let not man prevail against You" -- and Judah won decisively (2 Chronicles 14:9-13). The prophet Azariah urged him to stay courageous in seeking the LORD, prompting further reform and a covenant renewal at Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 15:1-15). But Asa's faith faltered late in his reign: when King Baasha of Israel fortified Ramah to blockade Judah, Asa emptied the temple treasury to bribe Ben-hadad of Aram into breaking his treaty with Israel and attacking it instead -- a political solution rather than a prayer (1 Kings 15:16-22). The seer Hanani rebuked him for relying on Aram instead of the LORD who had given the Cushite victory; Asa imprisoned him in a rage for it (2 Chronicles 16:7-10). Diseased in his feet in old age, Asa "did not seek the LORD, but the physicians" -- Scripture's own contrast with his earlier prayers (2 Chronicles 16:12-13). He died and was buried with great honor (16:14).

Asa was a king of Judah who ruled for 41 years. Early in his reign, he did a lot of good -- he removed idols and false altars from the land and even took away his own grandmother's royal position because she had made an idol. When a huge army from Cush attacked, Asa prayed and trusted God for the victory, and God delivered Judah. But later in life, when the king of Israel threatened Judah, Asa didn't trust God the way he used to -- he paid a foreign king for help instead of asking the LORD, and a prophet warned him about it. Asa's story shows both real faithfulness and how easy it is to slip away from trusting God, even after years of walking with Him.

Thought for Today

Asa’s story teaches us about religious reform; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
