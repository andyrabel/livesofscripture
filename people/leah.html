Leah (F)

First named in Genesis 29:16-35

OT Patriarchal

Leah, Laban's older daughter, was substituted for her sister Rachel on Jacob's wedding night through her father's deception, becoming Jacob's wife though he had worked seven years to marry Rachel instead (Genesis 29:16-25). Genesis states plainly that Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah, but adds that 'the LORD saw that Leah was unloved, and He opened her womb,' while Rachel remained barren (29:30-31) -- God's attention landing precisely where human affection did not. Leah bore Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah in succession, naming each with words that trace her shifting hope: first that Jacob would now love her, then simple praise to the LORD at Judah's birth (29:32-35). Later, in a domestic rivalry with Rachel over mandrakes and children, she bore Issachar, Zebulun, and a daughter, Dinah (30:14-21). Leah is listed among the ancestors buried with Jacob in the cave at Machpelah, not Rachel, who died in childbirth on the road (49:31; 35:19-20). Her son Judah became the ancestor of David's royal line and ultimately of Christ, and Ruth 4:11 later invokes both Leah and Rachel as builders of the house of Israel. Leah's story is Scripture's clear witness that God's covenant purposes advanced through the wife who was overlooked by her husband, not the one who was preferred.

Leah was Laban's older daughter. Her father tricked Jacob into marrying her before he could marry her sister Rachel, whom Jacob truly loved. Because Leah wasn't loved the way Rachel was, it could have been a very sad story -- but the Bible says God saw that Leah was unloved and blessed her with children, including sons named Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah. Leah named her sons with prayers of hope that Jacob would love her more, and eventually with simple praise to God. Judah, Leah's son, became the ancestor of King David and, much later, of Jesus Himself -- meaning God's rescue plan for the world ran directly through the wife who felt overlooked.

References: Genesis 29:16-35; Genesis 30:9-21; Genesis 35:23; Genesis 49:31; Ruth 4:11
