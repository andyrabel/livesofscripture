Eve (F)

Name means “Living; enlivening” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 3:20

OT Primeval History

Eve is formed by God from Adam's rib after none of the animals proves to be a companion "corresponding to" Adam (Genesis 2:18-23); Adam names her "Woman" and later "Eve," "because she was the mother of all the living" (3:20). She and Adam are together commanded not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and it is Eve the serpent approaches, twisting God's word ("Indeed, has God said...?") until she believes the fruit is "good for food... a delight to the eyes, and... desirable to make one wise" (3:1-6). She eats, gives some to Adam, who eats without objection, and the couple's eyes are opened -- not to wisdom, but to shame (3:7). When God confronts her, Eve names the deception honestly: "The serpent deceived me, and I ate" (3:13); Paul later notes specifically that Eve, not Adam, was deceived, while Adam sinned with full knowledge (1 Timothy 2:14) -- Scripture assigns the two of them different, not lesser, culpability. God's judgment on the serpent includes the first gospel promise in Scripture: Eve's offspring will one day crush the serpent's head, at the cost of being struck himself (3:15) -- a promise that looks forward to Christ. Eve is driven with Adam from Eden, bears Cain, Abel, and later Seth, and calls Cain's birth a gift "with the help of the LORD" (4:1) -- even after the fall, still trusting God for life.

Eve was the first woman God ever made. God formed her from a rib taken from Adam, so she would have a companion just like him. Adam and Eve lived together in the beautiful garden of Eden, caring for it and enjoying friendship with God. But a clever serpent lied to Eve, telling her that eating fruit from the one tree God said not to touch would make her wise like God. Eve believed the lie, ate the fruit, and gave some to Adam, who ate it too. This first sin brought sadness and hardship into the world. But right after, God made a promise: that one day, someone from Eve's own family line would defeat the serpent for good -- a promise that points forward to Jesus. Eve became "the mother of all the living," and later she and Adam had more children, including Seth, and kept trusting God.

Thought for Today

Eve’s story teaches us about creation; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 2:18-25; Genesis 3:1-24; Genesis 4:1-2; Genesis 4:25; 2 Corinthians 11:3; 1 Timothy 2:13-14
