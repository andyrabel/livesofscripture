Shechem (M)

Name means “Part; portion; back early in the morning” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 33:19

OT Patriarchal

Shechem, son of Hamor the Hivite and prince of the city that bore his family's name, saw Dinah, Jacob's daughter, seized her, and raped her (Genesis 34:1-2). Scripture does not soften this: he "violated her." Afterward, however, "his soul was strongly attracted to Dinah," and he asked his father to arrange marriage (34:3-4). Hamor proposed intermarriage and trade between the two peoples; Shechem himself, undeterred by what he had done, offered any bride-price and dowry the family required (34:8-12). Dinah's brothers answered deceitfully: they would agree only if every male of the city was circumcised. Shechem and Hamor persuaded their city to accept the terms, and "Shechem did not delay to do the thing, because he was delighted with Jacob's daughter" (34:19). Three days later, while the men of the city were still in pain, Simeon and Levi -- Dinah's full brothers -- attacked, killed every man including Shechem and Hamor, plundered the city, and took Dinah out of Shechem's house (34:25-29). Jacob rebuked his sons for endangering the family among the Canaanites, but they replied, "Should he treat our sister as a harlot?" (34:30-31). The episode leaves no one blameless -- Shechem's violence, and his sons' violent deceit in response -- a chapter Genesis narrates without endorsing any party's conduct.

Shechem was a young prince whose father, Hamor, ruled the city of Shechem in Canaan. Shechem did something terribly wrong to Jacob's daughter Dinah, hurting and dishonoring her. Afterward, he wanted to marry her and asked his father to arrange it with Jacob's family. Jacob's sons were furious about what had happened to their sister. Two of them, Simeon and Levi, tricked Shechem's whole city and then attacked it, killing Shechem, his father, and many others, and rescuing Dinah. It is a sad, difficult story that shows how one person's sin can lead to terrible pain and violence for many people -- and reminds us how much the world needed a Savior.

References: Genesis 33:19; Genesis 34:2-26; Joshua 24:32; Judges 9:28
