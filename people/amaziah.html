Amaziah (M)

First named in 2 Kings 12:21

OT Divided Monarchy

Amaziah became king of Judah at twenty-five after his father Joash was assassinated by his own servants. He executed the assassins but, in careful obedience to the Law of Moses, spared their children, "each one shall be put to death for his own sin" (2 Kings 14:5-6; Deuteronomy 24:16). He then mustered an army of Judah and hired 100,000 Israelite mercenaries for a campaign against Edom -- but a prophet warned him that the LORD was not with Israel's army, so Amaziah dismissed the mercenaries at a steep financial loss, trusting God's word over his investment (2 Chronicles 25:6-10). He went on to defeat Edom decisively, killing thousands and capturing their capital (25:11-12; 2 Kings 14:7). Victory brought pride and apostasy: Amaziah took the gods of the defeated Edomites, set them up, and worshiped them -- and when a prophet confronted him, he silenced the man with threats rather than repenting (2 Chronicles 25:14-16). Flush with success, Amaziah then recklessly challenged King Jehoash of Israel to battle despite Jehoash's warning parable that he was inviting his own destruction (25:17-19; 2 Kings 14:8-10). Judah was routed, Jerusalem's wall was broken down, and the temple was plundered (2 Kings 14:11-14). Amaziah lived another fifteen years under a conspiracy that finally caught him at Lachish, where he was assassinated (14:17-20).

Amaziah became king of Judah after his father, King Joash, was murdered by his own officials. Amaziah punished the men responsible, but he obeyed God's law by not punishing their children for what their parents did. Amaziah went to war against Edom and won a big victory. But afterward, he made a serious mistake -- he brought back the statues of Edom's gods and started worshiping them instead of the LORD, even though a prophet warned him not to. Feeling proud after his win, Amaziah then challenged the king of Israel to battle, even though he was warned against it. Judah lost badly, Jerusalem's walls were broken down, and years later, Amaziah was killed in a conspiracy against him.

Thought for Today

Amaziah’s story teaches us about partial obedience; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Kings 12:21; 2 Kings 13:12; 2 Kings 14:1-23; 2 Kings 15:1-3; 1 Chronicles 3:12; 2 Chronicles 24:27; 2 Chronicles 25:1-27; 2 Chronicles 26:1-4
