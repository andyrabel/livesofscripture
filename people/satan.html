Satan (M)

OT United Monarchy

Satan (Hebrew for "adversary") first appears by that name accusing Job before God's heavenly court, testing whether Job's faithfulness would survive the loss of everything he had -- though Job never once curses God (Job 1-2). In 1 Chronicles 21:1, Satan incites David to take a sinful census of Israel, bringing God's judgment on the nation; the parallel account in 2 Samuel 24:1 attributes the same event to the LORD's anger, an unresolved tension Scripture leaves standing rather than explaining away. In Zechariah's vision, "the satan" stands to accuse the high priest Joshua before the LORD's angel, only to be rebuked (Zechariah 3:1-2). The New Testament names him plainly as tempter: after Jesus's baptism, Satan tested Him three times in the wilderness with appeals to appetite, presumption, and power, and each time Jesus answered from Scripture and refused (Matthew 4:1-11). Jesus calls him "a liar and the father of lies" and "a murderer from the beginning" (John 8:44), and Paul warns that he disguises himself "as an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14). Satan entered Judas before the betrayal (Luke 22:3) and demanded to sift Peter "like wheat" (Luke 22:31). Revelation portrays him as the great dragon, deceiver of the nations, whose defeat is already secured by Christ and who will finally be "thrown into the lake of fire" forever (Revelation 20:10) -- Scripture's real but always subordinate, already-defeated enemy.

Satan (a name that means "accuser" or "adversary") is the Bible's name for the being who opposes God and tries to lead people away from Him. We first meet him testing Job's faith by bringing him terrible troubles, always within limits God set. He also tried to tempt Jesus to sin when Jesus was alone in the wilderness for forty days -- but Jesus resisted every temptation by trusting God's word. Throughout the Bible, Satan is described as a liar, an accuser, and an enemy of God's people, but he is never shown to be equal to God in power. The Bible's story ends with Satan's complete and final defeat, judged forever, while God's people live safely with Him.
