Micaiah (M)

First named in 1 Kings 22:1-28

OT Divided Monarchy

Micaiah, son of Imlah, is introduced only when King Ahab of Israel, planning a joint campaign with King Jehoshaphat of Judah against Aram at Ramoth-gilead, is pressed by Jehoshaphat to consult a prophet of the LORD, since Ahab's 400 court prophets had already unanimously promised victory (1 Kings 22:1-6). Ahab admitted plainly, 'there is yet one man by whom we may inquire of the LORD, but I hate him, because he does not prophesy good concerning me, but evil... Micaiah' (22:8). Summoned, Micaiah first answered sarcastically in the same false tone as the other prophets, but when pressed by Ahab to tell the truth, he described a vision of Israel scattered like sheep without a shepherd and of God allowing a deceiving spirit into the mouths of Ahab's prophets specifically to lure Ahab to his death at Ramoth-gilead (22:15-23). One of the false prophets, Zedekiah, struck Micaiah on the cheek in anger, and Ahab had Micaiah imprisoned on scant bread and water until his safe return -- to which Micaiah replied, 'If you indeed return safely the LORD has not spoken by me,' calling on all the people present as witnesses (22:24-28). Ahab went to battle anyway, disguising himself, but was struck by a stray arrow and died that evening, exactly as Micaiah alone, against four hundred voices, had faithfully declared.

Micaiah was a true prophet of the LORD during the reign of wicked King Ahab. When Ahab wanted to go to war against Aram, he gathered 400 prophets who all cheerfully predicted victory. But Ahab reluctantly also called for Micaiah, admitting he hated Micaiah because Micaiah never told him what he wanted to hear -- only the truth. Sure enough, Micaiah alone predicted Israel's defeat and Ahab's own death in the battle. For telling the truth, Micaiah was slapped and thrown into prison on bread and water. Despite the pressure to lie like everyone else, Micaiah's prophecy came true exactly as he said, and his courage to speak truth to a powerful king, even when it cost him, is a lasting example of faithfulness.

References: 1 Kings 22:1-28; 2 Chronicles 18:1-27
