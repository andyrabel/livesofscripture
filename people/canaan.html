Canaan (M)

Name means “Merchant; trader; or that humbles and subdues” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 9:18-27

OT Primeval History

Canaan was the son of Ham and grandson of Noah (Genesis 9:18; 10:6). After the flood, Noah planted a vineyard, drank its wine, became drunk, and lay uncovered inside his tent. Ham "saw the nakedness of his father" and told his two brothers, Shem and Japheth, who instead walked in backward and covered Noah without looking (9:20-23). When Noah awoke and learned what Ham had done, he pronounced judgment -- not on Ham directly, but on Ham's son: "Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants he shall be to his brothers," followed by a blessing on Shem and Japheth (9:24-27). Scripture does not explain why the curse fell on Canaan rather than on Ham himself, and interpreters differ over exactly what Ham's offense involved and why his son bore the consequence. This passage has a painful history of misuse -- for centuries it was wrongly invoked to justify the enslavement of Black Africans, a reading the text itself does not support: Canaan's descendants are simply the peoples later called Canaanites who settled the land later promised to Abraham (10:15-19), and the text nowhere mentions skin color or any particular race. Canaan fathered Sidon, Heth, and the ancestors of the Jebusites, Amorites, and other Canaanite peoples (10:15-18), the very nations Israel would later be commanded to dispossess in the conquest of the promised land.

Canaan was one of Noah's grandsons, the son of Ham. After the flood, Noah planted a vineyard, drank too much wine, and was found uncovered in his tent. Ham saw this and told his brothers instead of covering their father with respect, but Shem and Japheth walked in backward and covered Noah without looking. When Noah woke up and learned what had happened, he spoke a curse over Canaan, saying Canaan's descendants would serve the descendants of Shem and Japheth. Canaan grew up to father many peoples who later lived in the land of Canaan -- the land God would one day promise to Abraham and his family. Canaan's story is a sad reminder of how one family's sin had consequences that lasted for generations.

Thought for Today

Canaan’s story teaches us about Noah's curse; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 9:18-27; Genesis 10:6-15; Genesis 28:1-8; Genesis 31:18; Genesis 36:2; 1 Chronicles 1:8-13
