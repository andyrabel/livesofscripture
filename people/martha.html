Martha

NT Gospels

Sister of Lazarus and Mary in Bethany, known for her hospitality toward Jesus and gentle rebuke for being anxious over serving, who made a striking confession of faith at Lazarus's tomb.

For younger readers

Martha lived in Bethany with her siblings Mary and Lazarus, and the family was close with Jesus. In one story, when Jesus visited their home, Martha was busy preparing food and serving, while her sister Mary simply sat and listened to Jesus teach. Martha grew frustrated and asked Jesus to tell Mary to help her, but Jesus gently told Martha that Mary had chosen the better priority in that moment. Later, when Lazarus died, Martha was the first to go out and meet Jesus as He arrived, telling Him with real faith that she believed He was the Christ, the Son of God, and that even now God would give Him whatever He asked. Martha's story shows real growth -- from an anxious, busy hostess to someone who spoke one of the clearest declarations of faith in the whole Gospel of John.

Life Story

Martha welcomed Jesus into her home in Bethany, but while she busied herself with the many preparations of hosting, her sister Mary sat at Jesus's feet listening to His teaching instead of helping (Luke 10:38-40). Martha, "distracted with all her preparations," complained to Jesus and asked Him to tell Mary to help her; Jesus responded gently but pointedly, telling her she was "worried and bothered about so many things," while "only one thing is necessary," and Mary had chosen it (10:40-42). This brief scene is often read as a caution against letting good service crowd out attentiveness to Jesus Himself, though Martha's hospitality itself was never condemned. When Lazarus died, it was Martha who went out first to meet Jesus on the road while Mary remained at home, telling Him plainly, "if You had been here, my brother would not have died," yet immediately following it with continued trust: "even now I know that whatever You ask of God, God will give You" (John 11:20-22). When Jesus told her Lazarus would rise again, Martha responded with a remarkably clear confession: "I do believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God, even He who comes into the world" (11:24-27) -- one of the most direct statements of faith in Jesus's identity anywhere in the Gospels, spoken in the middle of raw grief before the miracle had even happened. Martha's story traces real growth from anxious busyness to confident, articulate faith.

First named in Luke 10:38-41

References: Luke 10:38-41; John 11:1-39; John 12:2
