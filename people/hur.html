Hur

OT Exodus/Wilderness

A leader of Israel during the wilderness years who, with Aaron, held up Moses' hands during the battle against Amalek and was left in charge of the people alongside Aaron while Moses was on Mount Sinai; grandfather of Bezalel, chief craftsman of the tabernacle.

For younger readers

Hur was one of the leaders of Israel during the years the people traveled through the wilderness after leaving Egypt. When Israel fought a battle against the Amalekites, Moses stood on a hill holding up his hands, and as long as his hands were raised, Israel was winning. But Moses grew tired, so Hur and Aaron stood on either side of him, holding up his arms until the battle was won (Exodus 17:10-12). Later, when Moses went up Mount Sinai to receive God's law, he left Hur and Aaron in charge of the people (Exodus 24:14). Hur's grandson, Bezalel, grew up to be the chief craftsman who built the tabernacle and its furnishings, filled by God with special skill for the work.

Life Story

Hur first appears at Rephidim during Israel's battle against Amalek. As Joshua led Israel's fighting men, Moses stood on a hilltop holding up the staff of God, and "whenever Moses held his hand up, Israel prevailed, and whenever he let his hand down, Amalek prevailed" (Exodus 17:11). When Moses' arms grew heavy, Aaron and Hur set a stone under him to sit on, then "supported his hands, one on one side and one on the other," steadying them until sunset and Israel's victory (17:12-13). Later, as Moses prepared to ascend Mount Sinai to receive the law, he left Aaron and Hur in charge of the people below, telling the elders, "whoever has a legal matter, let him approach them" (24:14) -- a position of real civil authority during Moses' forty-day absence, the same period in which the people made the golden calf. Genealogies in 1 Chronicles identify Hur as a son of Caleb of the tribe of Judah and the father of Uri, whose son Bezalel became the tabernacle's master craftsman, filled by God's Spirit "with skill, with intelligence, with knowledge and in all craftsmanship" (Exodus 31:2-3; 1 Chronicles 2:19-20). Hur is never given his own speech or crisis in Scripture, but he appears twice at pivotal moments of Israel's wilderness leadership, and his family line produced the man who built the tabernacle itself.
