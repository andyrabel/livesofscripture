Goliath (M)

First named in 1 Samuel 17:4-57

OT United Monarchy

Goliath was a champion of the Philistine army from Gath, described as towering in height and armored head to foot in bronze, who for forty days taunted the armies of Israel encamped in the Valley of Elah to send a man to fight him one-on-one, with the losing nation becoming the other's servants (1 Samuel 17:1-11). King Saul and his soldiers were terrified, but the young shepherd David -- sent by his father to bring provisions to his brothers in the camp -- was indignant that this "uncircumcised Philistine" should defy "the armies of the living God" (17:26). Refusing Saul's armor, David went out with only his shepherd's staff, a sling, and five smooth stones, declaring that he came "in the name of the LORD of hosts" and that the battle belonged to God, not to sword or spear (17:45-47). David felled Goliath with a single stone to the forehead, then used the giant's own sword to kill him and cut off his head, sending the Philistine army fleeing (17:48-51). Goliath's defeat became a defining moment establishing David's reputation (18:6-7) and was still remembered decades later, when David took Goliath's sword with him while fleeing Saul (21:9). Later biblical texts describe further Philistine giants from Gath killed by David's men, including a warrior named Lahmi, called "the brother of Goliath" (1 Chronicles 20:5; compare 2 Samuel 21:19). Goliath's story is remembered above all as a demonstration that victory belongs to God, not to human size or strength.

Goliath was a huge Philistine soldier, so tall and strong that he scared the whole army of Israel. Every day for forty days, he challenged the Israelites to send one man to fight him, but everyone was too afraid -- even King Saul. Then a young shepherd boy named David arrived at the battle camp to bring food to his brothers. David couldn't believe that anyone would be allowed to mock God's people this way. He told Saul that God, who had helped him defeat lions and bears while watching his sheep, would help him defeat Goliath too. David refused armor and a sword, choosing instead his sling and five smooth stones. With his very first throw, David knocked Goliath down, and the Philistine army ran away in fear. David's courage came from his trust that God, not the size of a soldier, wins the battle.

Thought for Today

Through Goliath’s experience of God's power over human strength, God teaches us to trust His wisdom and follow Jesus faithfully.

References: 1 Samuel 17:4-57; 1 Samuel 18:6; 1 Samuel 21:9; 1 Samuel 22:10; 2 Samuel 21:19; 1 Chronicles 20:5
