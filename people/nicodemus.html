Nicodemus (M)

First named in John 3:1-9

NT Gospels

Nicodemus, a Pharisee and "a ruler of the Jews" -- likely a member of the Sanhedrin -- came to Jesus by night, an approach that suggests either caution about being seen with Him publicly or simply a preference for uninterrupted conversation (John 3:1-2). He opened respectfully, acknowledging Jesus as "a teacher come from God," and Jesus responded with one of His most foundational teachings: "unless one is born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God" (3:2-3). Nicodemus, a recognized "teacher of Israel," struggled to grasp the spiritual meaning, asking how a grown man could enter his mother's womb a second time, and Jesus clarified He meant a birth "of water and the Spirit," a work of God's Spirit rather than human effort or heritage (3:4-10). The conversation includes what may be Jesus's own words or John's commentary building toward the Gospel's most quoted verse: "For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son..." (3:16). Nicodemus reappears twice more, each time showing gradually more open courage: he cautioned his fellow Sanhedrin members against condemning Jesus without a fair hearing, provoking their scorn (7:50-52), and after the crucifixion, he came openly in daylight with about seventy-five pounds of myrrh and aloes to help Joseph of Arimathea prepare Jesus's body for burial (19:39-40). Nicodemus's arc, from secretive nighttime visitor to public participant in Jesus's burial, traces a quiet but real growth from curiosity into costly devotion.

Nicodemus was an important religious leader and teacher among the Jews, part of a ruling council. He was curious about Jesus but nervous about being seen with Him publicly, so he came to talk with Jesus secretly at night. Jesus told him that to enter the kingdom of God, a person must be "born again" -- not physically, but spiritually, through the Holy Spirit. Nicodemus didn't fully understand at first, but the conversation clearly stayed with him. Later, when other religious leaders wanted to condemn Jesus without a fair hearing, Nicodemus spoke up in His defense. After Jesus died on the cross, Nicodemus helped provide expensive spices and assisted in preparing Jesus's body for burial, showing that his private curiosity had grown into real, though quiet, courage and faith.

Thought for Today

Nicodemus’s story teaches us about new birth; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: John 3:1-9; John 7:50; John 19:39
