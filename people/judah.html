Judah (M)

First named in Genesis 29:35

OT Patriarchal

Judah, Jacob's fourth son by Leah, proposed selling Joseph to Ishmaelite traders rather than killing him outright (Genesis 37:26-27) -- self-interested, but it spared Joseph's life. He then married a Canaanite wife, and after his sons Er and Onan died under God's judgment, failed to give his daughter-in-law Tamar to his third son as promised. Tamar disguised herself and conceived by Judah himself; when he threatened to have her burned for apparent immorality, she revealed he was the father, and Judah admitted, "she is more righteous than I" (Genesis 38:1-26) -- a rare moment of self-indicting honesty. Years later in Egypt, facing the unknowing Joseph's demand to keep Benjamin as a slave, Judah offered himself in Benjamin's place rather than let their father Jacob lose another beloved son, a striking reversal of the man who once sold his own brother (44:18-34). Jacob's deathbed blessing gave Judah preeminence among the tribes: "the scepter shall not depart from Judah... until Shiloh comes" (49:8-10), and the tribe of Judah became Israel's leading tribe, providing its kings from David onward. Judah's arc -- from selling a brother into slavery to offering himself for one -- models repentance's real shape: not merely regret, but changed action when the same kind of test comes again.

Judah was one of Jacob's twelve sons and the ancestor of the tribe of Judah, which King David and eventually Jesus would come from. As a young man, Judah made bad choices -- it was his idea to sell his brother Joseph as a slave instead of killing him, and he treated his daughter-in-law Tamar unfairly. But Judah changed over time. Years later, when the brothers returned to Egypt and Joseph (now a powerful ruler) threatened to keep their youngest brother Benjamin as a slave, Judah bravely offered to take Benjamin's place, showing how much he had grown. Jacob's final blessing promised that kings would come from Judah's family line -- a promise that pointed all the way to Jesus.

Thought for Today

Judah offered himself in Benjamin's place rather than let their father lose another son. Jesus offered Himself in our place so we would not bear what we deserved.
