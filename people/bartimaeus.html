Bartimaeus (M)

Name means “Son of Timaeus” — Aramaic

First named in Mark 10:46

NT Gospels

Bartimaeus, whose name means "son of Timaeus," was a blind beggar sitting by the roadside as Jesus left Jericho with a large crowd (Mark 10:46). Hearing that Jesus of Nazareth was passing, he began crying out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" -- a striking messianic title from a man who could not see, showing spiritual insight the sighted crowd lacked (10:47). Many in the crowd told him sharply to be quiet, but he only shouted louder, refusing to let the moment pass (10:48). Jesus stopped and called for him; Bartimaeus threw off his cloak -- likely his only real possession, spread out to collect coins -- and sprang up to come to Jesus (10:49-50). Jesus asked him directly, "what do you want Me to do for you?", the identical question He had just asked James and John when they requested positions of honor (10:36); Bartimaeus's request could not have been more different: "Rabboni, I want to regain my sight!" (10:51). Jesus healed him on the spot, telling him, "go; your faith has made you well," and Bartimaeus, rather than simply returning to his old life, "began following Him on the road" toward Jerusalem and the cross (10:52). His story is a vivid picture of persistent, undeterred faith rewarded, and of true sight leading immediately to discipleship.

Bartimaeus was a blind man who sat by the road near Jericho, begging for money to survive, since he had no other way to support himself. When he heard that Jesus of Nazareth was passing by, he began shouting loudly, calling Jesus "Son of David" and asking for mercy. Many people in the crowd told him to be quiet, but Bartimaeus refused to stop, shouting even louder. Jesus heard him and stopped, calling him over. Bartimaeus threw off his cloak, jumped up, and came to Jesus, who asked what he wanted. Bartimaeus asked to see again, and Jesus healed him immediately, telling him his faith had made him well. Instead of just walking away healed, Bartimaeus chose to follow Jesus down the road as a disciple.

Thought for Today

Bartimaeus kept crying out to Jesus though the crowd told him to be quiet. May we call out to Jesus just as boldly.
