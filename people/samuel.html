Samuel

OT Judges

Born to Hannah in answer to prayer and dedicated to the LORD from infancy, Samuel served as Israel's last judge and a prophet who anointed both Saul and David as king, confronting Saul's disobedience directly and mourning him even after rejecting him as king.

For younger readers

Samuel was given to God by his mother Hannah, who had prayed for a son for a long time and promised to give him back to serve God if her prayer was answered. As a boy growing up in God's house, Samuel heard God's voice calling to him at night and learned to listen and obey. Samuel grew up to become a respected prophet and leader for the whole nation of Israel. When the people asked for a king, Samuel warned them what that would cost, but he obeyed God and anointed Saul as the first king anyway. Later, Samuel anointed young David as the next king, choosing him because God looks at a person's heart instead of their outward appearance. Samuel's story shows the importance of listening closely to God and being willing to say hard things, even when they're not popular.

Interpretive note: 1 Samuel 28:3-19 describes a figure identified as Samuel appearing after Saul consults a medium at En-dor, despite Samuel having forbidden such practices during his lifetime. Interpreters are divided over whether this was genuinely Samuel's spirit, permitted by God for this one final pronouncement of judgment on Saul, or a deceptive apparition. Scripture does not explain the mechanism, only that a true and devastating prophecy of Saul's coming death was delivered.

Life Story

Samuel was born to Hannah, a barren woman who prayed with such anguish at Shiloh that the priest Eli mistook her for drunk, and who vowed any son God gave her would be given back to serve the LORD for life (1 Samuel 1:1-20). Once weaned, Samuel was brought to Shiloh to live and serve under Eli, visited only once a year (1 Samuel 1:24-28; 2:19). As a boy, Samuel heard the LORD calling his name at night, and after mistaking the voice for Eli three times, was told to answer, 'Speak, LORD, for Your servant is listening' — his first message was judgment against Eli's own household, which Samuel faithfully delivered (1 Samuel 3:1-18). He was recognized as a prophet whose word never failed, leading Israel to victory over the Philistines and judging Israel for life (1 Samuel 3:19-20; 7:3-17). In old age, with his sons perverting justice as judges, Israel demanded a king 'like all the nations' — really a rejection of God's kingship — and Samuel anointed Saul anyway at God's direction (1 Samuel 8; 10:1). Samuel confronted Saul's disobedience twice, decisively when Saul spared King Agag against God's command, prompting the rebuke 'to obey is better than sacrifice' (1 Samuel 13:8-14; 15:10-29). At Bethlehem, Samuel anointed the least likely of Jesse's sons, David, after God told him not to judge by outward appearance, 'for the LORD looks at the heart' (1 Samuel 16:1-13). Samuel stands at the hinge of Israel's history, anointing its first two kings.
