Eutychus (M)

Name means “Happy; fortunate” — Greek

First named in Acts 20:9

NT Apostolic

Eutychus was a young man present at a late-night gathering in Troas, where believers had assembled to break bread on the first day of the week and Paul, about to depart the next morning, "prolonged his message until midnight" (Acts 20:6-7). The room was crowded and lit with many lamps, likely warm and airless, and Eutychus, sitting in a third-story window, was overcome with sleep as Paul kept talking; he fell fast asleep and tumbled down from the window to the ground below (20:8-9). He was picked up dead. Paul went down, threw himself on the young man, and embraced him, telling the alarmed crowd, "do not be troubled, for his life is in him" (20:10) -- language that closely echoes similar accounts of the prophets Elijah and Elisha raising the dead (1 Kings 17:21-22; 2 Kings 4:34-35), and that Luke, the physician-author of Acts, records as a genuine raising to life rather than merely a survived fall. The gathering then returned upstairs, shared the meal, and Paul kept talking until daybreak before finally departing (20:11). Luke closes the account with a simple, understated line: "they took away the boy alive, and were greatly comforted" (20:12). The episode, part practical mishap and part genuine miracle, is one of the New Testament's more vivid, almost wry glimpses into the ordinary human texture of the early church's gatherings.

Eutychus was a young man attending a late-night gathering in Troas where Paul was preaching, since Paul was set to leave the next day and had a great deal to teach the believers there. The meeting went on so long -- with many oil lamps burning in the crowded upstairs room -- that Eutychus, sitting in a window, fell into a deep sleep and tumbled all the way down from the third story. He was picked up dead. Paul went down, embraced him, and told the alarmed crowd not to be troubled, because his life was still in him. Everyone went back upstairs, and after eating together, Paul kept talking until daybreak. The people were greatly comforted that Eutychus had been brought back alive, in what is remembered as one of the more surprising and memorable moments recorded in the book of Acts.

Thought for Today

Eutychus fell dead from a window during Paul's long sermon, and Paul raised him back to life. Jesus alone is the source of life that conquers death.
