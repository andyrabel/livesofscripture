Philemon (M)

First named in Philemon 1:1

NT Apostolic

Philemon was a Christian in Colossae, apparently converted through Paul's ministry ("you owe to me even your own self," Philemon 1:19), wealthy enough to host a church in his home and known for love that had "refreshed the hearts of the saints" (1:2, 5-7). His slave Onesimus ran away, apparently after wronging him in some way, eventually crossing paths with the imprisoned Paul and coming to faith in Christ (1:10-11, 18). Paul's brief letter to Philemon is a masterwork of persuasion built entirely on relationship rather than command: Paul calls himself "an old man and now also a prisoner of Christ Jesus," appeals to Philemon's love, and sends Onesimus back with the letter, urging Philemon to receive him no longer as a slave but "as a beloved brother" -- explicitly in the flesh and in the Lord (1:9, 16). Paul offers to personally repay anything Onesimus owes, even as he gently reminds Philemon of the far greater spiritual debt Philemon himself owes Paul (1:18-19). He closes expecting to visit and asks Philemon to prepare him a guest room, expressing confidence Philemon will do even more than asked (1:21-22). The letter never explicitly demands Philemon free Onesimus outright, but its relentless appeal to brotherhood in Christ -- a slave and his master now equally "beloved" -- planted a seed that would eventually work to unravel slavery's logic entirely from within the Christian conscience.

Philemon was a Christian in the city of Colossae who had likely come to faith through the apostle Paul's ministry, and he hosted a church that met in his own home. Philemon owned a slave named Onesimus who ran away from him, but Onesimus later met Paul and became a Christian himself. Paul wrote Philemon a warm, personal letter -- one of the shortest books in the Bible -- asking him to forgive Onesimus and welcome him back not as a slave, but as a beloved brother in Christ. Paul even offered to personally cover any debt Onesimus owed. This letter shows how the gospel was meant to change relationships between people, encouraging real forgiveness and treating former slaves and masters as family.

Thought for Today

Paul asked Philemon to receive his runaway slave Onesimus not as property but as a beloved brother. In Jesus, slave and master give way to one family.
