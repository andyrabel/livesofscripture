First named in Judges 18:30

Jonathan (M)

OT Judges

Jonathan, a young Levite from Bethlehem, appears anonymously through most of Judges 17 before being named at the chapter's close. Wandering in search of a place to live, he was hired by a man named Micah in the hill country of Ephraim to serve as personal priest for a household shrine Micah had built, complete with a carved image and household idols made partly from silver Micah had stolen from his own mother (Judges 17:1-13). Micah considered securing a Levite priest a guarantee of God's blessing -- itself a distortion of true worship in an era when "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (17:6). Later, warriors from the tribe of Dan, searching for territory to settle, passed through, recognized Jonathan's voice, and asked him to inquire of God on their behalf; when they returned with an armed company to seize Micah's idols and priestly garments, they invited Jonathan along, asking, "Is it better for you to be a priest to the house of one man, or to be priest to a tribe and a family in Israel?" Jonathan agreed and went with them (18:1-20). Micah pursued in protest but was overpowered and returned home. The Danites installed Micah's idol at their new city of Dan, and Jonathan and his descendants served as priests there -- in direct rivalry with the LORD's tabernacle worship -- "until the day of the captivity of the land" (18:27-31).

Jonathan was a young Levite who was supposed to serve God, but during a confusing time in Israel's history "when everyone did what was right in their own eyes," he agreed to become a hired priest for a man named Micah, who had made his own idol to worship instead of the true God. Later, warriors from the tribe of Dan came through, saw Jonathan serving as priest, and convinced him to come be priest for them instead, worshiping the same idol at their new settlement. Jonathan's own children and grandchildren carried on this priesthood at Dan for generations. His story shows how easily true worship of God can be traded away when everyone simply does what seems right to themselves instead of listening to God.
