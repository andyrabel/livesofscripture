Joram

Also called: Hadoram
OT United Monarchy

Son of Toi, king of Hamath, sent by his father with articles of silver, gold, and bronze to greet and congratulate King David after David's victory over their common enemy Hadadezer of Zobah.

For younger readers

Joram (also called Hadoram) was the son of Toi, a king who ruled a place called Hamath. When Toi heard that King David had won a big battle against Hadadezer, a king who had also been fighting against Hamath, he was very glad. Toi sent his son Joram to visit King David, greet him, and congratulate him on the victory. Joram also brought David gifts of silver, gold, and bronze. David later used gifts like these, along with treasure from other conquered nations, for special purposes dedicated to the Lord. Joram's short visit shows how news of David's God-given victories reached and impressed even kings from other lands.

Life Story

Joram, also called Hadoram, was the son of Toi, king of Hamath. When Toi learned that David had defeated Hadadezer, king of Zobah -- a rival who had also warred against Hamath -- he sent his son Joram to David "to greet him and bless him, because he had fought against Hadadezer and had defeated him" (2 Samuel 8:10). Joram brought with him articles of silver, gold, and bronze as gifts. David received these alongside plunder from the many nations he had subdued -- Edom, Moab, the Ammonites, the Philistines, Amalek, and the spoil of Hadadezer himself -- and dedicated them to the LORD, the same treasure his son Solomon later drew on for the temple (2 Samuel 8:11-12; 1 Chronicles 18:8, 11). Joram's brief appearance is a diplomatic footnote to David's wider military success, but it marks a real moment: a foreign king's own son personally acknowledging that Israel's God-given victories were significant enough to require tribute and goodwill, not hostility. The parallel account in 1 Chronicles 18:10 preserves the same episode almost word for word, confirming its place in the record of David's reign at its height.

First named in 2 Samuel 8:10
