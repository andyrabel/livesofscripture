Amnon (M)

First named in 2 Samuel 3:2

OT United Monarchy

Amnon was David's firstborn son, born to Ahinoam of Jezreel (2 Samuel 3:2). Scripture records that he became infatuated with his half-sister Tamar, Absalom's full sister, to the point of physical illness (2 Samuel 13:1-2). On the advice of his cunning friend Jonadab, Amnon feigned sickness to lure Tamar into his private quarters under the pretense of wanting her to prepare food for him -- and there, despite her explicit pleas and warning that such an act would be an outrage in Israel, he raped her (13:3-14). Immediately afterward, "Amnon hated her with a very great hatred... greater than the love with which he had loved her," and he had her thrown out and the door bolted behind her (13:15-17). Tamar was left desolate, and though David heard of it and was furious, he took no action against his son (13:21). Absalom, Tamar's full brother, said nothing to Amnon but nursed his hatred silently for two full years, until he arranged a feast and had his servants kill Amnon at his command (13:22-29). Amnon's crime and death mark the beginning of the violent unraveling of David's household that the prophet Nathan had foretold as consequence for David's own sin with Bathsheba (2 Samuel 12:10-11).

Amnon was King David's oldest son. The Bible tells a very sad and serious story about him: Amnon did something terribly wrong to his half-sister Tamar, hurting her badly and refusing to make it right afterward. David was angry when he found out, but he didn't punish Amnon. Tamar's full brother, Absalom, held onto his anger for two full years. Then Absalom had Amnon killed to get revenge. Amnon's story is one of the saddest in David's family, showing how one person's wrong choice, left unaddressed, led to even more pain and violence in the family that followed.

Thought for Today

Amnon’s story warns us about sexual sin and violence; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
