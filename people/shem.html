Shem (M)

OT Primeval History

Shem was a son of Noah named first in most genealogical lists, though not necessarily Noah's eldest (Genesis 5:32; 9:24; 10:21). He, his wife, and his brothers Ham and Japheth with their wives were among the eight people who survived the flood in the ark (Genesis 7:13). Afterward, Noah planted a vineyard, drank its wine, and lay uncovered and drunk inside his tent. Ham saw his father's nakedness and told his brothers outside rather than covering him; Shem and Japheth instead took a garment, walked in backward, and covered Noah without looking at him (9:20-23). When Noah awoke and learned what had happened, he cursed Ham's son Canaan but blessed Shem directly: "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Shem" (9:26) -- language distinct from the blessing given to Japheth, singling out Shem's line as the one through whom the LORD would particularly be known. Genesis 10 traces Shem's descendants -- Elam, Asshur, Arpachshad, Lud, and Aram -- as ancestors of many peoples of the ancient Near East, and Genesis 11's genealogy narrows that line specifically through Arpachshad to Terah, father of Abram (11:10-26). Through this line Shem stands as an ancestor not only of Israel but, ultimately, through Abraham's family, of the Messiah himself.

Shem was one of Noah's three sons who survived the flood together with their wives. After the flood, Noah planted a vineyard, drank too much wine, and ended up uncovered in his tent. When Shem found out, he and his brother Japheth walked in backward, carrying a garment between them, and covered their father without looking at him -- treating him with respect even in an embarrassing moment. Because of this, Noah blessed Shem in a special way, honoring the LORD as "the God of Shem." Shem's family line, listed in Genesis, leads all the way to Abraham -- and through Abraham's family, eventually to Jesus. Shem is remembered as the ancestor of the Semitic peoples, including Israel.

References: Genesis 5:32; Genesis 6:10; Genesis 7:13; Genesis 9:18-27; Genesis 10:1-31; Genesis 11:10-11; 1 Chronicles 1:4-24; Luke 3:36
