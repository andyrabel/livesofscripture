Tola (M)

First named in Genesis 46:13

OT Judges

Tola, of the tribe of Issachar, "arose to save Israel" after the violent and short-lived kingship of Abimelech, who had massacred his own brothers and was eventually killed by a millstone dropped from a tower (Judges 9:1-57; 10:1). Tola lived in Shamir in the hill country of Ephraim and judged Israel for twenty-three years before he died and was buried there (10:1-2). No battle, deliverance, or moral failure is recorded of him at all -- his entire account is two verses. Coming directly after Abimelech's brutal and self-serving reign, Tola's long, unremarkable tenure reads as a deliberate contrast: after chaos and bloodshed, Israel simply had two decades of a judge who is remembered for nothing except that he "saved Israel" and then quietly served out his years. Scripture does not need every faithful life to be dramatic to record it as significant.

After a very troubled time under a harsh ruler named Abimelech, God raised up a man named Tola to lead and judge Israel. Tola lived in the hill country of Ephraim and led the people faithfully for twenty-three years. The Bible doesn't record any dramatic battles or miracles from his time as judge -- just steady, faithful leadership over many years. Tola's story reminds us that not every leader God uses has a big, dramatic story; sometimes faithfulness over the long haul is exactly what God's people need.

Thought for Today

Tola’s story teaches us about quiet faithfulness; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
