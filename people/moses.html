Moses (M)

Name means “Taken out; drawn forth” — Hebrew

First named in Exodus 2-40

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Moses was born under a death sentence — Pharaoh had ordered every Hebrew infant son drowned — and survived because his mother hid him, then set him adrift in a basket where Pharaoh's own daughter found and raised him at court (Exodus 1:22-2:10). At forty, seeing an Egyptian beating a Hebrew slave, Moses killed the Egyptian and fled into the wilderness (Exodus 2:11-15). He spent forty years as a shepherd in Midian, marrying Zipporah, before God met him at a burning bush and commissioned him to demand Pharaoh let Israel go — a task Moses resisted until God provided his brother Aaron as a spokesman (Exodus 3-4). Through Moses, God struck Egypt with ten plagues, instituted the Passover, and led Israel out through the Red Sea (Exodus 5-14). At Sinai, Moses received the Law and mediated the covenant, a role the New Testament treats as a real but incomplete forerunner of Christ's mediation (Exodus 19-24; Hebrews 3:1-6). Moses was not sinless: he pleaded successfully for Israel after the golden calf, yet later, provoked at Meribah, struck the rock in anger instead of speaking to it as commanded, taking credit alongside God for the water — serious enough that God barred him from the promised land he had led Israel toward for forty years (Numbers 20:1-13; Deuteronomy 34:1-8). Moses died on Mount Nebo, seeing the land only from a distance; Scripture records no prophet like him arose again until the greater one the Law itself anticipated (Deuteronomy 34:10-12; Acts 3:22).

Moses grew up in an Egyptian palace after his mother bravely hid him as a baby to save his life. As a young man, Moses made a terrible mistake and had to run away, spending many years as a shepherd far from home. But God met Moses in an amazing way — through a bush that was on fire but never burned up — and sent him back to Egypt to lead God's people out of slavery. Moses wasn't perfect either; later on, he disobeyed God in anger during a difficult moment, and that mistake kept him from entering the promised land himself. Even so, Moses led God's people faithfully for forty years, received God's Law, and is remembered as one of the greatest leaders in the whole Bible. Moses' story shows that God can use imperfect people to accomplish incredible things.

Thought for Today

Moses led God's people out of slavery in Egypt. Jesus leads us out of something even bigger — sin itself. Whatever has us trapped, may we remember He's the one who can truly set us free.

References: Exodus 2-40; Numbers; Deuteronomy 34
