Isaac (M)

First named in Genesis 21:1-7

OT Patriarchal

Isaac was born to Abraham and Sarah in their old age, exactly as God had promised, his name ('he laughs') carrying his mother's astonished laughter (Genesis 21:1-7). The defining moment came while still young: God commanded Abraham to offer him as a burnt offering on a mountain in Moriah, and Isaac carried the wood for his own sacrifice, asking only where the lamb was, before God stopped Abraham's hand and provided a substitute ram (Genesis 22:1-14). The New Testament treats this as faith so complete Abraham reasoned God could raise Isaac from the dead (Hebrews 11:17-19). Isaac's marriage came through providence: Abraham's servant prayed for a sign at a well, and Rebekah answered it; Isaac loved her, one of the Bible's first statements of a husband's love for his wife (Genesis 24). After twenty years of Rebekah's barrenness, Isaac prayed for her, and she conceived twins, Jacob and Esau, with an oracle that the older would serve the younger (Genesis 25:19-26). Isaac repeated his father's exact failure in Gerar, telling King Abimelech that Rebekah was his sister out of fear (Genesis 26:6-11). In old age, blind and near death, Isaac set out to bless his favored son Esau against God's earlier oracle; Rebekah and Jacob deceived him into blessing Jacob instead, and Isaac let the blessing stand — an act Hebrews 11:20 still counts as faith, entrusting the future to God's promise. Isaac died at 180, and Jacob and Esau together buried him beside Abraham and Sarah (Genesis 35:27-29).

Isaac was the son God had promised to Abraham and Sarah, born when they were already very old. As a young man, Isaac trusted his father completely during an incredibly hard test on a mountain, when Abraham offered him back to God — and God provided another way at the last moment. When Isaac grew up, his father's servant found him a wife named Rebekah, and Isaac loved her deeply. Isaac and Rebekah had twin sons, Jacob and Esau, but the two parents ended up favoring different sons, which caused real problems in their family later on, including a moment where Isaac was tricked into blessing the wrong son. Even so, God's promise kept moving forward through Isaac's family. Isaac's story shows how trust in God can be passed from one generation to the next, imperfectly but faithfully.

Thought for Today

Isaac’s story teaches us about sacrifice; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 21:1-7; Genesis 22; Genesis 24; Genesis 25:19-34; Genesis 26; Genesis 27; Genesis 35:27-29
