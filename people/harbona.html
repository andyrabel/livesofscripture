Harbona (M)

Also called: Harbonah
Name means “Meaning uncertain (possibly Persian)” — Persian

First named in Esther 1:10

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Harbona appears twice in the book of Esther as one of the seven eunuchs who personally served King Ahasuerus of Persia (Esther 1:10). His decisive moment comes at the climax of the story, during Queen Esther's second banquet, when she exposed Haman's plot to destroy her people and identified him before the king as "a foe and an enemy" (7:1-6). As the enraged king stepped out into the palace garden and Haman, in desperation, fell across Esther's couch pleading for his life, the king returned to find what looked like an assault on the queen (7:7-8). At that moment, Harbona spoke up: "Behold indeed, the gallows standing at Haman's house fifty cubits high, which Haman made for Mordecai who spoke good on behalf of the king!" (7:9). The king immediately ordered, "Hang him on it," and Haman was executed on the very structure he had built for his intended victim (7:9-10). Harbona's single, well-timed sentence turns out to be the hinge that seals Haman's downfall and clears the way for Mordecai's rise and the Jews' deliverance later in the book. Nothing else is recorded of him, but his brief intervention fits Esther's larger pattern of God's unseen providence working through ordinary court officials at exactly the right moment.

Harbona was one of the king's personal servants (a eunuch) in the palace of King Ahasuerus in Persia. He is mentioned twice in the book of Esther. At the very end of the story, when the wicked official Haman's evil plan against Mordecai and the Jewish people was being exposed at Queen Esther's banquet, it was Harbona who spoke up. He told the king about the tall gallows Haman had built just to execute Mordecai. Because of what Harbona said, the king ordered Haman to be hanged on the very gallows Haman had built for someone else. Harbona's small but timely words became part of how God's people were rescued in the story of Esther.

Thought for Today

Harbona's single sentence about Haman's gallows sealed the wicked plot's collapse and Israel's rescue. God still works through small, unnamed moments to accomplish salvation, fully in Jesus.
