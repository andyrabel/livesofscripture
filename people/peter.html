Peter (M)

Also called: Simon, Simon Peter, Cephas
Name means “A rock or stone” — Greek

First named in Matthew 4:18-20

NT Gospels

Simon, a Galilean fisherman, was called by Jesus early in His ministry and given the name Peter ('rock'), with his brother Andrew (Matthew 4:18-20; John 1:40-42). He became the most prominent of the twelve apostles — often speaking first, often wrong first. He confessed Jesus as the Christ, the Son of the living God, and heard Jesus call him a rock on which He would build His church (Matthew 16:16-18); minutes later, rebuking Jesus for predicting His own death, Peter was called Satan for setting his mind on man's concerns, not God's (Matthew 16:21-23). Peter walked briefly on water toward Jesus before fear overtook him (Matthew 14:28-31), and at the Last Supper swore he would die before disowning Jesus, only to deny Him three times within hours, exactly as predicted (Matthew 26:33-35, 69-75). Scripture does not omit this collapse to protect Peter's reputation. After the resurrection, the risen Christ restored Peter personally, asking three times whether he loved Him — matching the three denials — and recommissioned him to feed and shepherd His sheep (John 21:15-19). At Pentecost, Peter preached the sermon behind three thousand conversions, and later a vision sent him to the Gentile centurion Cornelius, opening the church's mission beyond Jewish believers (Acts 2; 10). Even after this, Paul publicly opposed Peter at Antioch for withdrawing from table fellowship with Gentile believers under pressure, calling it hypocrisy (Galatians 2:11-14). Tradition, not Scripture, holds Peter was later martyred in Rome, crucified upside down at his own request (John 21:18-19).

Peter was a fisherman who left his nets behind to follow Jesus and became one of His closest friends and apostles. Peter sometimes got things wonderfully right, like when he bravely said Jesus was the promised Messiah — but he also made big mistakes, like when he denied even knowing Jesus three times during Jesus' hardest hour. After Jesus rose from the dead, He lovingly forgave Peter and gave him an important job: taking care of His followers. Peter went on to boldly preach about Jesus and helped many people become believers, even reaching out to people who weren't Jewish, which was a big step for the early church. Peter's story shows that even after failing badly, God can forgive us completely and still use us for important things.

Thought for Today

Peter’s story teaches us about discipleship; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Matthew 4:18-20; Matthew 16:13-23; Matthew 26:69-75; John 21:15-19; Acts 2; Acts 10; Galatians 2:11-14
