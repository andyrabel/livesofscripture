Nabal (M)

First named in 1 Samuel 25:3-39

OT United Monarchy

Nabal was a wealthy Calebite of Maon with three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, married to the wise and beautiful Abigail; Scripture introduces him bluntly as "harsh and evil in his dealings," noting that "as his name is, so is he... Nabal (Fool) is his name and folly is with him" (1 Samuel 25:2-3, 25). David's men had guarded Nabal's shepherds and flocks in the wilderness without taking anything, and at sheep-shearing time David sent a respectful request for provisions in return (25:4-8). Nabal answered with contemptuous insults, denying he even knew who David was (25:10-11). Enraged, David armed four hundred men to wipe out Nabal's household (25:13, 21-22). Abigail intervened without her husband's knowledge, meeting David with generous provisions and a wise appeal that stopped him from bloodshed and needless vengeance (25:18-35). When Abigail told Nabal the next morning, after he had sobered from a drunken feast, "his heart died within him and he became as a stone"; about ten days later, "the LORD struck Nabal and he died" (25:36-38). David recognized God's hand in restraining him from taking vengeance himself, and Abigail became David's wife shortly after (25:39-42). Nabal's story is a stark portrait of wealth without wisdom or gratitude, and of a fool's own folly bringing about his ruin.

Nabal was a very wealthy man who owned huge flocks of sheep and goats. David and his men had protected Nabal's shepherds and flocks out in the wilderness without taking anything for themselves, so when David's men later asked Nabal for some food and supplies, it seemed like a fair request. But Nabal responded rudely, insulting David and refusing to help at all. David, furious, set out to attack Nabal's household. Nabal's wise wife Abigail quickly gathered supplies herself and rode out to meet David, apologizing on her husband's behalf and calming David down before anyone was harmed. When Abigail told Nabal what had happened the next morning, he was so shocked that his heart seemed to fail, and he died about ten days later. Nabal's name literally means "fool," and his story shows the danger of pride and stinginess.

Thought for Today

Nabal’s story warns us about foolishness; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
