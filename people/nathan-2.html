Nathan (M)

First named in 2 Samuel 7:2-17

OT United Monarchy

Nathan served as court prophet during David's reign, delivering God's covenant promise that David's throne would be established forever — a promise the New Testament identifies as fulfilled in Christ (2 Samuel 7:1-16; Luke 1:32-33). When David committed adultery with Bathsheba and arranged her husband Uriah's death, Nathan confronted the king directly with a parable about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb; when David pronounced judgment on the story's villain, Nathan replied, "You are the man!" (2 Samuel 12:1-7). David's genuine repentance in response, recorded in Psalm 51, is credited to Nathan's courageous confrontation of the most powerful man in the kingdom. Later, when David was old and his son Adonijah tried to seize the throne without his father's knowledge, Nathan acted decisively, alerting Bathsheba and orchestrating Solomon's public anointing as king before Adonijah's coup could succeed (1 Kings 1:5-40). Nathan is also credited, alongside the prophet Gad, with writing records of David's reign and later of Solomon's, though these source documents are not preserved as separate books (1 Chronicles 29:29; 2 Chronicles 9:29). Nathan's willingness to confront a king he served made him one of Scripture's clearest examples of prophetic courage exercised for truth rather than convenience.

Nathan was a prophet who served as an advisor to King David. When David did something terribly wrong — taking another man's wife and causing that man's death — Nathan bravely told David a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb. When David got angry at the story, Nathan said, "You are that man!" This helped David realize what he had done and truly say sorry to God. Later in David's life, Nathan also made sure that Solomon, the son God had chosen, became the next king instead of someone trying to grab the throne unfairly. Nathan's story shows how important it is to tell the truth kindly but firmly, even to someone powerful, and how that honesty can lead to real change.

Thought for Today

Nathan’s story teaches us about prophecy; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.
