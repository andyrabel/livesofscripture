Melchizedek (M)

Name means “King of justice” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 14:18-20

OT Patriarchal

After Abram rescued Lot and defeated a coalition of eastern kings, Melchizedek, king of Salem, came out to meet him, bringing bread and wine (Genesis 14:17-18). Genesis identifies him as 'priest of God Most High' (El Elyon) -- the first person in Scripture to bear the title priest -- who blessed Abram in God's name and credited God, not Abram's own strength, with the victory (14:19-20). Abram responded by giving him a tenth of the spoils, an act later writers treat as highly significant. Genesis supplies no genealogy, birth, or death for Melchizedek, an absence Hebrews 7:3 draws attention to directly. Centuries later, Psalm 110:4 declares of the coming Davidic king, 'You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek' -- combining kingship and priesthood in one figure, an unusual pairing since Israel's later law kept the offices separate (kings from Judah, priests from Levi). Hebrews 5-7 develops this at length, arguing that Christ's priesthood is superior to the Levitical priesthood precisely because it follows Melchizedek's pattern rather than Aaron's: a priesthood not based on ancestry or law but on the power of an indestructible life. Melchizedek's brief, mysterious appearance in Genesis becomes, through Hebrews, one of the Old Testament's clearest anticipations of Christ as king and priest together.

After Abram won a big battle to rescue his nephew Lot, a mysterious king named Melchizedek came out to meet him. Melchizedek was both a king of a place called Salem and a priest of God Most High -- someone who represented God to people. He blessed Abram and brought out bread and wine, and Abram gave him a tenth of everything he had. Melchizedek only appears for a few verses in Genesis, but he shows up again much later in the Bible, in Psalms and in Hebrews, where he's used as a picture of Jesus -- a king and a priest at the same time, which is exactly what Jesus is for us.

Thought for Today

Melchizedek’s story teaches us about priesthood; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 14:18-20; Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 5:6-10; Hebrews 7:1-17
