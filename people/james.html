James (M)

First named in Matthew 4:21

NT Gospels

James, son of Zebedee and older brother of John, was mending nets with his father on the Sea of Galilee when Jesus called him; he "immediately left the boat and his father, and followed Him" (Matthew 4:21-22). Jesus nicknamed James and John "Boanerges," or "Sons of Thunder" (Mark 3:17), a name that fits their temperament: they once asked Jesus to call down fire on a Samaritan village that rejected Him (Luke 9:54), and James and John themselves asked Jesus for the two highest seats in His coming kingdom, provoking the other ten disciples' indignation (Mark 10:35-41). Yet James belonged to Jesus' innermost circle: with Peter and John, he alone witnessed the raising of Jairus's daughter (Mark 5:37), Jesus' Transfiguration (Mark 9:2), and His agony in Gethsemane (Mark 14:33), being asked there to "remain here and keep watch" -- and falling asleep instead. James became the first apostle martyred for the faith: Herod Agrippa I "had James the brother of John put to death with a sword" to please the Jewish leaders (Acts 12:2), fulfilling Jesus' earlier warning that James and John would indeed drink His cup of suffering (Mark 10:38-39). Ambitious and imperfect, James nonetheless followed Christ to the point of death.

James was a fisherman on the Sea of Galilee, working with his father Zebedee and his brother John, when Jesus called the two brothers to leave their nets and follow Him. James became one of Jesus' twelve closest followers and part of an even smaller inner group -- with Peter and his brother John -- who were with Jesus at some of His most important moments, like when He was transfigured in dazzling glory on a mountain. Jesus gave James and John the nickname "Sons of Thunder," maybe because of their fiery tempers. James wasn't perfect -- he once asked for a place of honor in Jesus' kingdom -- but he stayed faithful, becoming the very first apostle to die for following Jesus.

Thought for Today

James left his fishing nets the moment Jesus called him, choosing a costly new life. Jesus still calls ordinary people to leave comfort and follow him fully.
