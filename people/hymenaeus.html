Hymenaeus (M)

First named in 1 Timothy 1:18-20

NT Apostolic

Hymenaeus appears twice in Paul's letters to Timothy, both times as a warning example of doctrinal shipwreck. In the first letter, Paul charges Timothy to fight the good fight, 'keeping faith and a good conscience, which some have rejected and suffered shipwreck in regard to their faith,' naming Hymenaeus and Alexander specifically as men Paul had 'handed over to Satan, so that they will be taught not to blaspheme' (1 Timothy 1:18-20) -- language describing a serious act of church discipline, likely expulsion from the fellowship of believers, intended for their correction rather than mere punishment. In the second letter, Paul warns Timothy to avoid 'worldly and empty chatter,' since it leads to further ungodliness, 'and their talk will spread like gangrene, among whom are Hymenaeus and Philetus, men who have gone astray from the truth saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and thus they upset the faith of some' (2 Timothy 2:16-18). This specific false teaching -- that the resurrection was already a completed, purely spiritual event with no future bodily reality yet to come -- struck at a core Christian hope and evidently gained enough traction to genuinely destabilize other believers' faith. Paul's comparison of this teaching to spreading gangrene captures how seriously he viewed the danger: not a minor disagreement, but a corrosive error requiring direct confrontation and correction to protect the wider church.

Hymenaeus was a man Paul mentions in two of his letters to Timothy as a warning. Paul says that Hymenaeus, along with another man, had rejected a good conscience and suffered shipwreck in their faith, leading Paul to hand them over to church discipline. Later, Hymenaeus and a man named Philetus began teaching that the resurrection of believers had already happened -- an idea that wasn't true and that upset the faith of some people who heard it. Paul compares this kind of false teaching to gangrene, spreading and doing serious damage if not stopped. Hymenaeus's story is a warning about how far false teaching can drift from the truth, and how seriously it can harm others.

References: 1 Timothy 1:18-20; 2 Timothy 2:16-18
