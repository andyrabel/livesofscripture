First named in Ezekiel 38:2-18

Gog (M)

OT Exile

Gog appears only in Ezekiel's extended oracle of judgment in chapters 38-39, introduced as "prince of Rosh, Meshech, and Tubal" from "the land of Magog," leading a vast coalition -- including Persia, Cush, Put, Gomer, and Beth-togarmah -- in a massive invasion of Israel "in the latter years" (38:2-9). God declares that He himself will draw Gog into this attack, "to bring you against My land, that the nations may know Me" (38:16), turning what looks like an unstoppable assault into the occasion for His own vindication. The invasion triggers a catastrophic divine response: a great earthquake, panic among Gog's own troops turning them against each other, plague, torrential rain, hailstones, and fire (38:19-22). Ezekiel 39 describes Gog's defeat in graphic detail -- his weapons fueling Israel's fires for seven years, his armies' bodies requiring seven months to bury, and birds and beasts summoned to a gruesome "sacrificial feast" on the corpses of the fallen (39:9-20). Interpreters differ sharply on how to read this: as a specific future invasion, a symbolic portrait of every hostile power arrayed against God's people (an image Revelation 20:8 later reuses for the final rebellion before judgment), or an oracle addressed to threats nearer Ezekiel's own historical horizon. What the text itself states plainly is God's purpose in it: "I will magnify Myself, sanctify Myself, and make Myself known in the eyes of many nations. Then they will know that I am the LORD" (38:23).

In the book of Ezekiel, God gives the prophet a message about someone named Gog, a powerful leader from a faraway land who will one day gather a huge army to attack God's people. God says that even though this army will look unstoppable, He himself will step in to defeat Gog and rescue His people, showing every nation that He alone is the true God. Bible teachers don't all agree on exactly who Gog represents or when this happens -- some think it points to a specific future event, others think it's a picture of every enemy that ever rises up against God's people. Either way, the message is the same: no army, however strong, can stand against God's power and His promise to protect His own.

References: Ezekiel 38:2-18; Ezekiel 39:1-11
