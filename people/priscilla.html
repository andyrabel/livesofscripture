Priscilla

Also called: Prisca
NT Apostolic

A tentmaker and teacher, married to Aquila, who partnered with Paul in ministry, hosted churches in their home, and together with her husband more accurately instructed the eloquent preacher Apollos.

For younger readers

Priscilla and her husband Aquila were tentmakers, the same trade as Paul, and the couple had to leave Rome when the emperor ordered all Jews to leave the city. They settled in Corinth, where Paul met them, worked alongside them, and stayed in their home. Priscilla and Aquila traveled with Paul afterward and became close partners in his ministry, hosting a church that met in their own house in more than one city they lived in. In one memorable story, the couple heard a gifted preacher named Apollos teaching accurately about Jesus but with some incomplete understanding, so they took him aside privately and explained the way of God to him more fully. Priscilla is mentioned by name several times in the New Testament, often listed before her husband, suggesting she was particularly notable in ministry and teaching alongside him.

Life Story

Priscilla and her husband Aquila, both tentmakers, met Paul in Corinth after the emperor Claudius had ordered all Jews to leave Rome; Paul, sharing their trade, stayed and worked with them (Acts 18:1-3). The couple accompanied Paul to Ephesus and remained there when he moved on, and it was there that they encountered Apollos, an eloquent and scripturally learned preacher who nonetheless knew "only the baptism of John" -- an incomplete understanding of the gospel; "they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately" (18:18-26), a striking picture of a husband-and-wife team correcting and completing the theology of a gifted public teacher, done privately and without public embarrassment. They hosted a church in their home, first apparently in Ephesus and later again in Rome once they had returned there (Romans 16:3-5; 1 Corinthians 16:19). Paul calls them "my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their own necks for my life," indicating some unrecorded moment of real danger they faced on his behalf (Romans 16:3-4). Notably, Priscilla is named before her husband in four of the six New Testament references to the couple, an unusual ordering by ancient convention that many readers take as a sign of her particular prominence or giftedness in teaching and ministry alongside Aquila (Acts 18:18, 26; Romans 16:3; 2 Timothy 4:19).
