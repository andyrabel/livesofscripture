Doeg (M)

OT United Monarchy

Doeg the Edomite, Saul's chief herdsman, happened to be at Nob when the priest Ahimelech, unaware David was fleeing Saul, gave him bread and Goliath's sword (1 Samuel 21:1-9; 22:9-10). When Saul later raged that no one had told him David was conspiring against him, Doeg volunteered what he had seen (22:6-10). Saul summoned Ahimelech and the priests of Nob, accusing them of treason; Ahimelech protested he had no idea David was a fugitive and had only helped him as he always had (22:11-15). Saul ordered his guards to kill the priests, but they refused to "put forth their hands against the priests of the LORD" (22:17). Doeg alone was willing: he personally killed eighty-five priests wearing the linen ephod, then struck down the entire town of Nob -- men, women, children, infants, and livestock (22:18-19). Only Abiathar, one of Ahimelech's sons, escaped to tell David, who confessed he had known Doeg was there and felt responsible for the deaths of Ahimelech's whole household (22:20-23). David later wrote Psalm 52 directly about Doeg, condemning his boastful, deceitful cruelty and declaring that God would bring him down -- one of Scripture's most direct psalms of judgment against a specific named individual.

Doeg was an Edomite who worked for King Saul in charge of his herds. While David was running from Saul, he stopped at the town of Nob and received help from the priest Ahimelech, not realizing that Doeg had witnessed it. Doeg reported what he saw to Saul, who was furious that a priest had helped David. Saul ordered his own guards to kill Ahimelech and the other priests of Nob, but they refused, since these were priests of God. Doeg, however, was willing to do it himself, killing eighty-five priests and many others in the town. David, learning what happened, felt responsible and grieved deeply for it, and wrote a psalm about Doeg's cruelty and betrayal.

References: 1 Samuel 21:7; 1 Samuel 22:9-22; Psalms 52:0
