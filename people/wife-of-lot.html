Lot's Wife

OT Patriarchal

Lot's unnamed wife, who fled Sodom with her family at the angels' warning but looked back toward the destroyed city in disobedience to their explicit instruction, and became a pillar of salt; later invoked by Jesus as a warning about attachment to what is being judged.

For younger readers

When God sent angels to rescue Lot's family from the city of Sodom before He destroyed it, the angels gave one clear instruction: don't look back. Lot, his wife, and their daughters fled together, but as they were escaping, Lot's wife looked back toward the city anyway -- and she immediately turned into a pillar of salt. Scripture doesn't tell us her name, but it does tell us why her story matters: Jesus Himself later used her as an example, telling His followers to 'remember Lot's wife' as a warning against holding on to the very things God is calling us to leave behind.

Life Story

Lot's wife is never named in Scripture, but her single act of disobedience is one of the most memorable warnings in Genesis. As the angels hurried Lot's family out of Sodom before its destruction, their instruction was explicit: 'Do not look behind you... escape to the mountains, or you will be swept away' (Genesis 19:17). Fleeing with her husband and daughters, she looked back -- and became a pillar of salt (19:26). Genesis does not explain her motive; interpreters have long read the look back as reflecting longing for the life, home, and comforts of Sodom she was leaving behind, rather than mere curiosity. Her fate stands in the text as an immediate, visible consequence for disregarding a direct command meant to save her. Centuries later, Jesus invoked her by name in His teaching about the suddenness of the coming judgment and the danger of clinging to worldly life when God calls His people to leave it: 'Remember Lot's wife' (Luke 17:32), placed alongside warnings not to turn back for possessions once the moment of decision has come. Her brief, tragic story -- a woman who was rescued yet still lost, through one backward glance of divided loyalty -- became Scripture's own shorthand for the danger of holding on to what God has already told His people to release.

First named in Genesis 19:15-26
