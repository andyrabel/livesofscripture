Samson (M)

First named in Judges 13:24

OT Judges

Samson was born to Manoah and his wife after an angel announced he would be a Nazirite from the womb, set apart to begin delivering Israel from the Philistines (Judges 13:24-25; cf. Hebrews 11:32). Yet Samson's story is a catalog of compromise as much as strength. He demanded a Philistine wife against his parents' objections, killed a lion with his bare hands, and used a riddle from the encounter to provoke a bloody dispute at his wedding feast that cost thirty Philistines their lives (14:1-19). He burned Philistine fields with foxes and torches, slaughtered a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone, and once carried off the gates of Gaza after visiting a prostitute there (15:1-16:3) -- feats of raw power paired with reckless, self-indulgent choices. His downfall came through Delilah, who wore him down until he told her the secret of his Nazirite vow; his hair was cut, his strength left him, and the Philistines gouged out his eyes and set him grinding grain in prison (16:4-21). But as his hair grew back, so did his dependence on God: blind and humiliated, Samson prayed for strength one last time and pulled down the temple of Dagon, killing more Philistines in his death than in his life (16:22-30). Judges' final verdict is sober, not celebratory -- flawed to the end, yet still counted among those who acted "by faith" (Hebrews 11:32).

Samson was set apart before he was even born to be a Nazirite, someone specially devoted to God, and God gave him amazing strength. But Samson often made poor choices, especially about who he spent time with, and he didn't take his special calling as seriously as he should have. Time after time, his choices got him into trouble with the Philistines, Israel's enemies, though God kept using him anyway to fight against them. Eventually, Samson trusted the wrong person with the secret of his strength, and he was captured and mistreated by his enemies. But in his very last moment, Samson turned back to God and prayed for strength one final time -- and God answered, giving Samson a last, powerful victory over Israel's enemies, even though it cost him his own life.

References: Judges 13:24; Judges 14:1-20; Judges 15:1-16; Judges 16:1-30; Hebrews 11:32
