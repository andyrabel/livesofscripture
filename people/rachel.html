Rachel (F)

Name means “Sheep” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 29:1-30

OT Patriarchal

Rachel, Laban's younger daughter, was the wife Jacob loved and worked fourteen years to marry, after being deceived into marrying her older sister Leah first (Genesis 29:16-30). While Leah bore son after son, Rachel remained barren, and her anguish is recorded starkly: 'Give me children, or else I die' (30:1) -- a cry Jacob answered with anger rather than comfort. Rachel gave her servant Bilhah to Jacob as a surrogate, as Sarah once had with Hagar, gaining sons Dan and Naphtali 'through' her (30:3-8). Eventually 'God remembered Rachel... and opened her womb,' and she bore Joseph, naming him with a prayer for yet another son (30:22-24). Fleeing her father's household years later, Rachel secretly stole her father's household idols and hid them, lying to Laban about it when he searched -- a episode Genesis records without excusing (31:19, 34-35). She died in labor with her second son on the road near Bethlehem, naming him with her last breath Ben-oni ('son of my sorrow'), though Jacob renamed him Benjamin ('son of the right hand'); Jacob buried her there and set up a pillar (35:16-20). Centuries later, Matthew 2:18 applies Jeremiah's image of 'Rachel weeping for her children' to Herod's slaughter of Bethlehem's infants, linking her grief to Christ's own perilous infancy.

Rachel was the wife Jacob loved most, and he worked fourteen years for her father Laban just to marry her. For a long time, Rachel could not have children while her sister Leah had several, and Rachel struggled deeply with jealousy and longing during those years. Eventually God remembered Rachel and she gave birth to Joseph, who would later become a great leader in Egypt. Sadly, Rachel died giving birth to her second son, Benjamin, on the road to Bethlehem, and Jacob buried her there. Rachel's story is an honest picture of how painful waiting and longing can be, and how God does not forget the people who feel forgotten.

Thought for Today

Rachel’s story teaches us about longing for children; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 29:1-30; Genesis 30:1-24; Genesis 31:19-35; Genesis 35:16-20; Matthew 2:18
