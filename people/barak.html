Barak (M)

Also called: Bedan

First named in Judges 4:6-24

OT Judges

Barak, from Kedesh in Naphtali, was summoned by the prophetess and judge Deborah with God's specific battle plan: gather ten thousand men from Naphtali and Zebulun at Mount Tabor, and God would draw out Sisera, commander of Canaan's oppressive army with its 900 iron chariots, to be defeated at the Kishon river (Judges 4:6-7). Barak's response reveals a real, if imperfect, faith: 'If you will go with me, then I will go; but if you will not go with me, I will not go' (4:8) -- he was willing to obey, but only with Deborah's presence assuring him of God's guidance. Deborah agreed to accompany him, though she told him the LORD would deliver Sisera 'into the hands of a woman,' a statement whose fulfillment came not through her but through Jael (4:9, 17-22). Barak did lead the assault that routed Sisera's chariots and army completely, pursuing the survivors himself, even though Sisera fled the scene before Barak could capture him personally (4:14-16, 22). Afterward Barak joined Deborah in the extended victory song of Judges 5, praising God's intervention. Despite his initial hesitation, Hebrews 11:32 lists Barak among the great heroes of faith, alongside Gideon, Samson, and David -- Scripture's own verdict that Barak's obedience, even when it required reassurance, still counted as genuine faith that accomplished God's purposes for Israel.

Barak was called by the prophetess Deborah to lead Israel's army against a powerful Canaanite general named Sisera, who had 900 iron chariots. Barak agreed to go, but only if Deborah came with him too. Deborah agreed, and told Barak that because of his request, the credit for defeating Sisera would go to a woman instead of to him. God gave Israel the victory just as He had promised, and Barak and Deborah celebrated together afterward with a song of praise. Even though Barak needed some encouragement to step out in faith, the New Testament later remembers him as one of the heroes of faith, showing that God can use even hesitant obedience for great purposes.

Thought for Today

Barak’s story teaches us about hesitant faith; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Judges 4:6-24; Judges 5:1-15; Hebrews 11:32
