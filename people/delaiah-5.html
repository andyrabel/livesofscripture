Delaiah (M)

Name means “The poor of the Lord” — Hebrew

First named in Jeremiah 36:12-25

OT Divided Monarchy

Delaiah, son of Shemaiah, was one of the officials serving in King Jehoiakim's court in Jerusalem. When Baruch, at Jeremiah's direction, read aloud the scroll of the prophet's warnings against Judah in the chamber of Elishama the scribe, Delaiah was among the officials present (Jeremiah 36:12). Struck by what they heard, the officials went to report the words to the king, first telling Baruch to hide, along with Jeremiah (36:16-19). When the scroll was brought before Jehoiakim and read to him, the king grew increasingly hostile, cutting each section off with a scribe's knife and throwing it into a brazier as it was read, until the whole scroll was consumed (36:21-23). Unlike the king and his attendants, who showed no fear or torn garments at what they heard, Delaiah joined Elnathan and Gemariah in specifically urging Jehoiakim not to burn the scroll -- but the king would not listen to them (36:24-25). Delaiah's brief appearance shows a minority voice of conscience within a corrupt court: he recognized the seriousness of God's word even as his king treated it with contempt, though his intervention could not stop the burning. God's response was to have Jeremiah dictate the scroll again, with additional words of judgment against Jehoiakim (36:27-32).

Delaiah worked as an official in the king's palace in Jerusalem. One day, Jeremiah's friend Baruch read aloud a scroll containing all of God's warnings that Jeremiah had spoken. Delaiah and the other officials were alarmed by what they heard and knew the king needed to know. When wicked King Jehoiakim began cutting the scroll apart and burning it in a fire piece by piece, Delaiah was one of three officials who spoke up and begged the king to stop. Sadly, the king would not listen and burned it anyway. Delaiah's story reminds us that speaking up for God's word, even when the powerful don't want to hear it, still matters.

Thought for Today

Delaiah was one of the few in Jehoiakim's court who begged the king not to burn God's word. May we treasure God's word about Jesus just as much.
