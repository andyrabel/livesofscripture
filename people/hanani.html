Hanani (M)

Name means “My grace; my mercy” — Hebrew
Prophesied to Judah, c. 880 BC–870 BC

First named in 1 Kings 16:1-7

OT Divided Monarchy

Hanani was a prophet -- called "the seer" -- during the reign of King Asa of Judah. Early in his reign Asa had torn down idols and relied on the LORD for a great victory over a massive Cushite army (2 Chronicles 14:9-15). But later, when King Baasha of Israel threatened Judah, Asa paid the king of Aram with silver and gold from the temple to break his treaty with Israel and attack it instead -- a political solution in place of prayer (16:1-6). Hanani confronted Asa directly: "Because you have relied on the king of Aram and have not relied on the LORD your God, therefore the army of the king of Aram has escaped out of your hand" (16:7), reminding him that God had delivered far larger armies when Asa trusted Him. Rather than repent, Asa was enraged, put Hanani in prison in the stocks, and "oppressed some of the people at the same time" (16:10) -- a king who had once walked faithfully now silencing the prophet who told him the truth. Hanani's son Jehu carried on his father's prophetic ministry, later confronting King Baasha of Israel (1 Kings 16:1-7) and King Jehoshaphat of Judah (2 Chronicles 19:2), with both men's records preserved in "the annals of Jehu the son of Hanani" (2 Chronicles 20:34). Hanani's brief, costly courage stands as a rebuke to trusting human power over God.

Hanani was a prophet who lived during the time of King Asa of Judah. Early in his reign, Asa had trusted God and won a great battle. But later, when a threat came from a northern kingdom, Asa paid a foreign king to help him instead of asking God for help like he used to. Hanani the prophet came to Asa and told him honestly that he had done wrong by trusting a human king instead of the LORD, who had helped him before. Instead of listening, King Asa became angry and had Hanani put in prison. Hanani's story is a reminder that speaking the truth for God is not always welcomed, but it still matters. Hanani's son Jehu also became a prophet after him.

Thought for Today

Hanani was imprisoned for telling King Asa that trusting an alliance instead of God had cost him God's help. Jesus is the truth we can trust, even unwelcome.

References: 1 Kings 16:1-7; 2 Chronicles 16:7; 2 Chronicles 19:2; 2 Chronicles 20:34
