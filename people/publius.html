Publius (M)

First named in Acts 28:7-10

NT Apostolic

Publius, described as 'the leading man of the island' of Malta, welcomed Paul and the roughly 276 passengers and crew who had survived a violent shipwreck on their voyage toward Rome, hosting them 'courteously' for three days (Acts 28:1, 7). During this time of hospitality, Paul learned that Publius's own father was confined to bed with fever and dysentery; Paul went in to him, prayed, and laid his hands on him, and he was healed (28:8). News of this healing spread across the island, and 'the rest of the people... who had diseases... were coming to him and getting cured' as well, turning Publius's private act of hospitality into an opportunity for widespread physical healing and, presumably, gospel witness across the whole community (28:9). In gratitude, the islanders honored Paul and his companions greatly, and when they finally set sail again three months later, the Maltese supplied them generously with everything needed for the journey (28:10-11). Luke's account gives no indication that Publius's original welcome was motivated by anything beyond ordinary hospitality toward shipwrecked strangers, yet that simple, costly kindness -- housing and feeding a large group of unexpected castaways for days -- became the occasion through which God worked significant healing across an entire island community, an example of ordinary generosity opening the door to remarkable blessing.

Publius was the leading official on the island of Malta when Paul and his companions were shipwrecked there during a violent storm. Publius generously welcomed them and entertained them courteously for three days. During that time, Paul learned that Publius's father was sick with fever and dysentery, so Paul went to him, prayed, laid his hands on him, and healed him. Word of this healing spread quickly, and many other sick people on the island came to Paul and were healed too. Because of this, Publius and the islanders honored Paul and his companions greatly and supplied them generously with everything they needed before they eventually continued their journey. Publius's hospitality opened the door to real blessing for his whole community.

Thought for Today

Publius's simple hospitality toward shipwrecked strangers opened the door for his own father's healing and many more besides. Jesus still works through the doors that ordinary kindness opens.
