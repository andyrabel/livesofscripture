Ahinoam (F)

Name means “Beauty of the brother; brother of motion” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Samuel 25:43

OT United Monarchy

Ahinoam of Jezreel became one of David's wives during his years as a fugitive from Saul, marrying him around the same time as Abigail (1 Samuel 25:43). She lived with David among the Philistines at Gath and later at Ziklag, the town Achish gave him (27:3). While David and his men were away, Amalekite raiders attacked Ziklag, burned it, and carried off everyone in it, including Ahinoam and Abigail (30:1-5). David, greatly distressed but strengthened by the LORD, pursued the raiders and recovered everyone taken captive -- "nothing was missing... David also recovered his two wives" (30:18-19). When David was anointed king over Judah at Hebron, Ahinoam went up with him (2 Samuel 2:2-3). She bore David his firstborn son, Amnon (3:2; 1 Chronicles 3:1) -- the same son whose later sin against his half-sister Tamar and subsequent murder by Absalom would tear David's family apart, though Scripture records nothing more about Ahinoam herself once her son is named. Her story is bound up entirely with David's turbulent rise to the throne and its costs.

Ahinoam was a woman from the town of Jezreel who became one of David's wives during the years before he became king. While David and his men were away from their home in Ziklag, raiders attacked the town, burned it down, and carried off everyone living there, including Ahinoam and David's other wife, Abigail. David and his men chased after the raiders and rescued everyone who had been taken, including his wives. Later, when David became king, Ahinoam went with him to live in Hebron. She became the mother of Amnon, David's very first son.

Thought for Today

Ahinoam’s story teaches us about David's wives; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 1 Samuel 25:43; 1 Samuel 27:3; 1 Samuel 30:5; 2 Samuel 2:2; 2 Samuel 3:2; 1 Chronicles 3:1
