Hananiah (M)

Name means “Grace; mercy; gift of the Lord” — Hebrew
Prophesied to Judah, c. 594 BC–593 BC

First named in Jeremiah 28:1-17

OT Divided Monarchy

Hananiah, son of Azzur, was a prophet from Gibeon who confronted Jeremiah publicly in the temple during the early reign of Zedekiah, while Babylon's power over Judah was mounting (Jeremiah 28:1). Jeremiah had been wearing a wooden yoke as a sign that Judah must submit to Babylonian rule for the time God had appointed. Hananiah declared instead, "in the name of the LORD," that within two years God would break Babylon's yoke and bring back the exiled king Jeconiah, the temple vessels, and all the captives (28:2-4). Jeremiah answered that he wished it were so, but that true prophets are known by whether their words come to pass (28:5-9). Hananiah then seized the wooden yoke off Jeremiah's neck and broke it in front of the people, repeating his false promise (28:10-11). The LORD then sent Jeremiah back with a sharper word: the broken wooden yoke would be replaced with an iron one -- Babylon's rule would be harder, not shorter -- and because Hananiah had made the people trust a lie, he would die within the year, "for you have counseled rebellion against the LORD" (28:12-16). Hananiah died two months later, in the seventh month of that same year (28:17), a sobering example of the weight Scripture places on those who speak falsely in God's name.

Hananiah was a man in Jerusalem who claimed to speak for God, but what he said was not true. At a time when Jeremiah the prophet was warning the people that hard times from Babylon were coming and would last a long while, Hananiah stood up in the temple and said the opposite -- that everything bad would be over in just two years. To make his point, he even broke a wooden yoke that Jeremiah had been wearing as a symbol of Babylon's coming rule. Jeremiah warned Hananiah that breaking a wooden yoke did not change what God had actually said, and that Hananiah would not live much longer because he had told the people a lie in God's name. Sadly, Hananiah died just two months later, showing how seriously God takes those who claim to speak for Him falsely.

Thought for Today

Hananiah spoke a comforting lie in the LORD's name instead of the harder truth Jeremiah gave. Jesus is the truth, and truth about Him alone sets us free.

References: Jeremiah 28:1-17; Jeremiah 36:12
