Shishak (M)

Name means “Present of the bag; of the pot; of the thigh” — Egyptian

First named in 1 Kings 3:1

OT Divided Monarchy

Shishak was a king of Egypt (widely identified by historians with Pharaoh Shoshenq I) who appears at two points in Israel's story. Early in Solomon's reign he gave asylum to Jeroboam son of Nebat after Jeroboam fled Solomon's anger, sheltering him in Egypt until Solomon's death (1 Kings 11:40). Years later, after the kingdom split and Rehoboam had reigned over Judah for five years, Shishak invaded with a massive force of chariots, cavalry, and troops drawn from Egypt, Libya, and Cush (2 Chronicles 12:2-3). Scripture is explicit about the cause: "because they had been unfaithful to the LORD" (2 Chronicles 12:2). Rehoboam and Judah's leaders humbled themselves when the prophet Shemaiah confronted them, and God relented from destroying them entirely, but still allowed Shishak to plunder the treasures of the temple and the royal palace, including the gold shields Solomon had made -- shields Rehoboam later replaced with bronze ones (1 Kings 14:25-28; 2 Chronicles 12:6-12). Shishak's invasion, recorded in Scripture and corroborated by an Egyptian temple inscription at Karnak listing conquered cities, stands as a vivid historical example of the covenant principle running through Israel's kings: unfaithfulness to the LORD brings real, national consequence, even under a Solomon-built kingdom's own heir.

Shishak was a powerful king of Egypt during the time of Solomon and his son Rehoboam. Early on, Shishak gave shelter to a man named Jeroboam, who was hiding from King Solomon. Years later, after Israel split into two kingdoms and Rehoboam turned away from following God's ways, Shishak marched his army into Judah. He captured Jerusalem's treasures, even taking the gold shields Solomon had made for the temple. The Bible tells us this happened because Rehoboam and Judah's leaders had abandoned the LORD's instruction. Shishak's invasion is a sober reminder that turning away from God has real consequences for a nation.

Thought for Today

Shishak invaded Judah because Rehoboam and the people had been unfaithful to the LORD. May we stay faithful to Jesus rather than learn obedience only through hard consequences.

References: 1 Kings 3:1; 1 Kings 9:16-24; 1 Kings 11:18-40; 1 Kings 14:25; 2 Chronicles 8:11; 2 Chronicles 12:2-9
