Hadad (M)

Name means “Joy; noise; clamor” — Aramaic

First named in 1 Kings 11:14-25

OT United Monarchy

Hadad was a child of the royal family of Edom when Joab, David's army commander, came to Edom to bury Israel's slain and "struck down every male in Edom" during a prolonged military campaign (1 Kings 11:15-16). Hadad escaped the slaughter, fleeing to Egypt with a group of his father's Edomite servants while still "a young boy" (11:17). Pharaoh received him with striking generosity -- giving him a house, an allowance of food, an assignment of land, and eventually his own sister-in-law Tahpenes in marriage. Their son Genubath was raised in Pharaoh's own household "among the sons of Pharaoh" (11:18-20). Only after David and Joab were both dead did Hadad ask Pharaoh to let him return home; despite Pharaoh's reluctance to lose him, Hadad insisted, and went back to Edom (11:21-22). There, Scripture says, he became one of the adversaries "the LORD raised up" against Solomon (11:14) -- part of a pointed pattern in 1 Kings 11, where God specifically stirs up outside threats (Hadad, Rezon, and eventually Jeroboam) as consequences for Solomon's unfaithfulness in marrying foreign wives and worshiping their gods late in his reign. Hadad's decades-long path from fugitive child to adversary illustrates how thoroughly Scripture treats even foreign political trouble as under God's sovereign hand, deployed as discipline for His own covenant king's disobedience.

Hadad was a young prince from the land of Edom. When he was just a boy, King David's army came into Edom and many men there were killed, but Hadad escaped and fled all the way to Egypt with some of his father's servants. Pharaoh welcomed him kindly, gave him a home, and even let him marry into his own family. Years later, after David and his general Joab had died, Hadad asked Pharaoh to let him go home to his own country. He returned to Edom and became a source of trouble for King Solomon later in his reign. The Bible tells us God allowed this trouble to rise up because Solomon had turned away from following Him fully.

Thought for Today

Hadad's decades-long path from fugitive child to Solomon's adversary shows even foreign kings serve God's sovereign plan. Nothing, not even sin, escapes what God accomplishes through Jesus.
