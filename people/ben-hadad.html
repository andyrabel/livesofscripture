Ben-hadad (M)

Name means “Son of Hadad (the Aramean storm-god)” — Aramaic

First named in 1 Kings 15:18-20

OT Divided Monarchy

Ben-hadad, son of Tabrimmon, ruled Aram from Damascus during Israel's divided-monarchy wars, appearing across the reigns of Asa, Baasha, and Ahab (many scholars think "Ben-hadad" names father and son bearing a shared dynastic title, not one long-reigning man -- see note). Judah's King Asa bribed him with temple treasure to break his alliance with Baasha and attack Israel instead (1 Kings 15:18-20; 2 Chronicles 16:2-7) -- a move the prophet Hanani condemned as trusting Aram instead of the LORD. Later, besieging Samaria with thirty-two allied kings, Ben-hadad arrogantly demanded Ahab's wives and children, then, boasting drunk in his tent, was routed by a small Israelite force; the next year he returned with a valley army, confident Israel's God only ruled hills, and was crushed again (1 Kings 20). Ahab spared him and made a covenant, drawing a prophet's rebuke for releasing a man devoted to destruction. Ben-hadad's officers later hunted Elisha for exposing his battle plans, only for God to blind the search party and for Elisha to feed and release them (2 Kings 6:8-23); he then besieged Samaria himself, starving the city (6:24). Finally, sick in Damascus, he sent his servant Hazael to ask Elisha whether he would recover -- not knowing Hazael would soon smother him and seize his throne (2 Kings 8:7-9).

Ben-hadad was a powerful king of Aram (Syria) who fought against Israel again and again during the time of Kings Asa and Ahab. At first, Judah's King Asa paid him to attack Israel's northern border. Later, Ben-hadad surrounded Israel's capital city with a huge army and demanded that King Ahab give up his treasures and his family -- but Ahab refused, and God gave Israel a surprising victory even though Ben-hadad's army was much bigger. Ahab showed him mercy and let him go free. Ben-hadad's soldiers also tried to capture the prophet Elisha, who kept warning Israel about Aram's war plans, but God protected Elisha instead. Ben-hadad's story shows that no army is too strong for God to overcome when He chooses to protect His people.

Thought for Today

Ben-hadad sent his own servant to ask Elisha about his fate, never expecting that servant to kill him. Jesus alone holds every person's future, unlike any human plan.

References: 1 Kings 15:18-20; 1 Kings 20:1-33; 1 Kings 22:3-31; 2 Kings 5:1-5; 2 Kings 6:8-24; 2 Kings 8:7-9; 2 Chronicles 16:2-7; 2 Chronicles 18:30
