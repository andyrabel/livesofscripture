Silas (M)

Also called: Silvanus

First named in Acts 15:22-40

NT Apostolic

Silas was a leading member and prophet in the Jerusalem church, chosen along with Judas Barsabbas to carry the Jerusalem council's decision about Gentile believers to the church in Antioch, where he "encouraged and strengthened the brethren with a lengthy message" (Acts 15:22, 32). When Paul and Barnabas split over John Mark, Paul chose Silas as his new companion for the second missionary journey (15:40). In Philippi, after Paul cast a demon of divination out of a slave girl, her furious owners had Paul and Silas dragged before the magistrates, severely beaten with rods, and thrown into the inner prison with their feet in stocks (16:16-24). At midnight, rather than despairing, Paul and Silas were "praying and singing hymns of praise to God" while the other prisoners listened, when a sudden earthquake shook the prison, opening every door and loosening every chain (16:25-26). Silas continued alongside Paul through Thessalonica, Berea, and eventually Corinth, where he rejoined Paul after a separate journey (17:1-15; 18:5). His steady, trusted partnership is reflected in the New Testament letters themselves: he is listed as a co-sender of both letters to the Thessalonians (1 Thessalonians 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:1), and Peter mentions writing "through Silvanus," likely the same man, as a faithful brother who helped deliver his first letter (1 Peter 5:12).

Silas was a respected leader and prophet in the early Jerusalem church, chosen to travel and deliver the council's important decision to the church in Antioch about how Gentile believers should be received. When Paul and Barnabas later split up over a disagreement about John Mark, Paul chose Silas as his new missionary partner, and the two of them traveled together through many cities preaching the gospel. In the city of Philippi, Paul and Silas were arrested, beaten severely, and thrown into prison for casting a demon out of a servant girl. Remarkably, instead of despairing, they sang hymns and prayed at midnight, and God sent an earthquake that broke open the prison doors. Silas remained one of Paul's most trusted and steady companions throughout much of his ministry, and his name appears as a co-sender in several of Paul's New Testament letters.

References: Acts 15:22-40; Acts 16:19-29; Acts 17:4-15; Acts 18:5; 2 Corinthians 1:19; 1 Thessalonians 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:1; 1 Peter 5:12
