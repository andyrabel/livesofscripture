Heldai (M)

Name means “Meaning uncertain” — Hebrew

First named in Zechariah 6:10-14

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Heldai was one of three men -- along with Tobijah and Jedaiah -- named as recently returned exiles from Babylon whose silver and gold the LORD instructed Zechariah to collect (Zechariah 6:10; the crown is later said to be a memorial "to Helem," likely an alternate form of Heldai's name, in verse 14). Zechariah was told to take the offering and make from it "an elaborate crown," then set it on the head of Joshua the son of Jehozadak, the high priest (6:11). The act was a prophetic sign: Zechariah was to declare over Joshua, "Behold, a man whose name is Branch... He will build the temple of the LORD and bear the honor and sit and rule on His throne. Thus, He will be a priest on His throne, and the counsel of peace will be between the two offices" (6:12-13) -- a picture of the coming Messiah uniting kingship and priesthood, offices Israel had always kept separate. The crown itself was to remain in the temple "as a reminder" to Heldai and his companions of the promise their gift had helped enact (6:14). Heldai performs no independent action beyond bringing his offering from exile, but Scripture ties his generosity directly to one of the Old Testament's clearest prophetic pictures of the Messiah's dual office.

Heldai was one of a small group of Jewish exiles who had come back from Babylon and brought silver and gold with them. The prophet Zechariah was told by God to take their gift and use it to make a beautiful crown. Then Zechariah placed the crown on the head of Joshua, the high priest -- not to make Joshua king, but as a picture pointing forward to someone God would send later, called "the Branch," who would be both priest and king and would build the LORD's temple. Heldai's gift became part of a real reminder kept in the temple of that promise. He isn't described doing anything else in the Bible, but his generosity became a small piece of a big prophecy about the coming Messiah.

Thought for Today

Heldai's silver helped crown the high priest Joshua as a picture of the Branch, the coming king-priest who would build God's temple. Jesus alone holds both offices forever.
