Lo-ruhamah (F)

First named in Hosea 1:6-8

OT Divided Monarchy

Lo-ruhamah was the second child born in the prophet Hosea's marriage to Gomer, a marriage that itself dramatized God's relationship with unfaithful Israel (Hosea 1:2-3). At her birth, "the LORD said to him, 'Name her Lo-ruhamah, for I will no longer have compassion on the house of Israel, that I would ever forgive them. But I will have compassion on the house of Judah and deliver them by the LORD their God'" (1:6-7). Her name -- meaning "not pitied" or "no compassion" -- made Israel's spiritual crisis unavoidably public: God was declaring that persistent idolatry had, for a time, exhausted the compassion Israel had presumed upon, even while Judah would still be shown mercy. Like her younger brother Lo-ammi ("not My people"), Lo-ruhamah's very existence and name were themselves the prophetic message, a sign-act lived out in a child rather than merely spoken. Yet Hosea does not leave the name as the last word: chapter 2 reverses it explicitly -- "I will have compassion on her who had not obtained compassion, and I will say to those who were not My people, 'You are My people!'" (2:23) -- and the apostle Peter later applies this same reversal to God's mercy shown in Christ toward those who once "had not received mercy, but now have received mercy" (1 Peter 2:10). Lo-ruhamah's hard name pointed toward a withheld mercy that God Himself promised would not last forever.

Lo-ruhamah was the daughter of the prophet Hosea and his wife Gomer. God told Hosea to give her a name that means "not pitied" or "no compassion." It was meant to be a message to the people of Israel: because they kept turning away from God to worship false gods, God was warning that He would no longer show them the compassion they had grown used to -- at least not for a while. At the very same time, God said He would still show compassion to the people of Judah. Even though Lo-ruhamah's name sounds sad, it wasn't the end of the story. Later, God promised that compassion would come again. Her name was a warning, not God's final answer.

References: Hosea 1:6-8
