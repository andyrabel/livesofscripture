Sennacherib (M)

Name means “Bramble of destruction” — Assyrian (Akkadian)

First named in 2 Kings 18:13-37

OT Divided Monarchy

Sennacherib, king of Assyria, invaded Judah in Hezekiah's fourteenth year, capturing all its fortified cities; Hezekiah initially tried to buy him off with a heavy tribute, stripping gold even from the temple doors, but Sennacherib besieged Jerusalem anyway (2 Kings 18:13-16). He sent his field commander Rabshakeh with a large force to demand the city's surrender, delivering a speech in Hebrew designed to terrify the population directly, mocking Hezekiah's trust in Egypt and, most seriously, equating the LORD with the defeated gods of other nations Assyria had already conquered -- 'who among all the gods of the lands have delivered their land from my hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem?' (18:19-35). Hezekiah brought the letter containing this threat directly into the temple and spread it before the LORD in prayer, and sought out the prophet Isaiah, who promised Sennacherib would fail and 'return to his own land' (19:1-7, 14-19). That night, 'the angel of the LORD went out and struck 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians,' and Sennacherib withdrew to Nineveh in disgrace (19:35-36). There, some time later, while worshiping in the temple of his god Nisroch, Sennacherib was assassinated by two of his own sons, who then fled, and another son, Esarhaddon, reigned in his place (19:37) -- an empire's arrogance against the living God ending in humiliation and betrayal within his own household.

Sennacherib was the powerful king of Assyria who conquered many of Judah's cities and surrounded Jerusalem itself during King Hezekiah's reign. Sennacherib's messenger mocked the LORD, comparing Him to the powerless gods of other nations that Assyria had already defeated. Hezekiah brought this terrifying threat to God in prayer and to the prophet Isaiah, and God answered decisively: in a single night, an angel struck down 185,000 men in the Assyrian camp, and Sennacherib withdrew back to Nineveh in disgrace. Later, while worshiping his own god, Sennacherib was murdered by two of his own sons. His story is a powerful demonstration that no empire's arrogance can stand against God, no matter how invincible it seems.

Thought for Today

Sennacherib’s story teaches us about arrogance against God; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Kings 18:13-37; 2 Kings 19:1-37; 2 Chronicles 32:1-23; Isaiah 36-37
