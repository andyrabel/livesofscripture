Ezra (M)

Name means “Help; court” — Hebrew

First named in Ezra 7:1-25

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Ezra was a priest and scribe "skilled in the Law of Moses," who led a second wave of exiles from Babylon back to Jerusalem under authority and resources granted by King Artaxerxes of Persia (Ezra 7:1-26). Rather than requesting a military escort for the dangerous journey, Ezra proclaimed a fast and prayed, later explaining, "I was ashamed to ask the king for soldiers... since we had told him, 'The hand of our God is for good on all who seek him'" (Ezra 8:21-23). On arriving, Ezra discovered that many Israelites, including priests and Levites, had intermarried with surrounding peoples in violation of God's covenant law; Ezra tore his clothes in grief and led the community in a difficult, dramatic public repentance that included sending away the foreign wives (Ezra 9-10). Years later, alongside Nehemiah, Ezra stood on a wooden platform before the assembled people at Jerusalem's Water Gate and read the Law aloud from morning until midday while Levites moved through the crowd explaining its meaning, sparking both weeping and celebration as the people understood God's word freshly (Nehemiah 8:1-12). Later Jewish tradition, though not the biblical text itself, credits Ezra with a far larger role in preserving and shaping the Hebrew Scriptures.

Ezra was a priest who knew God's Law very well, and he led a group of Israelites on a long journey back home to Jerusalem from Babylon. Instead of asking for soldiers to protect them on the dangerous trip, Ezra and the people fasted and prayed, trusting God to protect them instead — and God did! When Ezra arrived, he found that some people had drifted from following God's ways, and he led the community back toward faithfully keeping their promises to God. Later, Ezra stood before a huge crowd and read God's Law out loud so everyone could understand it clearly, which made people both sorry for their mistakes and joyful about knowing God better. Ezra's story shows the power of knowing God's word well and helping others understand and love it too.

Thought for Today

Ezra’s story teaches us about scripture; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Ezra 7:1-25; Ezra 10:1-16; Nehemiah 8:1-13; Nehemiah 12:1-36
