Azariah (M)

First named in Jeremiah 43:2

OT Exile

After the Babylonian-appointed governor Gedaliah was assassinated, the surviving leaders of Judah -- fearing Babylonian retaliation for the murder -- came to Jeremiah and asked him to seek the LORD's guidance about whether to flee to Egypt, solemnly vowing, "whether it is pleasant or unpleasant, we will listen to the voice of the LORD our God" (Jeremiah 42:1-6). Ten days later, Jeremiah delivered God's answer: remain in the land, and the LORD would build them up and show them mercy even under Babylon's hand; go to Egypt instead, and the very sword and famine they feared would overtake them there (42:7-22). Azariah, son of Hoshaiah, was among those who rejected this word outright. Together with Johanan son of Kareah and "all the arrogant men," he accused Jeremiah to his face: "You are telling a lie! The LORD our God has not sent you... Baruch the son of Neriah is inciting you against us" (43:2-3). Despite their earlier vow to obey, Azariah and the others refused to stay in Judah. They forced the entire remnant -- including Jeremiah and his scribe Baruch, taken against their will -- down into Egypt, settling at Tahpanhes (43:4-7). Azariah's story is a study in the gap between asking for God's word and actually submitting to it: he sought a prophecy, received a clear answer, and then called the messenger a liar rather than obey it.

After Jerusalem fell and its governor Gedaliah was murdered, the Judeans who were left were afraid of what the Babylonians might do to them. They asked the prophet Jeremiah to pray and find out what God wanted them to do, and they promised to obey, whatever the answer was. Jeremiah told them that God wanted them to stay in the land, and promised to protect and bless them if they did. But a man named Azariah didn't believe him. He accused Jeremiah of lying and said Jeremiah's friend Baruch had convinced him to say it. Azariah and the other leaders refused to obey God's word, and they forced the whole remaining group -- including Jeremiah -- to go down to Egypt instead. It's a sad reminder that hearing God's word isn't the same as obeying it.

Thought for Today

Azariah asked Jeremiah to seek God's word, then rejected the answer and called it a lie. Jesus deserves the same trust we owe every true word God speaks.
