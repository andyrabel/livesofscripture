Mephibosheth (M)

Name means “Out of my mouth proceeds reproach” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Samuel 21:8

OT United Monarchy

This Mephibosheth was one of two sons born to Saul and his concubine Rizpah, daughter of Aiah (2 Samuel 21:8) -- to be distinguished from the far better-known Mephibosheth, son of Jonathan, whom David showed kindness for his father's sake (2 Samuel 9). The context of his death is grim: a three-year famine struck Israel during David's reign, and when David inquired of the LORD, he learned it was because of Saul, 'because he put the Gibeonites to death' (21:1) -- violating an ancient oath Israel's leaders had sworn to spare the Gibeonites (Joshua 9:15). Seeking to make atonement and end the famine, David asked the Gibeonites what would satisfy them; they requested seven male descendants of Saul to be handed over and executed before the LORD at Gibeah, Saul's hometown (21:6). David spared Mephibosheth, son of Jonathan, for the sake of his oath to Jonathan, but handed over the two sons of Rizpah -- Armoni and this Mephibosheth -- along with five sons of Saul's daughter Merab (21:8-9). The seven were killed and their bodies left exposed; Rizpah's subsequent vigil guarding their remains from birds and animals moved David to finally give Saul's house a proper burial (21:10-14). The episode is one of Scripture's starker illustrations that unrepented sin, even by a king long dead, could bring consequence upon a nation and a family.

This Mephibosheth was a son of King Saul, born to Saul's concubine Rizpah -- a different person from the more famous Mephibosheth who was Jonathan's son and David's close friend. Years earlier, Saul had broken an old promise Israel made to protect the Gibeonite people, and this brought a severe famine on the land. When David asked God how to make things right, the Gibeonites asked for seven of Saul's male descendants to be handed over to them. This Mephibosheth was one of the seven. It's a hard and sad part of the Bible, showing how seriously God takes broken promises and how sin can have consequences that reach into the next generation.

Thought for Today

Israel's famine ended only when blood atoned for Saul's broken oath, including this Mephibosheth's life. Jesus's blood atoned fully, so no more sacrifice is ever needed.
