Purah (M)

Name means “Meaning uncertain, possibly "branch" or "foliage"” — Hebrew

First named in Judges 7:10-11

OT Judges

Purah was Gideon's personal servant, present at one of the more understated but pivotal moments in the story of Gideon's victory over Midian. After God had already reduced Gideon's army from thousands down to just three hundred men, He told Gideon, 'if you are afraid to attack, go down to the camp with Purah your servant' to hear what the enemy was saying, promising that this would strengthen Gideon's resolve (Judges 7:9-11). Gideon took Purah and crept to the outskirts of the vast Midianite camp, arriving just as one soldier was telling another about a dream: a loaf of barley bread tumbling into the camp and striking a tent so hard it collapsed. The second soldier interpreted it as nothing less than 'the sword of Gideon,' by which God would hand Midian over to Israel (7:12-14). Hearing the enemy's own fear spoken aloud, Gideon worshiped God on the spot, returned to camp, and immediately rallied his three hundred men for the attack that routed Midian (7:15-25). Purah himself says and does nothing beyond accompanying Gideon on this reconnaissance, but his quiet presence at Gideon's side, exactly when God had promised reassurance would come, is part of how God turned a fearful commander into a confident one.

Purah was the servant of Gideon, the judge God chose to rescue Israel from the Midianites. The night before a huge battle, God told Gideon that if he was still afraid, he could sneak down to the enemy camp with Purah to listen in. So Gideon and Purah crept down together and overheard a Midianite soldier describing a strange dream to his friend -- a loaf of barley bread tumbling into the camp and knocking down a tent. His friend said the dream meant God was about to give Gideon victory. Hearing this, Gideon's fear turned to worship and confidence, and he went on to lead his small army to a great victory.

Thought for Today

Purah's quiet presence at Gideon's side came exactly when God had promised to turn fear into confidence. Jesus meets us in our fear too, turning it into faith.
