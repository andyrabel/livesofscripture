Jehoiada (M)

Name means “Knowledge of the Lord” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Samuel 8:18

OT United Monarchy

Jehoiada was a leader of the house of Aaron -- a senior figure among the priestly clan of Levi -- who joined David's growing forces at Hebron with 3,700 men when Saul's kingdom was passing to David, part of a much larger gathering of tribal leaders who came "to turn the kingdom of Saul to him, according to the word of the LORD" (1 Chronicles 12:23-27). Scripture says little else about Jehoiada directly; nearly everything else recorded under his name concerns his son, Benaiah, one of David's most celebrated warriors. Benaiah struck down two of Moab's finest fighters, killed a lion in a pit on a day of heavy snow, and killed a spear-wielding Egyptian giant armed with only a staff, feats that placed him among David's inner circle of mighty men (2 Samuel 23:20-22; 1 Chronicles 11:22-24). David made Benaiah commander of his personal bodyguard, the Cherethites and Pelethites (2 Samuel 8:18; 20:23; 1 Chronicles 18:17), and at the end of David's reign, Benaiah backed Solomon over the coup attempted by Adonijah, then personally executed Adonijah, Joab, and Shimei on Solomon's orders (1 Kings 1:8-44; 2:25-46). Solomon rewarded that loyalty by making Benaiah commander of the entire army (1 Kings 4:4; 1 Chronicles 27:5-6) -- a rise Jehoiada, whoever he was personally, never lived to see, but that his own early, decisive loyalty to David's cause helped make possible.

Jehoiada was a leader of the priestly family of Aaron who brought thousands of men to support David when God was making him king. The Bible doesn't tell us much more about Jehoiada himself, but it tells us a lot about his son, Benaiah, who became one of David's bravest soldiers. Benaiah once killed a lion in a snowy pit and defeated a huge Egyptian warrior armed only with a stick! David trusted Benaiah so much that he put him in charge of his royal bodyguard, and later King Solomon made him commander of the whole army.

Thought for Today

Jehoiada is barely mentioned himself, remembered mostly for showing up early with 3,700 men to support David's kingship at Hebron. Jesus doesn't need us to be famous to use our loyalty; a decision made in the background can still shape everything that follows, the way Jehoiada's did for his son Benaiah.

References: 2 Samuel 8:18; 2 Samuel 20:23; 2 Samuel 23:20-22; 1 Kings 1:8-44; 1 Kings 2:25-46; 1 Kings 4:4; 1 Chronicles 11:22-24; 1 Chronicles 12:27; 1 Chronicles 18:17; 1 Chronicles 27:6-34
