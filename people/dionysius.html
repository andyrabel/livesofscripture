Dionysius (M)

Also called: Dionysius the Areopagite

First named in Acts 17:34

NT Apostolic

Dionysius was a member of the Areopagus, the ancient council of Athens that met on Mars Hill to weigh new ideas and philosophies entering the city (Acts 17:19-21). When Paul stood before the Areopagus and reasoned from the Athenians' own altar "to an unknown god" to proclaim the one true Creator, who commands all people everywhere to repent because He has fixed a day of judgment through Jesus, whom He raised from the dead, the response split (17:22-31). Some mocked at the mention of resurrection, others deferred, saying they would hear him again -- but Luke records that "some men joined him and believed, among whom also were Dionysius the Areopagite and a woman named Damaris and others with them" (17:32-34). That Dionysius held a position on Athens's most prestigious council makes his conversion notable: a man of intellectual and civic standing in a famously skeptical, philosophy-saturated city chose to believe a message about a crucified and risen Jew whom most of his peers dismissed. Scripture says nothing further about him; later church tradition claims he became bishop of Athens, but this is tradition, not a stated biblical fact.

Dionysius was an important man in the city of Athens -- he belonged to a respected council of leaders called the Areopagus, who met on a hill to discuss ideas and judge important matters. When the apostle Paul stood before this council and explained who the true God is and how He raised Jesus from the dead, most of the crowd laughed or brushed him off. But Dionysius was different: he believed what Paul said and became a follower of Jesus, along with a woman named Damaris and some others. Dionysius's story shows that even in a place full of doubters, God can open the heart of a respected leader to trust in Christ.

Thought for Today

Dionysius believed in the risen Jesus even though most of Athens's philosophers mocked the idea. God can win over even the most respected skeptic.
