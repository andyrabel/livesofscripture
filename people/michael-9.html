Michael

OT Exile

An archangel named as 'one of the chief princes' who contends against the demonic 'prince of Persia' on behalf of Daniel and Israel, is identified as 'the great prince who stands guard over the sons of your people,' disputes with the devil over the body of Moses, and leads God's angels in war against the dragon in Revelation's vision of final judgment.

For younger readers

Michael is described in the Bible as a powerful angel, sometimes called an archangel, who fights on behalf of God's people. In the book of Daniel, an angel tells Daniel that Michael helped him against spiritual opposition while delivering an answer to Daniel's prayer. Michael is also called Israel's guardian, the great prince who watches over God's people. In the book of Jude, we learn that Michael argued with the devil over the body of Moses, but instead of insulting the devil himself, he simply said, 'The Lord rebuke you.' In Revelation, John sees a vision of Michael and his angels fighting against a great dragon (representing Satan) and winning. Michael's story reminds us that God has real spiritual power fighting for His people, even in ways we cannot see.

Life Story

Michael appears in Scripture as an angel of unusual rank and authority. In Daniel 10, an unnamed angelic messenger tells Daniel that he was delayed twenty-one days by 'the prince of the kingdom of Persia' -- an apparent demonic power opposing God's purposes -- until 'Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help me' (10:13). The same messenger later calls Michael 'your prince,' identifying him as Israel's particular angelic guardian, and states that 'there is no one who stands firmly with me against these forces except Michael your prince' (10:21). In Daniel 12:1, Michael is called 'the great prince who stands guard over the sons of your people,' with a promise that he will 'arise' at the time of the end, a period of unprecedented distress from which God's people will be delivered. Jude 1:9 records a brief but striking episode: 'Michael the archangel, when he disputed with the devil and argued about the body of Moses, did not dare pronounce a railing judgment against him, but said, "The Lord rebuke you!"' -- an incident Jude alludes to without full explanation, apparently drawn from a tradition surrounding Moses's death and burial (Deuteronomy 34:5-6) not otherwise detailed in the Old Testament text. In Revelation 12:7-9, Michael leads God's angels in war against 'the dragon,' identified as 'the serpent of old who is called the devil and Satan,' defeating him and casting him down. Across these four texts, Michael consistently appears as a chief angelic warrior specifically associated with defending God's people against hostile spiritual powers.

First named in Daniel 10:13-21

References: Daniel 10:13-21; Daniel 12:1; Jude 1:9; Revelation 12:7
