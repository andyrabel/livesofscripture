Stephen (M)

Name means “Same as Stephanas” — Greek

First named in Acts 6:5-9

NT Apostolic

Stephen was one of seven men chosen by the early Jerusalem church to oversee the daily distribution of food to widows, freeing the apostles for prayer and teaching; he is described as "a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit," who performed "great wonders and signs among the people" (Acts 6:1-8). Members of a synagogue opposed him in debate but "were unable to cope with the wisdom and the Spirit with which he was speaking," so they instigated false witnesses to accuse him of blasphemy against Moses and the temple before the Sanhedrin (6:9-14). Facing the council, whose members saw his face like an angel's, Stephen delivered a sweeping speech tracing Israel's history from Abraham through Moses and the prophets, arguing Israel had repeatedly resisted God's messengers and now, in rejecting Jesus, had resisted the Holy Spirit just as their ancestors persecuted the prophets (7:1-53). Enraged, the council rushed at him; Stephen, full of the Spirit, looked into heaven and declared he saw "the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God" (7:54-56). They dragged him outside the city and stoned him while a young man named Saul approved and guarded the stone-throwers' coats (7:57-58; 8:1). As he died, Stephen prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit," then cried, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" -- echoing Jesus's words from the cross (7:59-60). His martyrdom, the first in church history, triggered persecution that scattered believers and spread the gospel further (8:1-4).

Stephen was one of seven men chosen by the early church to make sure widows and others in need were fairly cared for, freeing the apostles to focus on teaching and prayer. He was full of faith and the Holy Spirit, and performed great signs and wonders. Some religious leaders couldn't out-argue his wisdom, so they arranged false accusations and brought him before the ruling council. Stephen answered with a bold speech tracing Israel's history, accusing the council of resisting the Holy Spirit just as their ancestors had. Furious, the crowd dragged him out of the city and stoned him to death. As he died, Stephen prayed for God to receive his spirit and, remarkably, asked God to forgive the people killing him, following Jesus's own example. Stephen became the first Christian martyr, and his death is tied to the start of Paul's story, since Saul approved of the killing.

Thought for Today

Stephen’s story teaches us about martyrdom; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Acts 6:5-9; Acts 7:59; Acts 8:2; Acts 11:19; Acts 22:20
