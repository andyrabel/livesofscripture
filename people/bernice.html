Bernice (F)

First named in Acts 25:13-23

NT Apostolic

Bernice, sister of King Herod Agrippa II, accompanied him to Caesarea to greet the newly arrived governor Festus, and the two entered the audience hall "with great pomp," a phrase capturing the deliberate royal spectacle of their appearance before the assembled military commanders and prominent men of the city (Acts 25:13, 23). She was present throughout Paul's extended personal testimony to Agrippa, hearing him describe his former life persecuting Christians, his encounter with the risen Jesus on the Damascus road, and his conviction that the Messiah's suffering and resurrection fulfilled what "the Prophets and Moses said was going to take place" (26:1-23). Scripture records no individual words or response from Bernice during the hearing itself; she appears as part of the royal party rather than as an independent participant in the exchange between Paul, Agrippa, and Festus. After the hearing concluded, she left with Agrippa and Festus to confer privately, where the group agreed Paul had done nothing deserving death, and could have been released had he not already appealed to Caesar (26:30-32). Extra-biblical historical sources identify Bernice as a woman of considerable political influence in her own right during this period, making her presence at this hearing, even without recorded words, a mark of how seriously the Roman and Jewish political establishment engaged with Paul's case and, by extension, with the message he carried.

Bernice was the sister of King Agrippa II, and she traveled with him to Caesarea to visit the Roman governor Festus. When Agrippa asked to hear Paul's case for himself, Bernice was present too, and the two of them arrived "with great pomp," a grand, ceremonial entrance emphasizing their royal status. Bernice sat and listened as Paul explained his faith in Jesus and his personal encounter with the risen Christ. Scripture doesn't record any specific words or reaction from Bernice herself during the scene, but she was there as a direct witness to one of Paul's most detailed and personal defenses of the gospel, delivered before some of the most powerful political figures in the region.

Thought for Today

Bernice sat through Paul's account of meeting the risen Jesus, yet Scripture records no response from her. May we not let Jesus pass us by unanswered.
