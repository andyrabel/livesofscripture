Shebna (M)

Also called: Shebnah

First named in 2 Kings 18:18-37

OT Divided Monarchy

Shebna held the powerful position 'over the household' in Hezekiah's court in Jerusalem -- essentially the palace's chief steward -- when Isaiah confronted him directly with God's specific displeasure: Shebna had carved himself a lavish tomb high in the rock and gloried in his splendid chariots, using a position meant for humble service to build a monument to himself (Isaiah 22:15-18). God's judgment through Isaiah was pointed: 'the LORD is about to hurl you headlong, O man... I will depose you from your office, and I will pull you down from your station' (22:17-19), and the very next verses describe God raising up Eliakim to take his place instead, given the honor of being 'a peg in a firm place' for his household in exactly the way Shebna had sought for himself through self-glorification (22:20-23). By the time of Sennacherib's siege of Jerusalem some years later, Shebna does still appear among Hezekiah's officials, but now holding the lesser title of secretary rather than steward of the household, standing alongside Eliakim (now in the position Shebna once held) and Joah the recorder to meet the Assyrian field commander Rabshakeh at the city wall (2 Kings 18:18, 26, 37). Shebna's demotion, while not removing him from service entirely, stands in Scripture as a clear warning that self-serving pride, even in high office, invites God's direct correction rather than lasting honor.

Shebna held a high position over King Hezekiah's household in Jerusalem, but he had used his position to carve himself a grand, showy tomb and to take pride in fancy chariots, more concerned with his own honor than with serving faithfully. The prophet Isaiah delivered a hard message from God: Shebna would be removed from his high position and replaced by Eliakim. By the time the Assyrian army under Sennacherib surrounded Jerusalem, Shebna appears with a lesser title, secretary, still serving but no longer in his former place of highest honor -- a vivid lesson about how God opposes proud self-glorification, even in someone still useful in a lesser role.

Thought for Today

Shebna’s story warns us about pride and self-monument; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Kings 18:18-37; 2 Kings 19:2; Isaiah 22:15-19; Isaiah 36:3-22; Isaiah 37:2
