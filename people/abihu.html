Abihu (M)

First named in Exodus 6:23

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Abihu, Aaron's second son, was set apart along with his father and three brothers as one of Israel's first ordained priests at Sinai, and was among the seventy-four elders and leaders privileged to go up the mountain and 'see the God of Israel' before the covenant meal (Exodus 24:1, 9-11; 28:1). This high honor makes what followed at the tabernacle's dedication especially severe: Abihu and his brother Nadab each took his own censer, put fire and incense in it, and 'offered strange fire before the LORD, which He had not commanded them' (Leviticus 10:1). Fire came out from the presence of the LORD and consumed them, and they died there before the LORD (10:2). Moses explained the judgment to a stunned Aaron by quoting God's own words: 'By those who come near Me I will be treated as holy, and before all the people I will be honored' (10:3) -- the newly consecrated priesthood was held to the highest standard precisely because of its nearness to God's presence. Aaron, forbidden even to mourn openly for his sons in the moment given the priesthood's ongoing duties, remained silent (10:3, 6-7). Abihu died without children, and the priesthood passed instead through his younger brothers Eleazar and Ithamar (Numbers 3:2-4; 26:60-61). His death remains one of Scripture's starkest warnings that access to worship God is a gift with real boundaries, not a license for self-styled devotion.

Abihu was Aaron's second son, and he was one of the very first priests set apart to serve God at the newly built tabernacle. This was an incredible honor and responsibility. But Abihu and his brother Nadab offered incense to God in a way God had not commanded, using what the Bible calls 'strange fire.' Fire came out from the LORD's presence and both brothers died instantly. It's a hard and sobering story, and it teaches that God takes worship seriously -- He wants to be approached in the way He asks, not however people decide on their own, especially for those given the responsibility of leading others in worship.

Thought for Today

Abihu’s story teaches us about unauthorized worship; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Exodus 6:23; Exodus 24:1-9; Exodus 28:1; Leviticus 10:1-3; Numbers 3:2-4; Numbers 26:60-61
