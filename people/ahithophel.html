Ahithophel (M)

First named in 2 Samuel 15:12-34

OT United Monarchy

Ahithophel served as David's counselor with a reputation so trusted that "the advice of Ahithophel, which he gave in those days, was as if one inquired of the word of God" (2 Samuel 16:23). When Absalom rebelled against his father, Ahithophel defected to him, and Absalom actively sought his counsel (15:12; 16:20). Ahithophel first advised Absalom to publicly take his father's concubines, humiliating David before all Israel and burning any bridge back (16:21-22) -- possibly connected to the fact that Bathsheba, David's wife whom he had taken adulterously, was Ahithophel's own granddaughter (11:3; 23:34), a link many readers see as motive for his betrayal. He then urged Absalom to let him pursue David immediately with an army before David could regroup, sound military advice that would likely have ended the rebellion in David's defeat (17:1-4). But David had prayed specifically that God would "make the counsel of Ahithophel foolish" (15:31), and Absalom, at God's providence, chose Hushai's contrary and slower advice instead (17:14). Seeing his counsel rejected and reading the rebellion's coming failure, Ahithophel went home, set his house in order, and hanged himself, and was buried in his father's tomb (17:23). His story is Scripture's clearest portrait of brilliant human wisdom that, turned against God's anointed king, leads only to despair.

Ahithophel was one of King David's most trusted advisors, so wise that people said asking his advice was almost like asking God directly. But when David's son Absalom rebelled and tried to take over the kingdom, Ahithophel switched sides and joined him, giving him advice on how to defeat David. David prayed that God would turn Ahithophel's wise advice into foolishness, and that's exactly what happened -- Absalom chose to follow different, worse advice instead of Ahithophel's plan. When Ahithophel realized his counsel had been rejected and that Absalom's rebellion would fail, he went home, put his affairs in order, and took his own life. His story is a sober reminder of where betrayal and wounded pride can lead.

Thought for Today

Ahithophel’s story warns us about betrayal; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Samuel 15:12-34; 2 Samuel 16:15-23; 2 Samuel 17:1-23; 2 Samuel 23:34; 1 Chronicles 27:33-34
