Uzziah (M)

Also called: Azariah

First named in 2 Kings 15:1-7

OT Divided Monarchy

Uzziah (also called Azariah) became king of Judah at sixteen and reigned fifty-two years, longer than almost any other king of Judah or Israel (2 Kings 15:1-2; 2 Chronicles 26:1-3). 'He did right in the sight of the LORD' during the years he sought guidance from Zechariah, a man instructed in the fear of God, and God gave him success: victories over the Philistines, Arabs, and Ammonites, strengthened fortifications in Jerusalem, an effective and well-equipped army, and innovative agricultural development, since Uzziah 'loved the soil' (2 Chronicles 26:4-15). But the text turns immediately: 'when he was strong, his heart was so proud that he acted corruptly, and he was unfaithful to the LORD his God' (26:16). Uzziah entered the temple himself to burn incense on the altar, a priestly duty reserved exclusively for Aaron's descendants; when the high priest Azariah and eighty other priests confronted him, Uzziah grew angry at being corrected rather than repenting (26:16-19). At that moment, leprosy broke out on his forehead in full view of the priests, and he was rushed out of the temple, recognizing God's judgment even as it struck (26:19-20). Uzziah lived in isolation with leprosy for the rest of his life, while his son Jotham governed the palace and the people in his place (26:21). Isaiah's famous temple vision of the LORD 'high and lifted up' is dated precisely to 'the year King Uzziah died' (Isaiah 6:1).

Uzziah became king of Judah at just sixteen years old and reigned for an impressively long fifty-two years. For much of his reign, Uzziah sought God, and God blessed him with military victories, strong cities, and a thriving economy. But as Uzziah grew powerful, his heart grew proud. One day, Uzziah entered the temple and tried to burn incense himself, a sacred task only the priests were allowed to do. When the priests confronted him, Uzziah grew angry, and at that very moment leprosy broke out on his forehead. He lived the rest of his life isolated because of the disease, with his son Jotham running the kingdom in his place. Uzziah's story is a strong warning that success can tempt even good leaders toward pride.

Thought for Today

Uzziah’s story teaches us about early faithfulness; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Kings 15:1-7; 2 Chronicles 26:1-23; Isaiah 6:1
