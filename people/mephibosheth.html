Mephibosheth (M)

Also called: Merib-baal

First named in 2 Samuel 4:4

OT United Monarchy

Mephibosheth was the son of Jonathan and grandson of King Saul. When he was five years old, news arrived that Saul and Jonathan had been killed in battle; in the panic, his nurse fled with him and dropped him, permanently crippling both his feet (2 Samuel 4:4). He grew up in hiding at Lo-debar, likely fearing for his life as a surviving member of the previous king's line. Years later, King David, remembering his covenant of loyal love with Jonathan, asked whether anyone remained of Saul's household to whom he could "show the kindness of God" (2 Samuel 9:1-3). David restored to Mephibosheth all of Saul's land, assigned Ziba and his household to farm it for him, and gave Mephibosheth a permanent place eating at the king's own table "like one of the king's sons" (9:7-11) -- a stunning reversal for a man who called himself "a dead dog" (9:8). During Absalom's later revolt, Ziba told David that Mephibosheth had stayed behind hoping to reclaim his grandfather's throne, and David gave Ziba all of Mephibosheth's property; when David returned victorious, Mephibosheth insisted Ziba had lied and left him behind despite his loyalty, and the text does not clearly settle who told the truth (16:1-4; 19:24-30). David split the land between them, and Mephibosheth's response -- that David's safe return mattered more to him than any property -- closes his story on a note of loyalty to David's house.

Mephibosheth was the son of Jonathan, King Saul's son, and Jonathan had been David's closest friend. When Mephibosheth was just five years old, he was hurt in an accident that left him unable to walk well for the rest of his life. Because he was part of the old king's family, Mephibosheth grew up hiding, afraid of what a new king might do to him. But David remembered his promise to always be kind to Jonathan's family. David found Mephibosheth and, instead of harming him, gave him back his grandfather's land and invited him to eat at the king's own table every day, just like one of David's own sons. Mephibosheth's story shows how keeping a promise can turn someone's fear into a place of honor and belonging.

References: 2 Samuel 4:4; 2 Samuel 9:6-13; 2 Samuel 16:1-4; 2 Samuel 19:24-30; 2 Samuel 21:7; 1 Chronicles 8:34; 1 Chronicles 9:40
