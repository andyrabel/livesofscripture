Jeshua (M)

Name means “The LORD saves; a deliverer (a form of Joshua)” — Hebrew

First named in Nehemiah 8:7

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

This Jeshua, son of Kadmiel, was one of the Levites active in Jerusalem during Ezra and Nehemiah's post-exilic reforms. When Ezra assembled the returned community and read publicly from the Law of Moses, Jeshua was named among thirteen Levites -- including Bani, Sherebiah, and others -- who "helped the people to understand the Law while the people remained in their place," reading from the book and "translating to give the sense so that they understood the reading" (Nehemiah 8:7-8). This was substantive teaching work, not mere ceremony: making sure a generation partly unfamiliar with the covenant actually grasped what was being read to them. Soon after, on a day set apart for fasting and confession, Jeshua appears again among the Levites who stood on the stairs and "cried with a loud voice to the LORD their God," leading the assembly in worship with the words, "Stand up, bless the LORD your God forever and ever!" before leading them into a long prayer of confession recounting Israel's history of rebellion and God's persistent faithfulness (Nehemiah 9:4-5). Jeshua's role across both scenes is the same: helping God's people actually understand and respond to His Word, at a pivotal moment when the returned exiles were recommitting themselves to the covenant their ancestors had broken.

After the Jewish exiles returned from Babylon and rebuilt Jerusalem's walls, the priest Ezra gathered everyone together to read God's Law out loud. It was not enough just to hear the words -- so Jeshua and a group of other Levites moved through the crowd, explaining what the reading meant so the people could really understand it. Not long after, the people gathered again to confess how far they and their ancestors had strayed from God. Jeshua and other Levites stood up and led the people in worship, calling out, "Stand up and bless the LORD your God forever and ever!" It was a fresh start built on really understanding God's Word.

Thought for Today

Jeshua helped the returned exiles actually understand God's Law being read to them. Jesus still opens our minds so Scripture makes sense and changes how we live.

References: Nehemiah 8:7; Nehemiah 9:4-5; Nehemiah 12:10-24
