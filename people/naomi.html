Naomi (F)

Also called: Mara
Name means “Beautiful; agreeable” — Hebrew

First named in Ruth 1

OT Judges

Naomi left Bethlehem with her husband Elimelech and two sons during a famine and settled in Moab, where her sons married Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. Within about ten years, Elimelech and both sons died, leaving Naomi without husband, sons, or security (Ruth 1:1-5). Hearing the famine had ended, she set out for home and urged her daughters-in-law to return to their own people and gods, releasing them from any obligation even in her own grief (Ruth 1:6-13). Orpah went back; Ruth refused and bound herself to Naomi permanently. Arriving in Bethlehem, Naomi told the women who recognized her not to call her Naomi ('pleasant') but Mara ('bitter'), because 'the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me' — Scripture records this raw complaint without softening it, an honest lament rather than a sin needing rebuke (Ruth 1:19-21). Her circumstances turned when Ruth, gleaning for them both, 'happened' upon the field of Boaz, a relative of Elimelech — providence, Naomi recognized: 'the LORD has not withdrawn His kindness' (Ruth 2:1-3, 20). Naomi devised the plan that sent Ruth to the threshing floor to invoke Boaz's role as kinsman-redeemer, guiding her through a custom Naomi understood and Ruth, as an outsider, would not have known alone (Ruth 3). When Boaz married Ruth and she bore a son, the women of Bethlehem told Naomi, 'a son has been born to Naomi,' and Naomi nursed him — the bitterness of chapter one answered by name in chapter four (Ruth 4:14-17).

Naomi went through an incredibly hard time — she lost her husband and both of her sons while living far from home, and she felt so sad and bitter that she asked people to call her a different name that meant 'bitter' instead of her own name, which meant 'pleasant.' But Naomi's story didn't end in sadness. Her daughter-in-law Ruth stayed loyally by her side, and Naomi wisely guided Ruth toward a kind relative named Boaz, who ended up marrying Ruth. When Ruth and Boaz had a baby, the women of the town told Naomi that a son had been born to her, and Naomi's joy returned. Naomi's story shows that even in our bitterest, saddest seasons, God can still bring comfort and joy back into our lives in ways we don't expect.

Thought for Today

Naomi’s story teaches us about bitterness and restoration; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Ruth 1; Ruth 2:1-3; Ruth 3; Ruth 4:14-17
