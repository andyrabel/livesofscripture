Naomi

Also called: Mara
OT Judges

An Israelite widow who lost her husband and both sons in Moab, returned to Bethlehem calling herself 'Mara' (bitter), and was restored through her daughter-in-law Ruth's loyalty and the kindness of her kinsman Boaz, becoming grandmother in the line leading to David.

For younger readers

Naomi lost almost everything, but God used her daughter-in-law Ruth's love to bring joy back into her life.

Life Story

Naomi left Bethlehem with her husband Elimelech and two sons during a famine and settled in Moab, where her sons married Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. Within about ten years, Elimelech and both sons died, leaving Naomi without husband, sons, or security in a foreign land (Ruth 1:1-5). When she heard the famine in Judah had ended, she set out for home and urged her two daughters-in-law to return instead to their own people and gods, releasing them from any obligation to her even in her own grief — a real, unforced blessing, not a test (Ruth 1:6-13). Orpah went back; Ruth refused and bound herself to Naomi permanently. Arriving in Bethlehem, Naomi told the women who recognized her not to call her Naomi ('pleasant') anymore but Mara ('bitter'), because 'the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me' — Scripture records this raw complaint against God without softening or correcting it, an honest lament rather than a sin needing rebuke (Ruth 1:19-21). Naomi's circumstances began turning when Ruth, gleaning to support them both, 'happened' to come to the field of Boaz, a relative of Elimelech — a coincidence the narrator clearly means as providence rather than chance, and Naomi recognized it as such: 'the LORD has not withdrawn His kindness' (Ruth 2:1-3, 20). Naomi then devised the plan that sent Ruth to the threshing floor to invoke Boaz's role as kinsman-redeemer, guiding her through a culturally coded request for protection and family continuity that Naomi, as an older Israelite widow, understood and Ruth, as a Moabite outsider, would not have known on her own (Ruth 3). When Boaz married Ruth and she bore a son, the women of Bethlehem told Naomi directly, 'a son has been born to Naomi,' and Naomi took the child, Obed, and nursed him — the bitterness of chapter one answered by name in chapter four (Ruth 4:14-17).

Family

Naomi was the wife of Elimelech and mother of Mahlon and Chilion, both of whom died in Moab; mother-in-law to Ruth and Orpah, and the one through whom Ruth's loyalty to Israel and to Israel's God is first tested and proven (Ruth 1:16-17). Through Ruth and Boaz, Naomi became grandmother, in the fullest covenantal sense of that word even though not by blood, to Obed, whose own descent runs forward to Jesse and to David — meaning the bitterest widow in the book of Ruth ends it as an ancestor, however unofficially, of Israel's greatest king (Ruth 4:17-22).

References: Ruth 1; Ruth 2:1-3; Ruth 3; Ruth 4:14-17
