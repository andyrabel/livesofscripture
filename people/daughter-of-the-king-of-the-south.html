daughter of the King of the South

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

An unnamed princess, daughter of the king described in Daniel's prophecy as "the king of the South," given in a political marriage to seal an alliance with a rival kingdom, which Daniel foretold would not hold, ending in her being "given up" along with those who had supported her.

For younger readers

In one of Daniel's long prophecies about future kings and kingdoms, he describes a princess called only "the daughter of the King of the South." Her father sent her to marry a king from a rival kingdom, hoping the marriage would create peace between the two nations. But Daniel's prophecy said this peace wouldn't last -- the alliance would fail, and she would lose the protection of her father and husband alike. Many Bible scholars believe this prophecy came true in real history, generations after Daniel, involving a real princess whose political marriage ended badly. It's a sobering example of how even careful human plans for peace can fail when they're not built on trusting God.

Interpretive note: Daniel 11 does not name this woman; she is identified only by her father's throne, "the king of the South." Many interpreters, reading Daniel 11 as detailed prophecy of the Hellenistic-era conflicts between the Ptolemaic and Seleucid kingdoms, identify her with Berenice, daughter of Ptolemy II Philadelphus of Egypt, given in marriage to the Seleucid king Antiochus II Theos to seal a peace treaty around 250 BC -- a marriage Scripture appears to describe collapsing with her death. This identification rests on extra-biblical Hellenistic history, not on the biblical text itself, so it is noted here as a common scholarly reading rather than a stated fact.

Life Story

In Daniel's long prophecy of chapter 11, tracing centuries of conflict between two rival kingdoms Daniel calls "the king of the North" and "the king of the South," one otherwise unnamed figure receives a brief but poignant mention: "After some years they will form an alliance, and the daughter of the king of the South will come to the king of the North to carry out a peaceful arrangement. But she will not retain her position of power, nor will he remain with his power, but she will be given up, along with those who brought her in and the one who sired her, as well as he who supported her in those times" (Daniel 11:6, NASB). The prophecy describes a diplomatic marriage arranged to seal peace between two hostile powers -- and foretells that the arrangement would fail, that she would lose her position, and that she, her father, her husband, and her supporters would all be "given up," language suggesting betrayal or death rather than a peaceful ending. Interpreters generally read this as remarkably precise prophecy of real Hellenistic-era history: a political marriage between rival Greek dynasties following Alexander the Great's empire, generations after Daniel's own lifetime (see interpretive note on the traditional identification). Scripture never gives her a personal name, only her father's title -- yet even a nameless political pawn in a worldly alliance is recorded and remembered by God, her fate foretold with precision no human observer could have engineered.
