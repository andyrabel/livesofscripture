Tirzah (F)

Name means “Benevolent; complaisant; pleasing” — Hebrew

First named in Numbers 26:33

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Tirzah was one of five daughters of Zelophehad, a man of Manasseh's tribe who died in the wilderness leaving no sons (Numbers 26:33; 27:1). Along with her sisters Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, and Milcah, Tirzah came before Moses, Eleazar the priest, and the leaders of the whole congregation at the tent of meeting with a direct legal claim: their father had died for his own sin, not as part of Korah's rebellion, and it was unjust that his name should be lost from his clan simply because he had no son -- they asked for a possession among their father's brothers (27:2-4). Moses, uncertain how to rule, brought their case directly to the LORD, who answered unequivocally: 'The daughters of Zelophehad are right in their statements... you shall surely give them a hereditary possession' -- and God used the occasion to establish a permanent statute of inheritance law for Israel covering exactly this situation (27:5-11). Later, when the leaders of their tribe raised a follow-up concern about land passing out of the tribe through marriage, God's law was refined so the sisters were required to marry within their own tribe, which they did, marrying their cousins (36:1-12), and their inheritance was confirmed in the land allotment under Joshua (Joshua 17:3-6). Tirzah's petition remains one of Scripture's clearest examples of God directly expanding His law in response to a reasoned, faith-filled appeal for justice.

Tirzah was one of five sisters -- Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah -- whose father Zelophehad died in the wilderness with no sons to inherit his share of the promised land. The sisters bravely came before Moses and the leaders of Israel and asked for their father's inheritance to be given to them instead, since it wasn't fair for his name and land to disappear just because he had no sons. Moses brought their case to God, and God agreed with them completely, creating a new law so that daughters could inherit when there were no sons. Tirzah and her sisters are a wonderful example of using respectful, faith-filled boldness to ask for what is right.

Thought for Today

Tirzah’s story teaches us about justice for women; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Numbers 26:33; Numbers 27:1-11; Numbers 36:1-12; Joshua 17:3-6
