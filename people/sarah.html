Sarah (F)

Also called: Sarai
Name means “Lady; princess; princess of the multitude” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 11:29-31

OT Patriarchal

Sarai left Ur and then Haran with her husband Abram at God's call, carrying the quiet grief of barrenness (Genesis 11:29-31). Twice Abraham told foreign rulers she was only his sister, endangering her until God intervened both times (Genesis 12:10-20; 20). When decades passed without a child, Sarai gave her servant Hagar to Abram to bear an heir — a solution that produced lasting grief, recorded plainly with neither woman cast simply as villain (Genesis 16:1-6). When the LORD renewed His promise and renamed her Sarah ('princess'), declaring she herself would bear the promised son within the year, Sarah laughed in disbelief at ninety, then denied laughing out of fear — prompting God's reply, 'Is anything too difficult for the LORD?' (Genesis 17:15-21; 18:1-15). The promise held: Isaac was born, and this time Sarah's laughter was pure joy (Genesis 21:1-7). Her hardest moment as a mother came when she saw Hagar's son Ishmael in some way threatening or mocking Isaac and demanded Abraham send both away — a request that distressed Abraham until God told him to listen to her, since the covenant would run through Isaac alone (Genesis 21:8-14). Sarah died at 127 in Hebron; Abraham's purchase of the cave of Machpelah to bury her became Israel's first legal foothold in the promised land (Genesis 23). The New Testament remembers her for faith: Hebrews 11:11 credits her considering God faithful despite her body's inability, and 1 Peter 3:5-6 holds her up as a model of hope set on God.

Sarah waited a very, very long time for God to give her the son He had promised — so long that she laughed in surprise when she was told it would finally happen, because she was already very old! Along the way, Sarah struggled with impatience and even made a difficult decision that hurt another woman in her household when she tried to make the promise happen her own way instead of waiting on God. But God kept His promise anyway, and Isaac was born, filling Sarah with real joy this time instead of doubt. The New Testament remembers Sarah especially for her faith — trusting that God would keep His word even when it seemed impossible. Sarah's story reminds us that God is patient with our doubts and mistakes, and that His promises come true in His own timing, not ours.

Thought for Today

Sarah’s story teaches us about faith; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 11:29-31; Genesis 12:10-20; Genesis 16; Genesis 17:15-21; Genesis 18:1-15; Genesis 20; Genesis 21:1-7; Genesis 23; Hebrews 11:11; 1 Peter 3:5-6
