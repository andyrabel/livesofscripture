Titus (M)

Name means “Pleasing” — Latin

First named in 2 Corinthians 2:13

NT Apostolic

Titus, a Gentile, accompanied Paul and Barnabas to the Jerusalem council, where Paul specifically notes he was not compelled to be circumcised, holding him up as living proof that Gentile believers did not need to adopt Jewish law to be full members of the church (Galatians 2:1-3). Paul relied on Titus for some of his most sensitive pastoral work, particularly with the troubled and often combative church at Corinth: he sent Titus to deliver a severe corrective letter and was so anxious for news of how it had been received that he could not rest until finding him (2 Corinthians 2:12-13; 7:5-7). When Titus finally arrived with a good report of the Corinthians' repentance, Paul was deeply comforted, writing that Titus's own spirit had been refreshed by them, and commending him as "my partner and fellow worker" (7:6-7, 13-15; 8:23). Paul later left Titus on the island of Crete specifically "to set in order what remains and appoint elders in every city," and Paul's short letter to Titus gives detailed qualifications for church elders alongside instruction on sound doctrine and godly living for every group within the church (Titus 1:5-9; 2:1-10). Paul's closing instructions ask Titus to meet him at Nicopolis for the winter, reflecting the ongoing, active partnership between the two through years of demanding ministry (3:12).

Titus was a Gentile who became one of Paul's trusted companions and co-workers in ministry. Titus is a great example of the truth Paul taught, that Gentile believers didn't need to follow the Jewish law's requirement of circumcision to be fully accepted as Christians -- Paul specifically didn't require it of him. Paul relied on Titus for difficult assignments, especially with the church in Corinth, which had many problems and tensions Paul needed to address carefully. Titus's visits and reports genuinely comforted and encouraged Paul during a difficult time. Paul later left Titus on the island of Crete with an important task: organizing the churches there and appointing qualified elders to lead them. Paul's short letter to Titus in the New Testament gives him detailed guidance on this responsibility and on living out sound Christian teaching in daily life.

Thought for Today

Titus’s story teaches us about mentorship; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Corinthians 2:13; 2 Corinthians 7:6-14; 2 Corinthians 8:6-23; 2 Corinthians 12:18; Galatians 2:1-3; 2 Timothy 4:10; Titus 1:4
