Israel (M)

Also called: Jacob, Jeshurun
Name means “Who prevails with God” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 25:19-34

OT Patriarchal

Jacob, Isaac and Rebekah's younger twin, emerged grasping Esau's heel and later bought Esau's birthright for a bowl of stew (Genesis 25:24-34). At his mother's urging, he deceived his blind, dying father into giving him Esau's blessing instead, disguising himself as his hairy brother -- a lie that sent him fleeing Esau's anger to Rebekah's family in Haran (27:1-45). On the way, God renewed Abraham's covenant promise to him in a dream of a ladder to heaven at Bethel (28:10-22). In Haran, Jacob served his uncle Laban twenty years, himself deceived on his wedding night into marrying Leah before Rachel, and fathered eleven sons and a daughter through Leah, Rachel, and their servants Bilhah and Zilpah before fleeing Laban's growing hostility (29-31). Returning toward Canaan, Jacob wrestled all night with a divine figure at Peniel, who renamed him Israel -- he struggles with God -- and left him limping (32:22-32). He reconciled peacefully with Esau, but his family's troubles continued: his daughter Dinah was assaulted, and his favorite son Joseph, born to Rachel, was sold into slavery by his own brothers, who told Jacob he was dead (34; 37). Decades later, famine drove Jacob's sons to Egypt, where Joseph, now a ruler there, revealed himself and brought his aging father to live out his final years in Goshen (42-47). Before dying, Jacob blessed each of his twelve sons with prophetic words shaping Israel's tribes, and was buried beside Abraham and Isaac at Machpelah (48-50).

Jacob was Isaac and Rebekah's son, and the twin brother of Esau. As a young man, Jacob tricked his father into giving him a special blessing meant for Esau, which made Esau furious, so Jacob ran away to live with his uncle Laban. Along the way, God gave him an amazing dream of a stairway reaching to heaven and promised to always be with him. Jacob worked for Laban many years, married Leah and Rachel, and had twelve sons and a daughter. One night, God wrestled with Jacob and gave him a new name, Israel. Jacob and Esau made peace years later. When Jacob was old, his family moved to Egypt to be near his son Joseph, and Jacob blessed each of his sons before he died.

Thought for Today

Jacob wrestled with God all night and wouldn't let go until he was blessed. May we learn to hold on to God that same way.
