Laban (M)

First named in Genesis 24:29-60

OT Patriarchal

Laban, Rebekah's brother, first appears eagerly welcoming Abraham's servant and the marriage proposal for his sister, drawn by the gifts of gold he saw (Genesis 24:29-31, 50-53). A generation later, his nephew Jacob arrived fleeing Esau and fell in love with Laban's younger daughter Rachel, agreeing to serve seven years for her (29:15-20). On the wedding night, Laban substituted his older daughter Leah in the dark, justifying it afterward by local custom that the older daughter must marry first -- deception matching, almost point for point, the deception Jacob himself had once practiced on his own father (29:21-27). Jacob served another seven years for Rachel, and over twenty years total, Laban changed his wages ten times, trying to limit Jacob's growing flocks (31:7, 41). When Jacob finally fled with his family and possessions without warning, Laban pursued him for seven days, but God warned Laban in a dream not to harm Jacob (31:22-24). Confronting Jacob over his household idols Rachel had secretly stolen, Laban's underlying suspicion and grievance surfaced fully before the two men settled their long, uneasy relationship with a covenant and a heap of stones at Mizpah, agreeing to part ways in peace (31:43-55). Laban's story shows deception passed down and repaid within one family, framed throughout by God's quiet, persistent protection of Jacob despite his uncle's repeated attempts to control and cheat him.

Laban was Jacob's uncle, and later his father-in-law too. When Jacob fled from his brother Esau and came to live with Laban's family, he fell in love with Laban's younger daughter Rachel and agreed to work seven years to marry her. But on the wedding night, Laban tricked Jacob into marrying his older daughter Leah instead, and Jacob had to work another seven years for Rachel. Laban kept changing Jacob's wages many times over the twenty years Jacob worked for him. Eventually Jacob left secretly with his family, and after Laban chased him down, the two made a peace agreement instead of fighting. Laban's story shows how deception can run in a family, but also how God protected Jacob through it all.

Thought for Today

Laban’s story warns us about deception; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 24:29-60; Genesis 29:1-30; Genesis 30:25-43; Genesis 31:1-55
