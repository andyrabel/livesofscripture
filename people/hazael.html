Hazael (M)

Name means “That sees God” — Aramaic

First named in 1 Kings 19:15-17

OT Divided Monarchy

Hazael first appears as a servant of Ben-hadad, king of Aram, whom the LORD instructed Elijah to anoint as Aram's future king (1 Kings 19:15-17), a sign of coming judgment on Israel's idolatry. Years later, when Ben-hadad sent Hazael to ask the prophet Elisha whether he would recover from illness, Elisha stared at him until Hazael felt ashamed, then wept -- foreseeing the atrocities Hazael would commit against Israel as king, from burning fortresses to killing children (2 Kings 8:8-13). Hazael returned and smothered Ben-hadad with a wet cloth, then took the throne himself (8:14-15). As king, he fought Israel and Judah for decades: wounding King Joram at Ramoth-gilead (8:28-29), oppressing Israel throughout Jehu's and Jehoahaz's reigns until Israel was reduced to almost no army (10:32; 13:3-7,22), and extorting tribute from Judah's King Jehoash by marching on Jerusalem, turned aside only when Jehoash emptied the temple treasury to pay him off (12:17-18). The prophet Amos later condemned Aram's cruelty under "the house of Hazael" (Amos 1:4). Hazael's decades-long reign of violence, foretold in weeping and fulfilled in full, stands in Scripture as a stark instance of a pagan king used, unknowingly, as an instrument of God's judgment on His own unfaithful people.

Hazael was an official who served Ben-hadad, the king of Aram (also called Syria). Long before Hazael became king himself, God told the prophet Elijah that Hazael would one day rule Aram. Later, the prophet Elisha met Hazael and wept, because he could see how much suffering Hazael would bring to Israel as king. Soon after, Hazael took the throne of Aram by killing his own master. As king, Hazael fought against Israel again and again for many years, capturing cities and causing great hardship for God's people. His long reign was hard for Israel, but it was also part of the difficult training God allowed His people to go through because of their own unfaithfulness -- and even Hazael's power had limits God had already set.

Thought for Today

Elisha wept, foreseeing the suffering Hazael's cruelty would bring on Israel, long before it happened. Jesus also wept over Jerusalem, grieving the judgment His people's unfaithfulness had brought.

References: 1 Kings 19:15-17; 2 Kings 8:8-28; 2 Kings 9:14-15; 2 Kings 10:32; 2 Kings 12:17-18; 2 Kings 13:3-25; 2 Chronicles 22:5-6; Amos 1:4
