Festus (M)

Also called: Porcius Festus

First named in Acts 24:27

NT Apostolic

Porcius Festus succeeded Felix as governor of Judea and, within days, was pressed by Jewish leaders to transfer Paul to Jerusalem for trial -- a request driven by a secret plot to ambush and kill Paul along the road (Acts 25:1-3). Festus instead invited the accusers to bring their case to him in Caesarea, where the unresolved charges from Paul's earlier hearing under Felix were repeated without solid proof (25:4-8). Uncertain how to frame charges for a case he did not understand, involving "a certain Jesus, who was dead, but whom Paul asserted to be alive," Festus asked if Paul was willing to go to Jerusalem for trial instead (25:9, 19-20). Paul, recognizing the danger and his rights as a Roman citizen, formally appealed his case directly to Caesar, a request Festus, after conferring with his council, was obligated to honor (25:10-12). When King Agrippa and his sister Bernice arrived to pay their respects to the new governor, Festus used the occasion to have Paul present his case again, hoping for help framing the charges to send to Rome (25:13-27). As Paul spoke of Christ's suffering, resurrection, and the light proclaimed to Jews and Gentiles, Festus interrupted loudly: "Paul, you are out of your mind! Your great learning is driving you mad" (26:24) -- a baffled reaction from a Roman official encountering the gospel's central claims. Paul's appeal ultimately sent him on to Rome, fulfilling what Jesus had earlier told him he must do (Acts 23:11; 27:1).

Festus became the new Roman governor after Felix, and he inherited Paul's ongoing legal case. When Jewish leaders in Jerusalem asked Festus to have Paul transferred there for trial (secretly hoping to ambush and kill him on the way), Festus instead invited them to bring their case to him in Caesarea. During the hearing, King Agrippa and his sister Bernice also came to hear Paul speak, since Festus wasn't sure exactly what charges to send along with Paul if he was sent to Rome. As Paul explained his faith and the resurrection of Jesus, Festus interrupted loudly, saying Paul's great learning was driving him mad. Because Paul, as a Roman citizen, had already appealed his case to Caesar, Festus ultimately had no choice but to send him on to Rome for trial before the emperor.

Thought for Today

Festus’s story teaches us about political expediency; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Acts 24:27; Acts 25:1-24; Acts 26:24-32
