James (M)

First named in Matthew 13:55

NT Apostolic

James grew up in Nazareth as one of Jesus' younger half-brothers, the son of Mary, named alongside Joseph, Simon, and Judas when Jesus' hometown neighbors questioned how a mere "carpenter's son" could teach with such authority (Matthew 13:55; Mark 6:3). During Jesus' earthly ministry His brothers were not among His followers -- John records elsewhere that "not even His brothers were believing in Him" -- which makes James's later transformation striking. By the time the New Testament epistle bearing his name was written, its author identifies himself simply: "James, a bond-servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ" (James 1:1), writing with pastoral authority to scattered Jewish believers about enduring trials, controlling the tongue, and the insistence that genuine faith always produces works -- "faith without works is dead" (James 2:26). His brother Jude introduces his own letter by identifying himself as "a bond-servant of Jesus Christ, and brother of James" (Jude 1:1), evidence of how recognized James's standing had become among early believers. Widely understood alongside these letters as the James who came to lead the Jerusalem church after the resurrection, his path -- from a skeptical brother to a servant who calls himself Christ's bond-servant -- traces one of the New Testament's clearest arcs from unbelief to devoted leadership.

James was one of Jesus' younger half-brothers, growing up in the same household in Nazareth. During Jesus' ministry, His brothers -- including James -- didn't yet believe He was the Messiah. But something changed for James, and he became a respected leader among the first Christians. James wrote one of the letters in the New Testament that bears his name, full of practical wisdom about living out real faith -- famous for saying that faith without action is dead. His brother Jude also wrote a New Testament letter and introduced himself simply as "a brother of James," showing how well-known and respected James had become among believers.

Thought for Today

James doubted his own brother Jesus during his earthly ministry. Jesus patiently turned him into a faithful servant — may he turn us too.

References: Matthew 13:55; Matthew 27:56; Mark 6:3; Mark 15:40; Mark 16:1; Luke 24:10; James 1:1; Jude 1:1
