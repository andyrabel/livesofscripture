Shaphan (M)

Name means “Rock badger (coney)” — Hebrew

First named in 2 Kings 22:3-14

OT Divided Monarchy

Shaphan the scribe served King Josiah of Judah, and was entrusted with overseeing the silver collected at the temple for its repair, instructing Hilkiah the high priest to have it paid out faithfully to the workmen without even requiring an accounting, 'for they deal faithfully' (2 Kings 22:3-7). During this repair project, Hilkiah discovered the Book of the Law in the temple and gave it to Shaphan, who first read it himself before bringing it to Josiah, reporting on the silver and then reading the newly found scroll aloud to the king directly (22:8-10). Josiah's reaction was immediate and severe: he tore his clothes, recognizing how far the nation had drifted from what the book commanded and fearing God's resulting wrath (22:11). Josiah then sent a delegation -- including Shaphan, his son Ahikam, and others -- to inquire of the LORD about the book's words, and they went specifically to Huldah the prophetess, who confirmed both the coming judgment on Judah for its unfaithfulness and God's mercy toward Josiah personally for his humble, repentant response (22:12-20). This discovery, delivered faithfully into the king's hands through Shaphan, set off one of Judah's most thorough religious reforms, including the reinstitution of the Passover on a scale not seen since the days of the judges (23:1-25; 2 Chronicles 34:8-21, 29-33). Shaphan's family continued serving faithfully in the reform generation that followed, including his son Ahikam, who later protected Jeremiah from execution.

Shaphan was King Josiah's scribe, a trusted official responsible for handling the money collected for repairing the temple in Jerusalem. During this repair work, the high priest Hilkiah found a copy of the Book of the Law that had been lost or forgotten, and gave it to Shaphan. Shaphan read it, then went and read it aloud to King Josiah himself. When Josiah heard the words of God's law and realized how far Israel had drifted from it, he tore his clothes in grief and sent Shaphan and others to consult the prophetess Huldah for guidance. Shaphan's faithful handling of both the temple funds and this life-changing discovery helped set off one of the greatest religious reforms in Judah's history.

Thought for Today

Shaphan's part was simple: carry God's rediscovered word into the king's hands without adding to it. That humble stewardship still points us to Jesus, God's Word made flesh.

References: 2 Kings 22:3-14; 2 Chronicles 34:8-21
