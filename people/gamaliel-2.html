Gamaliel (M)

First named in Acts 5:34

NT Apostolic

Gamaliel was a Pharisee and teacher of the law, "held in high esteem by all the people," a member of the Sanhedrin who later trained the young Saul of Tarsus, who would call himself "educated under Gamaliel, strictly according to the law of our fathers" (Acts 5:34; 22:3). When the apostles were arrested for continuing to preach in Jesus's name despite an earlier order to stop, some council members wanted them put to death; Gamaliel had them removed briefly and addressed his fellow council members with careful, historically grounded reasoning (5:33-35). He recalled two earlier movements -- led by Theudas and by Judas of Galilee -- that had drawn significant followings but collapsed and scattered once their leaders were killed, and argued the same pattern would apply here: "if this plan or action is of men, it will be overthrown; but if it is of God, you will not be able to overthrow them; or else you may even be found fighting against God" (5:36-39). The council was persuaded, had the apostles flogged, ordered them again not to speak in Jesus's name, and released them (5:40). Gamaliel's counsel reflects genuine wisdom and restraint rather than personal faith in Christ -- Scripture never states he became a believer -- but his cautious argument inadvertently protected the earliest apostles at a critical moment, and his most famous student would go on to become the New Testament's most consequential missionary and writer.

Gamaliel was a highly respected Pharisee and teacher of the Jewish law, so honored that he was later identified as the very teacher who trained the apostle Paul (then called Saul) in his early years. When the apostles were arrested for preaching about Jesus and some council members wanted to have them killed, Gamaliel stood up and urged real caution. He reminded the council of other movements led by rebellious figures that had eventually fallen apart on their own once their leaders died. His wise advice was that if this new movement about Jesus was merely a human plan, it would fail the same way -- but if it was truly from God, fighting against it would mean fighting against God Himself. The council listened to Gamaliel's counsel, had the apostles flogged as a lesser punishment, and let them go.

Thought for Today

Gamaliel counseled that a movement not of God would fail, yet Scripture never says he believed in Jesus himself. Wise counsel is not the same as trusting Christ.

References: Acts 5:34; Acts 22:3
