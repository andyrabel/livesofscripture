Zebah (M)

Name means “Victim; sacrifice” — Hebrew

First named in Judges 8:5-21

OT Judges

Zebah was one of two Midianite kings, alongside Zalmunna, whom Gideon pursued and defeated after routing Midian's main army with a mere three hundred men (Judges 7:19-25). Fleeing east of the Jordan with the remnant of their forces -- fifteen thousand men, all that remained of an army of a hundred and twenty thousand -- Zebah and Zalmunna were caught off guard and captured by Gideon at Karkor (8:10-12). Along the pursuit, exhausted and hungry, Gideon had asked the towns of Succoth and Penuel for bread for his men, but their leaders mocked him, doubting he would ever actually capture the two kings; Gideon vowed to return and punish them, which he later did (8:4-9, 13-17). Gideon then confronted Zebah and Zalmunna directly, asking about men they had killed at Tabor, and learned they were his own brothers; he declared that if the kings had spared his brothers' lives, he would have spared theirs (8:18-19). He ordered his young son Jether to execute them, but the boy, too frightened, could not draw his sword -- so Zebah and Zalmunna themselves urged Gideon to kill them personally, saying, "As the man is, so is his strength," unwilling to die at a boy's hand (8:20-21). Gideon killed them both and took the crescent ornaments from their camels' necks. Psalm 83:11 later recalls Zebah and Zalmunna together as examples of decisive divine judgment on Israel's enemies.

Zebah was one of two kings of Midian, a nation that had been attacking God's people. Along with the other king, Zalmunna, he led an army against Israel, but God helped a leader named Gideon defeat them using just three hundred men. Zebah and Zalmunna ran away, but Gideon chased them down and caught them. Along the way, some towns refused to help Gideon's tired soldiers, so Gideon promised to deal with those towns later, which he did. In the end, Gideon defeated Zebah and Zalmunna because of the harm they had caused his own family. Their story is remembered later in the Bible as an example of God bringing justice on Israel's enemies.

Thought for Today

Zebah's mighty army shrank to nothing before Gideon's three hundred. God doesn't need our strength to win — he just asks for our yes.

References: Judges 8:5-21; Psalms 83:11
