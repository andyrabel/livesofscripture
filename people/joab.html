Joab (M)

First named in 1 Samuel 26:6

OT United Monarchy

Joab, David's nephew through his sister Zeruiah, commanded David's army through most of his reign, leading the capture of Jerusalem from the Jebusites and numerous military victories (1 Chronicles 11:6; 2 Samuel throughout). He was capable, decisive, and fiercely protective of David's throne -- yet Scripture repeatedly shows him acting with a ruthlessness David could not control. He murdered Abner in peacetime to avenge his brother Asahel's death (2 Samuel 3:27), later murdered Amasa, his own replacement as commander, in the same treacherous manner (20:9-10), and directly disobeyed David's explicit order to spare Absalom's life, killing him himself with three spears while he hung trapped in a tree (18:5, 14-15). Joab could also be a voice of hard wisdom: he warned David against publicly mourning Absalom in a way that would demoralize the very soldiers who had saved his throne, and David heeded him (19:1-8). Yet his unresolved killings of Abner and Amasa weighed on David to the end of his life; on his deathbed, David charged Solomon not to let Joab's "gray hair go down to Sheol in peace," for shedding innocent blood in peacetime under the guise of war (1 Kings 2:5-6). When Joab, sensing the reckoning, fled to the altar's horns for sanctuary near the end of Solomon's succession struggle, Solomon had him executed there, closing out one of David's most capable but morally compromised servants.

Joab was King David's nephew and the commander of his army for most of David's reign. He was a skilled and often fiercely loyal military leader who helped David capture Jerusalem and win many battles. But Joab also had a ruthless, violent side. He killed several men outside of battle out of revenge or jealousy, including Abner and Amasa, even though David had made peace with both of them. Joab also directly disobeyed David's order to spare Absalom's life during the rebellion, killing him himself. Because Joab was never held accountable for this violence during David's lifetime, David gave instructions on his deathbed that Solomon should finally deal with it, and Solomon eventually had Joab executed for the killings he had committed.

Thought for Today

Through Joab’s experience of military loyalty, God teaches us to trust His wisdom and follow Jesus faithfully.
