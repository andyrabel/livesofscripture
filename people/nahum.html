Nahum (M)

Name means “Comforter; penitent” — Hebrew
Prophesied to Assyria, c. 663 BC–612 BC

First named in Nahum 1:1

OT Divided Monarchy

Almost nothing is recorded of Nahum beyond his hometown, Elkosh, a location no longer identifiable with certainty (Nahum 1:1). His entire brief book is directed at Nineveh, capital of the Assyrian empire that had brutalized Israel and Judah for generations — the same city that had repented under Jonah's preaching roughly a century earlier, but had since reverted to violent cruelty. Nahum opens by affirming God's patient character even as he pronounces judgment: "The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and the LORD will by no means clear the guilty" (Nahum 1:3). He describes Nineveh's coming destruction in vivid, almost cinematic detail — chariots racing in the streets, its defenses collapsing, its plunder stripped bare — announcing that the empire's cruelty toward conquered nations had finally reached its limit (Nahum 2:1-3:19). Nahum points to the earlier fall of Egyptian Thebes ("No-amon") as proof that no city, however fortified, is beyond God's reach when its cruelty is finished being tolerated (Nahum 3:8-10). For Judah, still under Assyrian domination, Nahum's message was meant as comfort as much as threat: "Behold, on the mountains the feet of him who brings good news, who proclaims peace!" (Nahum 1:15). Nineveh did fall to a coalition of Babylonians and Medes in 612 BC, within a generation of Nahum's prophecy.

We don't know much about Nahum except where he was from. His short book is all about Nineveh, the capital city of a cruel empire called Assyria that had hurt God's people for a long time. Interestingly, Nineveh had actually turned back to God once before, when the prophet Jonah preached there — but by Nahum's time, the city had gone back to its cruel ways. Nahum announced that God is patient, but He would not let Nineveh's cruelty go on forever, and the city's defeat was coming. For the people of Judah, who had suffered under this powerful empire, this news was actually a comfort — like hearing that a bully was finally going to be stopped. And sure enough, Nineveh really was defeated, not long after Nahum's warning. Nahum's story reminds us that God sees when people are treated unfairly, and He does not ignore it forever.

Thought for Today

Nahum announced that Nineveh's long-tolerated cruelty had finally reached its limit, since 'the LORD will by no means clear the guilty.' Jesus is the one place that same justice was actually satisfied for us instead of falling on us, at a cost we could never have paid ourselves.
