Abel (M)

First named in Genesis 4:2-25

OT Primeval History

Abel is Adam and Eve's second son, a keeper of flocks alongside his older brother Cain's work tilling the ground (Genesis 4:2). When the brothers each bring an offering to the LORD, Abel brings "of the firstlings of his flock and of their fat portions" -- the best of what he has -- and the LORD looks with favor on Abel's offering but not on Cain's (4:3-5). Scripture does not fully explain the distinction in the moment, but Hebrews 11:4 supplies the reason retrospectively: "By faith Abel offered to God a better sacrifice than Cain, through which he was commended as righteous... and through faith, though he is dead, he still speaks." Abel's righteousness draws his brother's jealousy rather than admiration; Cain lures him into a field and kills him, making Abel the first human being to die and the first martyr for righteousness in Scripture (4:8; Matthew 23:35 later calls him "righteous Abel"). His blood cries out to God from the ground (4:10) -- and the book of Hebrews draws a striking contrast between that cry and the "sprinkled blood" of Jesus, "which speaks better than the blood of Abel" (Hebrews 12:24): Abel's blood cried out for justice against his killer, while Christ's blood speaks a better word, securing mercy for sinners instead of vengeance against them. Abel's brief life ends in violence, but Scripture treats his faith, not his death, as what still speaks.

Abel was the second son of Adam and Eve, and he grew up taking care of sheep. When Abel brought an offering to God, he gave the best of what he had, and God was pleased with his gift, because Abel gave it with a trusting, faithful heart. Sadly, Abel's older brother Cain became jealous because God had not been pleased with his own offering. Cain's jealousy grew into anger, and in a terrible moment, Cain hurt Abel so badly that he died. Abel was the first person in the Bible to die, and the Bible calls him "righteous Abel" because of his faithful heart toward God. Even though his life was cut short, the Bible says Abel's faith "still speaks" to us today. Abel's story also points forward to Jesus, whose own sacrifice speaks an even better word than Abel's -- one of mercy instead of judgment.

Thought for Today

Abel’s story teaches us about faith; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 4:2-25; Matthew 23:35; Luke 11:51; Hebrews 11:4; Hebrews 12:24
