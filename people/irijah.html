Irijah (M)

First named in Jeremiah 37:13-14

OT Divided Monarchy

Irijah, son of Shelemiah and grandson of Hananiah, was a sentry stationed at Jerusalem's Gate of Benjamin during a brief withdrawal of the besieging Babylonian army, prompted by an advancing Egyptian force (Jeremiah 37:5, 11). When the prophet Jeremiah attempted to leave the city to attend to property among his people in the territory of Benjamin, Irijah seized him, declaring, "You are going over to the Chaldeans!" (37:13). Jeremiah denied it -- "A lie! I am not going over to the Chaldeans" -- but Irijah refused to listen and brought him before the city officials instead (37:14). Furious, the officials had Jeremiah beaten and imprisoned in a dungeon, where he remained many days before King Zedekiah secretly summoned him (37:15-17). Irijah's single recorded act -- a wrongful arrest built on suspicion rather than evidence -- set in motion one of the harshest episodes of Jeremiah's long suffering as a prophet, delivering God's unwelcome word to a city unwilling to hear it during its final days before Babylon's conquest.

Irijah was a guard stationed at one of Jerusalem's gates during a very hard time, when the Babylonian army was surrounding the city. One day the prophet Jeremiah tried to leave the city to attend to some family property. Irijah stopped him and accused him of running away to join the enemy. Jeremiah said this wasn't true, but Irijah didn't believe him. He arrested Jeremiah and brought him to the city officials, who were angry with Jeremiah and had him beaten and thrown into a prison. It's a sad reminder that even people who love God, like Jeremiah, can be treated unfairly -- but God was still watching over him.

Thought for Today

Irijah wrongly accused Jeremiah of treason and had him beaten and imprisoned for speaking God's true word. Jesus, too, was falsely accused and punished for telling the truth.
