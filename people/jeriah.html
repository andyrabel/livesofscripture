Jeriah

Also called: Jerijah
OT United Monarchy

Chief of the Hebronite clan of Levites in David's reign; when capable men from his family were found at Jazer in Gilead in the fortieth year of David's reign, the king placed Jeriah and his 2,700 relatives in charge of religious and royal affairs for the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh east of the Jordan.

For younger readers

Jeriah was a leader among the Levites, the tribe God set apart to serve in worship and help run the kingdom's affairs. He led a branch of the Levite family called the Hebronites. Late in King David's reign, David's officials searched the family records to find capable, trustworthy men, and they found a large group of Jeriah's relatives -- 2,700 of them -- living near a place called Jazer, across the Jordan River. David put Jeriah and this whole group in charge of important matters for God and for the king among three of Israel's tribes: Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh.

Life Story

Jeriah appears in the Levitical genealogies of 1 Chronicles as the firstborn son of Hebron, head of the Hebronite family within the clan of Kohath (1 Chronicles 23:19; 24:23). Scripture gives him one distinguishing notice beyond these lists: "In the fortieth year of David's reign the records were searched, and capable men were found among them at Jazer of Gilead" (1 Chronicles 26:31). David's officials had gone looking for reliable men from Jeriah's extended family, and found a large company of them -- 2,700 capable men, heads of households -- living east of the Jordan. The king responded by putting Jeriah and this whole company in charge of "all the affairs of God and of the king" among the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh (26:32), placing spiritual and civil oversight of three tribes in the hands of this one Levite leader and his kin late in David's reign. Jeriah's story is brief and administrative rather than dramatic, but it reflects the broader picture 1 Chronicles paints of David's final years: a king carefully organizing worship, administration, and the wider tribal structure of Israel, using capable men wherever his search found them, even in a Transjordan outpost far from Jerusalem.
