Demetrius (M)

Name means “Belonging to corn” — Greek

First named in 3 John 1:12

NT Apostolic

Demetrius receives one of the warmest personal commendations in any of the New Testament letters, in the closing verses of John's third letter: 'Demetrius has received a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself; and we add our testimony, and you know that our testimony is true' (3 John 1:12). This threefold witness -- from everyone who knew him, from the truth itself (meaning his life visibly matched sound doctrine and godly conduct), and from John and his companions personally -- represents about as complete an endorsement of character as Scripture gives to any single individual. The commendation is especially pointed given its placement directly after John's sharp criticism, just a few verses earlier, of Diotrephes, a man 'who loves to be first' and who refused to welcome traveling believers, even putting out of the church those who tried to do so (3 John 1:9-10). Set against that backdrop, Demetrius functions as a clear positive counter-example: whatever his specific role (possibly the very letter-carrier delivering this correspondence to Gaius, the letter's primary recipient), his consistent, well-attested character stood in visible contrast to Diotrephes's self-serving behavior. Scripture gives no further biographical detail about Demetrius, and he is generally regarded as a different person entirely from the silversmith Demetrius of Ephesus in Acts 19, whose opposition to Paul's ministry reflects a very different kind of character.

Demetrius is mentioned by the apostle John near the end of his third letter, and John speaks extremely highly of him -- saying that Demetrius has a good reputation with everyone, and even with the truth itself, and that John and others can testify to his good character too. This warm commendation comes right after John had criticized a man named Diotrephes for behaving selfishly and refusing to welcome traveling believers. Demetrius stands as a positive example, someone whose consistent good behavior and reputation made him easy for John to recommend wholeheartedly to the believers receiving the letter.

Thought for Today

Demetrius's life so matched the truth that even the truth itself testified for him. May our lives give that same good testimony to Jesus.
