Hamor (M)

Name means “An ass; clay; dirt” — Hebrew

First named in Genesis 33:19

OT Patriarchal

Hamor was the Hivite ruler of Shechem, where Jacob had settled after returning to Canaan (Genesis 33:18-19). When Hamor's son Shechem raped Jacob's daughter Dinah, and then wanted her as his wife, Hamor came to Jacob to negotiate -- proposing full intermarriage between their peoples, generous gifts, and free settlement in the land (34:1-12). Jacob's sons, furious over their sister's violation, answered deceitfully: they would agree only if every male in Shechem's city was circumcised like themselves (34:13-17). Hamor and Shechem persuaded their whole city to accept the terms, even offering to share the Israelites' growing wealth as an incentive (34:18-24). But on the third day, while the men of the city were still in pain, Simeon and Levi -- Dinah's full brothers -- attacked the city, killed every male including Hamor and Shechem, rescued Dinah, and the rest of Jacob's sons plundered the city (34:25-29). Jacob rebuked Simeon and Levi for endangering his family among the Canaanites, and later, on his deathbed, formally cursed their anger and cruelty (34:30; 49:5-7). Generations later, Joseph's bones were buried in the very plot of ground bought from Hamor's sons (Joshua 24:32); Judges 9:28 still remembers Shechem as tied to Hamor's name. The episode is one of Genesis's starkest accounts of deception and violence within a patriarchal family, met with no divine endorsement.

Hamor was a ruler of the city of Shechem in Canaan, during the time of Jacob's family. Hamor's son treated Jacob's daughter Dinah very wrongly. Afterward, Hamor came to Jacob to try to make things right by arranging a marriage and a peace agreement between their two families and peoples. Jacob's sons agreed to the plan, but only if all the men of Hamor's city followed one condition first. Sadly, two of Jacob's sons, Simeon and Levi, were not honest about their intentions, and they used the agreement as a chance to attack the city and take terrible revenge, killing Hamor and many others. Jacob was very upset with his sons for what they did, and this sad story shows how anger and dishonesty can cause great harm.

References: Genesis 33:19; Genesis 34:2-26; Joshua 24:32; Judges 9:28; Acts 7:16
