Gilead (M)

Name means “The heap or mass of testimony” — Hebrew

First named in Judges 11:1-2

OT Judges

Gilead is introduced at the start of Jephthah's story simply as "the father of Jephthah," whose mother was a prostitute (Judges 11:1). Gilead's wife also bore him sons, and when those legitimate sons grew up, they drove Jephthah out of the household entirely: "You shall not have an inheritance in our father's house, for you are the son of another woman" (11:2). No further action or word of Gilead himself is recorded -- Scripture does not say whether he approved, resisted, or was even alive when his other sons expelled Jephthah, only that the rejection came from within his own household. This single, painful family detail explains why Jephthah fled to the land of Tob and gathered a band of "worthless fellows" around him (11:3) before later being recalled by Gilead's elders to lead Israel against the Ammonites, ultimately becoming one of the more consequential and tragic judges of Israel (11:4-40). Gilead's household -- marked by a father's moral failure and a family's harsh exclusion of an illegitimate son -- forms the wounded soil out of which one of Judges' most complicated leaders grew.

Gilead was the father of Jephthah, a man who would later become one of Israel's judges. Jephthah's mother was not Gilead's wife, and when Gilead's other sons -- born to his actual wife -- grew up, they refused to let Jephthah share in the family inheritance, driving him away because of how he was born. This painful rejection shaped young Jephthah's early life and pushed him out on his own, long before he became known as a leader and deliverer for Israel.
