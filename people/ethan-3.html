Ethan (M)

Name means “Strong; the gift of the island” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Chronicles 6:44

OT United Monarchy

Ethan was a Levite of the Merarite clan, identified in the genealogies as the son of Kishi (1 Chronicles 6:44) and, in the account of the ark's procession, as the son of Kushaiah (15:17) -- likely the same man under two spellings of his father's name. When David organized the transfer of the ark of the covenant from the house of Obed-edom to Jerusalem, he directed the Levites to appoint singers and musicians to accompany the celebration with joy (1 Chronicles 15:16). From the Merarites, Ethan was chosen alongside Heman of the Kohathites and Asaph of the Gershonites -- one man from each of the three Levitical clans -- to lead the procession's music, appointed "to sound aloud cymbals of bronze" (15:19) while others played harps, lyres, and trumpets. Some later interpreters have identified Ethan the Merarite with Jeduthun, another chief musician named repeatedly afterward in Chronicles, though Scripture never explicitly states the two names belong to one man, so the identification remains a tradition rather than a stated fact. Whichever is true, Ethan's role -- however brief his mention -- placed him at the center of one of Israel's great worship celebrations, when the ark, the visible sign of God's presence, was carried into the city David had made his capital, amid singing, sacrifice, and rejoicing before the LORD.

Ethan was a Levite from the family of Merari, one of the tribes God set apart to serve Him by caring for the tabernacle and leading worship. When King David brought the ark of the covenant -- the special box that reminded Israel of God's presence -- to Jerusalem, he wanted the celebration to be full of music and joy. David chose three men to lead the singers and instruments: Heman, Asaph, and Ethan. Ethan's job was to play loud bronze cymbals while everyone praised God together. It was a small but important part of one of the happiest days in Israel's history, when God's people welcomed His presence back among them with singing.

Thought for Today

Ethan sounded cymbals as the ark, the sign of God's presence, was carried into Jerusalem with singing and joy. Jesus is God's presence, now with us always.
