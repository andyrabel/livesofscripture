Chilion (M)

Name means “Finished; complete; perfect” — Hebrew

First named in Ruth 1:2-5

OT Judges

Chilion was the younger son of Elimelech and Naomi, an Ephrathite family from Bethlehem in Judah who relocated to the country of Moab during a severe famine (Ruth 1:1-2). While there, his father Elimelech died, and Chilion and his brother Mahlon each married Moabite women -- Chilion to Orpah, Mahlon to Ruth (the marriages are confirmed retrospectively in 4:9-10, which identifies Ruth as "the widow of Mahlon" and names Chilion in the same breath). After about ten years in Moab, both Chilion and Mahlon died, leaving Naomi without husband or sons in a foreign land (1:5). Scripture records no words of Chilion's and no individual deed distinguishing him from his brother -- his death, paired with Mahlon's, is the hinge that turns the story toward Naomi's decision to return to Bethlehem and toward Ruth's famous loyalty to her mother-in-law (1:6-18). Chilion's brief, sorrowful account is a reminder that Scripture does not shy from ordinary grief: a family scattered by famine, a foreign marriage, an early death far from home. Out of that loss, God was already at work preparing the line that would lead, through Ruth and Boaz, to David and ultimately to Christ.

Chilion was the son of Elimelech and Naomi, an Israelite family that moved to the country of Moab because there wasn't enough food back home in Bethlehem. While living in Moab, Chilion married a Moabite woman named Orpah. Sadly, Chilion's father died, and then years later Chilion himself died too, along with his brother Mahlon. This left Naomi, Chilion's mother, without her husband or either of her sons. Chilion's story is short and sad, but it sets the stage for the rest of the book of Ruth, where God cares for Naomi and her daughter-in-law Ruth even after this hard loss.

Thought for Today

Chilion's death left Naomi and Ruth widowed in grief, yet God used that loss to shape the family line of Jesus. Our hardest losses can serve God's plan.

References: Ruth 1:2-5; Ruth 4:9
