Segub (M)

Name means “Fortified; raised” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Kings 16:34

OT Divided Monarchy

Segub's entire biblical record is a single clause: "In his days Hiel the Bethelite built Jericho; he laid its foundation with the loss of Abiram his firstborn, and set up its gates with the loss of his youngest son Segub, according to the word of the LORD, which He spoke through Joshua the son of Nun" (1 Kings 16:34). Some seven centuries earlier, after Israel's conquest of Jericho, Joshua had pronounced a curse on anyone who dared rebuild the city: "with the loss of his firstborn he will lay its foundation, and with the loss of his youngest son he will set up its gates" (Joshua 6:26). During wicked King Ahab's reign, Hiel rebuilt Jericho anyway, and Segub's death -- like his brother's -- stands in Scripture as sobering, exact proof that God's word outlasts generations.

Long before Segub was born, Joshua had warned that if anyone ever rebuilt the ruined city of Jericho, it would cost them their oldest and youngest sons (Joshua 6:26). Hundreds of years later, a man named Hiel decided to rebuild Jericho anyway. Sadly, exactly what Joshua had warned came true: Hiel's oldest son Abiram died when the foundation was laid, and his youngest son, Segub, died when the city gates were finally set up. Segub's short, sad story is a sober reminder that God's word, even spoken generations earlier, always comes true.

Thought for Today

Segub's death fulfilled a curse Joshua spoke seven centuries earlier, proving God's word never fails. The promises about Jesus, made centuries before, were fulfilled with that same certainty.
