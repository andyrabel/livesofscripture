David (M)

Name means “Well-beloved” — Hebrew
Reigned over the united kingdom, c. 1010 BC–970 BC

First named in 1 Samuel 16-31

OT United Monarchy

David was the youngest of Jesse's sons, a shepherd anointed king by Samuel while his older brothers were passed over and Saul still reigned — God explaining that He looks at the heart, not outward appearance (1 Samuel 16:1-13). He rose to prominence by killing Goliath, then spent years as a fugitive before becoming king over Judah, then all Israel (1 Samuel 17; 19-31; 2 Samuel 5:1-5). God made an unconditional covenant with David, promising his throne established forever — later fulfilled in Christ (2 Samuel 7:12-16; Luke 1:32-33). Scripture does not soften what came next: while his army was at war, David saw Bathsheba bathing, sent for her, and slept with her; when she became pregnant, he arranged her husband Uriah's death to cover it up (2 Samuel 11). The prophet Nathan confronted him, and David's response in Psalm 51 is one of Scripture's clearest models of repentance; God forgave him, but the child died, and Nathan said the sword would never depart from his house (2 Samuel 12:1-14). It did not: Amnon raped his half-sister Tamar, Absalom murdered Amnon in revenge, then led a rebellion that nearly cost David the throne and ended in Absalom's death, mourned bitterly by David (2 Samuel 13-18). Late in his reign, David also proudly counted Israel's fighting men, bringing a plague on the nation (2 Samuel 24). Despite these failures, David is remembered as a man after God's own heart — not blameless, but never defending his sin once confronted (Psalm 51).

David started out as a young shepherd boy, the youngest in his family, but God chose him to become Israel's king because God looks at a person's heart, not how they look on the outside. David bravely defeated a giant named Goliath and later became a great king. But David made some very serious mistakes as king, including hurting another family badly through choices he knew were wrong. When a prophet confronted David about it, David didn't make excuses — he was truly sorry and turned back to God. Even so, David's family faced a lot of pain afterward because of his choices. David's story shows that God can still love and use someone deeply, even after big mistakes, as long as that person is willing to be honest and truly sorry instead of hiding or excusing what they did.

Thought for Today

God promised David a son whose throne would never end, a promise Luke says came true in Jesus, born of David's own family line to reign forever. May we trust God's promises the way David did, even the ones we won't live to see fulfilled.

References: 1 Samuel 16-31; 2 Samuel; 1 Kings 1-2; 1 Chronicles 21
