Azariah (M)

First named in 1 Kings 4:2

OT Divided Monarchy

Azariah served as chief priest during the reign of King Uzziah, a king whose long rule brought Judah great strength and prosperity -- until, Scripture says plainly, "he became strong, his heart was so proud that he acted corruptly" (2 Chronicles 26:16). Uzziah entered the temple to burn incense on the altar himself, a duty reserved by law for Aaron's consecrated priests alone. Azariah did not stay silent. He went in after the king together with eighty other priests, described as valiant men, and confronted him directly: "It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron who are consecrated, to burn incense. Get out of the sanctuary, for you have been unfaithful, and will have no honor from the LORD God" (26:18). Uzziah, censer still in hand, grew enraged at being corrected by his own priests -- and in that moment of rage, leprosy broke out on his forehead in full view of them all. Azariah and the priests saw it immediately and hurried the king out of the temple; Uzziah himself rushed to leave once he realized the LORD had struck him (26:19-20). He remained a leper for life, barred from the temple he had defiled, while his son governed in his place (26:21). Azariah's willingness to confront a powerful king, at real personal risk, to protect the holiness of worship stands as one of Scripture's clearest examples of priestly courage.

King Uzziah of Judah did many good things, but as he became powerful, he grew proud. One day he walked into the temple to burn incense on the altar -- something only priests were allowed to do. A priest named Azariah, along with eighty other brave priests, went in after the king and stood up to him. They told him it wasn't right for him to do this, and that he should leave the temple. Uzziah became furious at being corrected. But right then, while he was still angry, a skin disease appeared on his forehead. The priests saw it immediately and hurried him out of the temple. Uzziah had this disease for the rest of his life and could never enter the temple again. Azariah's courage in standing up to a powerful king, even at personal risk, protected the holiness of God's house.

Thought for Today

Azariah’s story teaches us about priestly courage; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 1 Kings 4:2; 1 Chronicles 6:10-11; 2 Chronicles 26:17-20; Ezra 7:3
