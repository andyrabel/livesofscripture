Herod (M)

Name means “Son of a hero” — Greek

First named in Matthew 2:1-22

NT Gospels

Herod the Great was ruling as king of Judea, under Rome's authority, when magi from the East arrived in Jerusalem asking, "Where is He who has been born King of the Jews?" (Matthew 2:1-2). Matthew records that Herod "was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him" (2:3) -- a paranoid ruler already infamous for executing rivals, including his own family, to protect his throne. He assembled the chief priests and scribes to learn the Messiah was prophesied to be born in Bethlehem, then secretly summoned the magi to learn the star's exact timing and sent them off under the pretense of wanting to worship the child, while actually intending to kill Him (2:4-8). Warned in a dream not to report back to Herod, the magi returned home another way; Joseph, likewise warned in a dream, fled with Mary and Jesus to Egypt (2:12-14). "When Herod saw that he had been tricked... he sent and slew all the male children who were in Bethlehem and all its vicinity who were two years old and under" (2:16) -- a massacre Matthew connects to Jeremiah's prophecy of Rachel weeping for her children (2:17-18). Herod died shortly afterward (2:19-20). Luke 1:5 places John the Baptist's birth announcement "in the days of Herod, king of Judea," anchoring the Gospel's opening events within this same violent, fearful reign -- the earthly power structure into which the true King was born, and which moved immediately to try to destroy Him.

Herod was the powerful king ruling over Judea when Jesus was born. When wise men from the East arrived asking where they could find the newborn "King of the Jews," Herod was frightened -- he didn't want anyone taking his throne, not even a baby. He secretly asked the wise men to report back once they found the child, pretending he wanted to worship Him too, but he really planned to have Jesus killed. God warned the wise men in a dream not to return to Herod, and warned Joseph to escape to Egypt with Mary and Jesus. Furious at being outwitted, Herod gave a terrible order against the young boys in Bethlehem. Herod died soon after, but his cruelty shows just how much a proud, fearful ruler can fear even a baby who is truly a King.

Thought for Today

Herod tried to destroy the infant King the magi came to worship, but could not stop God's plan. Jesus, the true King, cannot be overthrown by human power.

References: Matthew 2:1-22; Luke 1:5
