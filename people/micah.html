Micah (M)

First named in Judges 17:1-13

OT Judges

Micah, from the hill country of Ephraim, opens Judges' closing section by confessing to his mother that he had stolen eleven hundred pieces of silver from her; she blessed him and dedicated the silver to the LORD, but then had part of it made into a carved and a cast image anyway, which ended up in Micah's own house alongside an ephod and household idols he had already made, with one of his sons serving as priest (Judges 17:1-5). The narrator interrupts to explain the moral chaos of the period plainly: 'In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes' (17:6). When a wandering Levite from Bethlehem passed through, Micah hired him as a live-in household priest, believing this would secure the LORD's blessing on him -- an entirely self-styled, unauthorized arrangement (17:7-13). Soon after, warriors from the tribe of Dan, searching for territory to settle, passed through, recognized the Levite, and persuaded him to become priest for their whole tribe instead, taking Micah's idols and ephod by force despite his protests that they were leaving him with nothing (18:14-26). Dan then set up Micah's images for organized tribal worship at Laish, a corruption of true worship that Judges notes continued for generations (18:27-31). Micah's entire story -- confession, restitution, and immediate relapse into idolatry -- captures Judges' broader portrait of Israel improvising its own religion in the absence of faithful, God-centered leadership.

Micah lived in the hill country of Ephraim during a chaotic time when Israel had no king and, as the book of Judges says, everyone did what was right in their own eyes. Micah had stolen silver from his own mother, but after confessing it, he used the silver to make a carved idol and set up his own private shrine, even hiring a traveling Levite to be his personal priest. Later, warriors from the tribe of Dan passed through, took Micah's idol and priest by force, and set up idol worship for their whole tribe. Micah's story is a vivid example of how far off track worship can drift when people just make up their own religion instead of following what God actually commanded.

Thought for Today

Micah invented his own worship — a homemade idol, a hired priest — sure God would bless it. Jesus alone is the true way to God.
