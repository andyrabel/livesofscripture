Hathach (M)

First named in Esther 4:5-10

OT Post-Exile/Intertestamental

Hathach was one of the king's eunuchs assigned to attend Esther in the Persian court at Susa (Esther 4:5). When Esther heard that Mordecai was in the city gate dressed in sackcloth and ashes, she sent Hathach to find out why. Mordecai told him everything: Haman's plot and the money pledged for the destruction of the Jews, and gave Hathach a copy of the written decree to show Esther, along with an urgent charge -- that she go before the king and plead for her people (4:6-8). Hathach carried Mordecai's words back to Esther (4:9), and when she objected that approaching the king unsummoned could mean death, Hathach carried her answer back to Mordecai (4:10-12), setting up Mordecai's famous reply that she may have come to royal position "for such a time as this" (4:14). Hathach never speaks in his own voice and never acts on his own initiative -- his entire role is faithful, repeated movement between two people who cannot speak directly to each other at the moment it matters most. Without his reliability as a messenger, the exchange that steels Esther's resolve to risk her life for her people could not have happened. Scripture records nothing else about him afterward.

Hathach worked in the king's palace as one of the servants assigned to take care of Queen Esther. When Esther's cousin Mordecai found out about a terrible plan to destroy the Jewish people, he needed to get an urgent message to Esther, who lived inside the palace and couldn't just come and go. Hathach was the one Esther sent to find out what was wrong. He went to Mordecai, learned about the danger, and carried the news -- and Mordecai's plea for Esther to help her people -- back to the queen. Hathach doesn't say much in the story, but without his faithful running back and forth between Mordecai and Esther, the message that saved a nation might never have gotten through.

Thought for Today

Hathach faithfully carried messages between Mordecai and Esther at the moment their people's survival hung in the balance. Jesus is the ultimate go-between, bringing us near to God.
