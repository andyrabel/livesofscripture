Solomon (M)

Also called: Jedidiah
Name means “Peaceable; perfect; one who recompenses” — Hebrew
Reigned over the united kingdom, c. 970 BC–931 BC

First named in 2 Samuel 12:24-25

OT United Monarchy

Solomon succeeded David after a contested succession in which his older half-brother Adonijah tried to seize the throne first, blocked by the prophet Nathan and Solomon's mother Bathsheba (1 Kings 1). Early in his reign, God appeared to Solomon in a dream and offered him anything he asked; Solomon requested 'an understanding heart to judge Your people' rather than long life or riches, and God granted both wisdom and, unasked, riches and honor besides (1 Kings 3:5-14). He demonstrated that wisdom at once, resolving a dispute between two women over an infant by proposing to divide it, exposing the true mother by her willingness to give him up (1 Kings 3:16-28). Over seven years, Solomon built the temple in Jerusalem, fulfilling the plan God had given David but not permitted him to build (1 Kings 5-8). His wisdom and wealth became internationally famous, drawing the Queen of Sheba to test him with hard questions, all answered to her astonishment (1 Kings 10:1-13). Scripture is direct about what followed: Solomon loved many foreign women the Law had warned against marrying, and by old age his seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines turned his heart after Ashtoreth, Milcom, and Chemosh, for whom he built high places facing Jerusalem (1 Kings 11:1-8). God's judgment followed: the kingdom would be torn from his son, though not entirely and not in Solomon's lifetime, for David's sake — the split between Israel and Judah that came right after his death (1 Kings 11:9-13; 12).

Solomon became king after his father David died, and when God offered to give him anything he wanted, Solomon wisely asked for wisdom to lead God's people well instead of asking for riches or power. God was so pleased that He gave Solomon wisdom and great wealth besides. Solomon built God's beautiful temple in Jerusalem and became famous around the world for his wisdom. But later in his life, Solomon made poor choices — he married many foreign wives who influenced him to worship false gods instead of staying fully devoted to the one true God. Because of this, trouble came to his family and kingdom after he died. Solomon's story shows that even someone given great wisdom can still make poor choices if they let other things slowly pull their heart away from God.

Thought for Today

Solomon’s story teaches us about wisdom; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 2 Samuel 12:24-25; 1 Kings 1-11; 2 Chronicles 1-9; Proverbs 1:1; Ecclesiastes 1:1
