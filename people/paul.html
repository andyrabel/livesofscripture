Paul (M)

Also called: Saul, Saul of Tarsus

First named in Acts 9

NT Apostolic

Saul of Tarsus was a Pharisee, trained under rabbi Gamaliel, and by his own admission a violent persecutor of the church — he approved of Stephen's stoning, dragged believers to prison, and was on his way to Damascus with letters authorizing further arrests when he met the risen Christ in a blinding light (Acts 7:58-8:3; 9:1-9; Galatians 1:13-14). Scripture does not minimize this; Paul calls it out repeatedly, describing himself as formerly a blasphemer, and later the least of the apostles, unworthy to be called one, for persecuting God's church (1 Corinthians 15:9). His conversion was a direct confrontation with Christ, followed by blindness, healing and baptism through a reluctant disciple, Ananias, and time in Arabia before he preached Jesus as God's Son in the very synagogues where he had hunted Christians (Acts 9:10-22; Galatians 1:15-17). Paul became the central figure of the Gentile mission, planting churches across Asia Minor and Greece and writing letters on justification by faith apart from works of the law (Romans 3-4; Galatians 3). He also had real conflict within the church: a sharp disagreement with Barnabas over John Mark split them into separate teams (Acts 15:36-41), and he confronted Peter at Antioch over gospel inconsistency (Galatians 2:11-14). Paul was repeatedly beaten, imprisoned, shipwrecked, and stoned for his preaching, a catalog he lists as evidence of his apostolic authority (2 Corinthians 11:23-28). He was eventually imprisoned in Rome, where the New Testament narrative ends; church tradition, not Scripture, holds he was later executed under Nero.

Paul, originally named Saul, used to hate and hurt Christians because he thought he was defending God by stopping them. But everything changed when Jesus appeared to him in a bright light on a road, and Paul became one of the most important messengers of the Gospel the world has ever known. Paul traveled to many different countries telling people about Jesus, started many new churches, and wrote a large part of the New Testament in letters explaining what it means to be saved by trusting in Jesus. Paul faced enormous hardships for his faith, including being beaten, shipwrecked, and put in prison, but he never stopped sharing the good news. Paul's story is one of the most dramatic examples in the Bible of someone completely transformed by meeting Jesus.

Thought for Today

Paul’s story teaches us about justification by faith; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Acts 9; Acts 13-28; Galatians 1-2; 1 Corinthians 15:9-10; 2 Corinthians 11:23-28
