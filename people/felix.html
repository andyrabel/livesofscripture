Felix (M)

First named in Acts 23:24-26

NT Apostolic

Felix, the Roman governor of Judea, heard the case against Paul brought by the high priest Ananias and other Jewish leaders after Paul's arrest in Jerusalem, though he "put them off" from a full hearing, having "a more exact knowledge about the Way" than the accusers assumed (Acts 24:1-22). He kept Paul in custody but with relative freedom for his friends to care for him, and later "sent for Paul and heard him speak about faith in Christ Jesus" along with his wife Drusilla, who was Jewish (24:23-24). As Paul "discussed righteousness, self-control and the judgment to come, Felix became frightened and said, 'go away for the present, and when I find time, I will summon you'" (24:25) -- a striking moment where genuine conviction under the gospel's confrontation with sin produced fear rather than repentance. Luke adds a candid, unflattering motive for Felix's continued interest: "he was hoping that money would be given him by Paul," so he sent for him often to converse, but no bribe was ever offered, since Paul would not corrupt his own case that way (24:26). Felix left Paul imprisoned for two full years, unwilling to release him and "wishing to do the Jews a favor," until he was replaced as governor by Porcius Festus (24:27). Felix's story is a sobering portrait of a man who came close enough to the truth to be afraid of it, yet kept indefinitely postponing the response it demanded.

Felix was the Roman governor over Judea when Paul was arrested in Jerusalem and brought to Caesarea for trial. Felix heard the accusations against Paul from Jewish leaders, but he already had a fairly accurate understanding of Christianity and kept postponing a final decision, keeping Paul in a relatively comfortable custody for two years. At one point, Felix and his wife Drusilla sent for Paul privately to hear him speak about faith in Jesus. As Paul spoke about righteousness, self-control, and the coming judgment, Felix became afraid and sent Paul away, saying he would call for him again at a convenient time -- a moment that never quite came. Felix also hoped Paul might offer him a bribe for release, so he kept sending for him to talk, but no bribe ever came, and Paul remained in custody until Felix was eventually replaced as governor.

Thought for Today

Felix’s story warns us about fear of conviction; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Acts 23:24-26; Acts 24:3-27; Acts 25:14
