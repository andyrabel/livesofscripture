Pekah

OT Divided Monarchy

King of Israel who assassinated Pekahiah to seize the throne, joined Aram's king Rezin in a major war against Judah that prompted Ahaz to appeal to Assyria for help, and lost much of Israel's territory to Tiglath-pileser III before being assassinated himself by Hoshea.

For younger readers

Pekah became king of Israel by assassinating Pekahiah, the previous king. During his reign, Pekah teamed up with the king of Aram to attack Judah, in an event the prophet Isaiah spoke about, urging Judah's King Ahaz to trust God instead of panicking. Ahaz didn't listen, and instead appealed to the powerful Assyrian empire for help, which brought Assyria's armies into the region in force. During Pekah's reign, Assyria's king Tiglath-pileser conquered large portions of Israel's territory and carried many people away into exile -- an early stage of the disaster that would soon end the northern kingdom entirely. Pekah himself was eventually assassinated by Hoshea, who became Israel's last king.

Life Story

Pekah, son of Remaliah, assassinated Pekahiah in the royal citadel and reigned twenty years over Israel, continuing the sins of Jeroboam like every northern king before him (2 Kings 15:25-28). His reign is defined largely by a major military alliance with Rezin, king of Aram, against Judah -- the Syro-Ephraimite war -- in which the two kings besieged Jerusalem, killed a great number of Judah's forces, and took captives, though Judah's ally-turned-enemy Assyria would soon overshadow the entire conflict (2 Kings 16:5; 2 Chronicles 28:5-8). This crisis is the backdrop for Isaiah's famous confrontation with King Ahaz of Judah, urging him to trust the LORD rather than fear Pekah and Rezin, whom Isaiah dismissed as two 'stubs of smoldering firebrands' soon to fail (Isaiah 7:1-9). Ahaz instead appealed directly to Tiglath-pileser III of Assyria for help, a decision with lasting consequences (2 Kings 16:7-9). Tiglath-pileser responded by attacking Israel itself during Pekah's reign, capturing large territories including Gilead and Galilee, and deporting their populations to Assyria -- an early, decisive stage of the process that would end the northern kingdom entirely within a generation (15:29). Pekah's reign ended when Hoshea conspired against him, struck him down, and took the throne as Israel's final king (15:30).

First named in 2 Kings 15:25-31

References: 2 Kings 15:25-31; 2 Kings 16:1-9; 2 Chronicles 28:5-8; Isaiah 7:1-9
