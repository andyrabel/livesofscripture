Pharaoh Hophra (M)

Also called: Hophra
Name means “Pharaoh, "great house" (a royal title, not a personal name); Hophra, from the Egyptian Wahibre, "constant is the heart of Ra"” — Egyptian

First named in Jeremiah 37:5-11

OT Exile

Pharaoh Hophra ruled Egypt when Judah's last king, Zedekiah, rebelled against Babylon and looked to Egypt for horses and troops (Ezekiel 17:15-17). While Nebuchadnezzar besieged Jerusalem, Pharaoh's army marched out of Egypt and the Babylonians withdrew. Zedekiah's court took hope, but Jeremiah warned that the Egyptian army would go home and the Babylonians would return and burn the city -- and so it happened (Jeremiah 37:5-11). Ezekiel, among the exiles in Babylon, prophesied against the same Pharaoh in a series of dated oracles: God would drag Egypt's king like a great monster out of his Nile (Ezekiel 29:2-3), break his arms so he could not hold a sword (30:21-25), and cut him down like a towering cedar that fell because its heart was proud (31:2-18); Ezekiel then raised a lament over him going down to the pit with the fallen nations (32:2-32). After Jerusalem fell, a remnant of Judah fled to Egypt against the LORD's word. At Tahpanhes, where Pharaoh had a palace, Jeremiah buried large stones and declared that Nebuchadnezzar would set his throne over them (Jeremiah 43:9-10). Then he named the king: the LORD would give Pharaoh Hophra into the hand of his enemies, just as He had given Zedekiah into Nebuchadnezzar's hand (44:30). Judah had trusted Egypt to rescue it; the prophets warned that Egypt's king could not even save himself.

Pharaoh Hophra was the king of Egypt during the last years of the kingdom of Judah. Judah's king Zedekiah hoped Egypt would save Jerusalem from the Babylonian army. When Pharaoh's army marched out, the Babylonians left for a while, but the prophet Jeremiah warned that they would come back -- and they did, and Jerusalem fell. The prophet Ezekiel compared this proud king to a great river monster and to a tall cedar tree that God would cut down. Later, Jeremiah said by name that God would hand Pharaoh Hophra over to his enemies. These messages warned God's people not to trust a powerful nation instead of trusting the LORD.

Thought for Today

Judah trusted Pharaoh's army to save Jerusalem, but it turned back and the city fell, just as Jeremiah said. Jesus is the only rescuer who never retreats when we need Him.

References: Jeremiah 37:5-11; Jeremiah 43:9; Jeremiah 44:30; Jeremiah 46:17-25; Ezekiel 17:17; Ezekiel 29:2-3; Ezekiel 30:21-25; Ezekiel 31:2-18; Ezekiel 32:2-32
