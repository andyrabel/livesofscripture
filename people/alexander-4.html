Alexander the Coppersmith (M)

First named in 1 Timothy 1:19-20

NT Apostolic

Alexander the coppersmith appears in Paul's second letter to Timothy with a pointed, personal warning: 'Alexander the coppersmith did me much harm; the Lord will repay him according to his deeds. Be on guard against him yourself, for he vigorously opposed our teaching' (2 Timothy 4:14-15). Paul does not specify exactly what harm Alexander caused -- whether physical, legal, or through active undermining of Paul's ministry and message -- but the language suggests direct, personal, and sustained opposition rather than a single incident. Notably, Paul does not call for retaliation of his own; he entrusts the matter of repayment entirely to the Lord's justice, a pattern consistent with Paul's broader teaching elsewhere that vengeance belongs to God, not to believers themselves (Romans 12:19). An Alexander is also mentioned earlier in Paul's first letter to Timothy, among those whose faith had been shipwrecked and whom Paul had 'handed over to Satan, so that they will be taught not to blaspheme' (1 Timothy 1:19-20) -- language describing a serious form of church discipline, likely removal from the fellowship of believers, aimed at correction rather than mere punishment. Whether this is the same Alexander as the coppersmith, or one of several men who shared the common name, Scripture does not say explicitly. Either way, Alexander's brief appearances mark him as a real source of personal and doctrinal opposition to Paul's ministry, whom Paul chose to leave to God's judgment rather than his own.

Alexander the coppersmith is mentioned by Paul in a letter to his younger coworker Timothy. Paul warns Timothy that Alexander did him a great deal of harm and strongly opposed the message Paul was teaching. Rather than trying to get even himself, Paul simply trusts that the Lord will repay Alexander justly for what he did, and tells Timothy to be on guard against him as well. Alexander's brief mention is a reminder that not everyone who encounters the gospel responds well to it, and that believers can choose to leave justice in God's hands rather than take revenge themselves.

References: 1 Timothy 1:19-20; 2 Timothy 4:14-15
