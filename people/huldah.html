Huldah

OT Divided Monarchy

A prophetess consulted by King Josiah's officials after the Book of the Law was rediscovered in the temple, who confirmed its authenticity and warned of coming judgment tempered by mercy for Josiah's humility.

For younger readers

Huldah was a prophetess living in Jerusalem during the reign of King Josiah. When workers repairing the temple found an old scroll -- the Book of the Law that had apparently been lost or forgotten for years -- it was read to King Josiah, and he was deeply troubled, realizing how far the nation had drifted from God's commands. Josiah sent his officials to ask a prophet to confirm what this scroll meant for Judah's future, and they went to Huldah. She confirmed that judgment was indeed coming on Judah for its unfaithfulness, but she also gave Josiah a message of mercy: because his heart was humble and he had responded rightly to God's word, he personally would not see the disaster in his lifetime. Her prophecy was trusted completely by the king's own officials.

Life Story

During Josiah's reign, workers repairing the temple discovered the Book of the Law, apparently lost or neglected for years, and when it was read aloud to Josiah, he tore his clothes in grief, recognizing how far Judah had strayed from its covenant obligations (2 Kings 22:8-13). Josiah sent the high priest Hilkiah and several of his highest officials to "inquire of the LORD" about the book's warnings, and they went specifically to Huldah the prophetess, who lived in Jerusalem's Second Quarter and was married to Shallum, keeper of the wardrobe (22:14). That the king's own delegation of senior officials sought out Huldah, rather than any other available prophet of the period such as Jeremiah or Zephaniah, reflects the genuine authority her prophetic office carried. Huldah confirmed the book's warnings as true: God would indeed bring the disaster described upon Jerusalem and its people for their idolatry (22:15-17). But she also delivered a specific word of mercy for Josiah personally -- because his heart was tender and he humbled himself before the LORD upon hearing the words, he would be gathered to his grave in peace and would not see the coming disaster with his own eyes (22:18-20). Josiah acted immediately on her confirmed word, leading Judah in one of its most sweeping covenant renewals and reforms (23:1-25).
