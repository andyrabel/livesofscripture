Mary (F)

First named in Matthew 1:16-24

NT Gospels

Mary was a young, unmarried woman of Nazareth, betrothed to Joseph, when the angel Gabriel announced she would conceive by the Holy Spirit and bear the Son of God, though she had never been with a man (Luke 1:26-38). Facing the risk of scandal under the law for apparent unfaithfulness, she answered simply, "Behold, the bondslave of the Lord; may it be done to me according to your word," and praised God in the song known as the Magnificat (Luke 1:46-55). She traveled to Bethlehem while pregnant, gave birth to Jesus among animals because there was no room at the inn, and treasured these events in her heart (Luke 2:5-19). She and Joseph fled to Egypt to escape Herod's massacre of infant boys (Matthew 2). At Cana, she prompted Jesus's first miracle, telling the servants, "Whatever He says to you, do it" (John 2:1-5). She bore other sons and daughters with Joseph after Jesus's birth (Matthew 13:55; Mark 6:3). Years later, she stood at the foot of the cross as Jesus died, and He entrusted her care to His disciple John (John 19:26-27). After the resurrection, she joined the apostles in prayer awaiting the Holy Spirit (Acts 1:14) -- her story is one of costly, uncertain obedience to a promise she could not fully understand.

Mary was a young woman from Nazareth engaged to a carpenter named Joseph when an angel named Gabriel appeared and told her she would give birth to God's own Son, Jesus, even though she was still a virgin. Mary was amazed and afraid, but she trusted God and said, "May it be done to me according to your word." She traveled to Bethlehem with Joseph, gave birth to Jesus in a stable, and later fled to Egypt to keep Him safe from a jealous king. Mary raised Jesus, watched Him begin His ministry, and stood near the cross when He died, grieving as any mother would. After Jesus rose from the dead, she prayed together with His followers, waiting for the Holy Spirit.

Thought for Today

Mary said, 'may it be done to me according to your word.' May we trust God that same way, even when we don't understand his plan.
