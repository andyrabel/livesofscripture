Maacah (F)

Also called: Micaiah

First named in 1 Kings 15:2-13

OT Divided Monarchy

Maacah, daughter of Abishalom (also called Uriel of Gibeah, 2 Chronicles 13:2), was a wife of King Rehoboam and the mother of his heir Abijah, who succeeded him as king of Judah (1 Kings 15:2; 2 Chronicles 11:20-22). She retained the influential position of "queen mother" into the reign of her grandson Asa -- Scripture calls her his "mother" as well (1 Kings 15:10), likely reflecting that continued office rather than literal parentage. Maacah used her position to promote idolatry: she "had made a horrid image as an Asherah" (1 Kings 15:13), a wooden cult object associated with pagan fertility worship. When Asa undertook a sweeping religious reform, removing male shrine prostitutes and foreign altars from Judah, he did not spare his own grandmother -- he "also removed Maacah his mother from being queen mother, because she had made a horrid image for the Asherah. Asa cut down her horrid image, crushed it, and burned it at the brook Kidron" (15:13; 2 Chronicles 15:16). This is one of the more striking scenes of reform in the divided kingdom: a young king dismantling his own grandmother's power and destroying her idol without hesitation, even though "the high places were not removed" during his reign in every respect (1 Kings 15:14). Maacah's downfall stands as a warning about idolatry's reach into the highest levels of power, and Asa's response as a model of reform that does not exempt family from accountability to God.

Maacah was the mother of King Abijah of Judah and grandmother of King Asa. She held an important position in the royal court called "queen mother." But Maacah had made an idol to worship, instead of worshiping the one true God. When her grandson Asa became king and began cleaning up Judah's worship, he took a bold stand: he removed his own grandmother from her royal position because of the idol she had made, cut the idol down, smashed it, and burned it. Maacah's story is a reminder that following God sometimes means making hard choices, even about people we love and respect, and that no one -- not even a king's own family -- is above God's standards.

Thought for Today

Asa removed his grandmother Maacah from power for her idolatry, refusing to let family come before God. Jesus taught that love for Him must come before family too.

References: 1 Kings 15:2-13; 2 Chronicles 11:20-22; 2 Chronicles 13:2; 2 Chronicles 15:16
