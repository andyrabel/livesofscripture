Debir (M)

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Debir, king of Eglon, was one of five Amorite kings -- along with Adoni-zedek of Jerusalem, Hoham of Hebron, Piram of Jarmuth, and Japhia of Lachish -- who formed a coalition to attack Gibeon after that city made a peace treaty with Israel (Joshua 10:1-5). Gibeon appealed to Joshua for help, and Joshua's forces marched through the night from Gilgal to relieve the siege (10:6-9). The LORD threw the five kings' armies into confusion, and as Israel pursued them, He sent large hailstones that killed more of the enemy than the sword did, while Joshua famously called on the sun and moon to stand still until Israel had finished avenging itself on its enemies (10:10-14). Debir and the other four kings fled and hid in a cave at Makkedah; Joshua had the cave sealed until the battle was finished, then had the kings brought out, had his army commanders symbolically put their feet on the kings' necks, and personally struck them down, hanging their bodies on trees until evening before burying them in the same cave where they had hidden (10:16-27). Debir's city of Eglon was then captured and destroyed along with the others in Joshua's southern campaign (10:34-39; 12:11-12). His account is part of a larger pattern in Joshua of decisive, complete judgment on Canaan's resisting kings.

Debir was the king of a city called Eglon in the land of Canaan. When the people of Gibeon made a peace agreement with Joshua and the Israelites, Debir joined four other Canaanite kings to attack Gibeon for switching sides. Gibeon asked Joshua for help, and Joshua's army marched all night to defend them. God helped Israel win an amazing victory that day, even making the sun stand still in the sky so there would be enough daylight to finish the battle. Debir and the other four kings tried to hide in a cave, but they were found, captured, and put to death, ending their resistance to Israel's conquest of the land.

References: Joshua 10:3-23; Joshua 12:11
