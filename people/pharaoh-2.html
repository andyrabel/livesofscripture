Pharaoh (M)

First named in Genesis 37:36

OT Patriarchal

This unnamed Pharaoh's two disturbing dreams -- seven gaunt cows devouring seven fat ones, and seven withered heads of grain swallowing seven plump ones -- stumped all of Egypt's magicians and wise men (Genesis 41:1-8). His cupbearer, remembering the Hebrew prisoner who had correctly interpreted his own dream in prison, recommended Joseph (41:9-13). Joseph told Pharaoh the dreams were one message from God: seven years of abundance in Egypt would be followed by seven years of severe famine, and he urged Pharaoh to appoint someone wise to store up grain during the good years (41:25-36). Pharaoh recognized God's Spirit at work in Joseph and set him over all Egypt, second only to himself, saying, "without your permission no one shall raise his hand or foot in all the land of Egypt" (41:37-44). He gave Joseph an Egyptian name and a wife, and Joseph's authority ended up saving not only Egypt but the surrounding nations, including his own family, from starvation (41:45; 45:9-11). When Joseph's brothers and then his father Jacob came to Egypt, Pharaoh welcomed them, granted them the fertile land of Goshen, and personally received and blessed Jacob (45:16-20; 47:1-10). Acts 7:10 later credits God with giving Joseph favor and wisdom "before Pharaoh, king of Egypt." This Pharaoh's generosity toward Joseph's family set the stage for Israel's centuries in Egypt -- a story that would end generations later under a very different Pharaoh, one who "did not know Joseph" (Exodus 1:8).

This Pharaoh, king of Egypt, had two troubling dreams that none of his wise men could explain -- one about seven skinny cows eating seven fat cows, and one about seven thin heads of grain swallowing seven full ones. His cupbearer remembered Joseph, a Hebrew prisoner with a gift for interpreting dreams. Joseph explained that Egypt would have seven years of great harvests followed by seven years of terrible famine, and suggested storing up grain to prepare. Impressed, Pharaoh put Joseph in charge of the whole country, second only to himself. Years later, when Joseph's family came to Egypt because of the famine, Pharaoh welcomed them warmly and gave them good land to live on.

Thought for Today

Pharaoh lifted Joseph from prisoner to ruler because he saw God's Spirit in him. Jesus was raised from death to the highest place to save the world.
