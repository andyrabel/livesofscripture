Ananias (M)

First named in Acts 23:2

NT Apostolic

Ananias served as high priest when Paul was brought before the Jewish Sanhedrin in Jerusalem after his arrest in the temple. As Paul opened his defense by declaring he had lived before God "in all good conscience," Ananias ordered those standing near Paul to strike him on the mouth (Acts 23:1-2). Paul responded sharply, "God is going to strike you, you whitewashed wall! Do you sit to try me according to the Law, and in violation of the Law order me to be struck?" -- then, told he had spoken against the high priest, Paul acknowledged he had not recognized him and cited the command not to speak evil of a ruler of the people (23:3-5). Ananias next appears in Acts 24:1, traveling to Caesarea with elders and an attorney named Tertullus to formally accuse Paul before the Roman governor Felix, charging him with being a troublemaker and temple-desecrator. Historically, Ananias son of Nedebaeus was a controversial high priest known for corruption and violence, later killed by Jewish rebels at the outset of the war with Rome in AD 66. His conduct toward Paul -- ordering an illegal assault mid-trial and later pursuing a politically motivated prosecution -- exemplifies the very abuse of religious power the prophets and Jesus both condemned.

Ananias was the high priest in Jerusalem when Paul was arrested and brought to stand before the Jewish council. When Paul began to speak, Ananias ordered the men standing near him to strike Paul on the mouth. Paul responded sharply, but then apologized when he realized Ananias was the high priest, since Scripture teaches respect for rulers. Later, Ananias traveled all the way to Caesarea with a lawyer to bring formal charges against Paul before the Roman governor. His story shows how some religious leaders misused their positions of authority instead of using them to seek justice and truth.

Thought for Today

Ananias abused his religious office, ordering Paul struck unlawfully mid-trial. Jesus, the perfect High Priest, never used His authority to harm the innocent but to save them.

References: Acts 23:2; Acts 24:1
