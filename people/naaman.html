Naaman (M)

OT Divided Monarchy

Naaman commanded the army of Aram (Syria) and was highly honored by his king, "but he was a leper" (2 Kings 5:1). A young Israelite girl, taken captive in a raid and serving Naaman's wife, told her mistress of a prophet in Samaria who could heal him (5:2-3). Naaman traveled to Israel bearing enormous wealth and a letter from his king, but when he arrived, Elisha did not even come out to meet him -- he simply sent a messenger instructing Naaman to wash seven times in the Jordan (5:9-10). Naaman was furious, expecting a dramatic display and finding Israel's rivers less impressive than his homeland's, and nearly left in rage; only his servants' reasoning -- that he would gladly have done something difficult, so why not something simple? -- persuaded him to try (5:11-13). He washed seven times and "his flesh was restored like the flesh of a young boy," and Naaman returned to declare, "now I know that there is no God in all the earth, but in Israel" (5:14-15). He asked to take Israelite soil home to worship the LORD on it, and Elisha sent him away in peace (5:17-19). Naaman's healing is one of Scripture's clearest pictures of God's grace reaching a powerful outsider, contingent not on status or wealth but on humbling himself to a simple act of obedient faith -- a story Jesus Himself later cited as an example of God's grace extending beyond Israel (Luke 4:27).

Naaman was a respected army commander for the king of Syria, but he suffered from leprosy, a serious skin disease. A young Israelite servant girl, captured during a raid, told Naaman's wife about a prophet in Israel who could heal him. Naaman traveled to Israel with expensive gifts, expecting a dramatic ceremony, but Elisha simply sent a messenger telling him to wash seven times in the Jordan River. Naaman was insulted at first, since it seemed too plain and simple, and he almost left angry. But his servants convinced him to try it, and when he humbly obeyed, he was completely healed. Naaman then declared that he would worship only the God of Israel from then on. His story shows that God's grace and healing reach even powerful people from outside Israel, and that pride can get in the way of receiving it.

References: Genesis 46:21; Numbers 26:40; 1 Chronicles 8:4
