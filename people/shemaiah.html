Shemaiah (M)

Name means “That hears or obeys the Lord” — Hebrew
Prophesied to Judah, c. 931 BC–913 BC

First named in 1 Kings 12:22

OT Divided Monarchy

Shemaiah was a prophet active in Judah during the reign of Rehoboam, Solomon's son, right at the moment the united kingdom split in two. When ten tribes rejected Rehoboam's harsh rule and made Jeroboam king over Israel, Rehoboam assembled 180,000 fighting men from Judah and Benjamin to force the northern tribes back by war. Before the army could march, "the word of God came to Shemaiah," instructing him to tell Rehoboam and all Israel, "You must not go up and fight against your relatives... for this thing is from Me" (1 Kings 12:21-24). Remarkably, the army listened and went home -- a rare instance in Scripture of a king's forces standing down entirely because of a prophet's word. Years later, after Rehoboam's kingdom "abandoned the law of the LORD," Shemaiah returned with a harder message: Egypt's King Shishak would invade Jerusalem because Judah had forsaken God (2 Chronicles 12:1-5). When Rehoboam and Judah's leaders humbled themselves, admitting "the LORD is righteous," the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah again -- God would not destroy them, though He would still let Shishak take some measure of Jerusalem's wealth, so that Judah would "learn the difference between My service and the service of the kingdoms of the countries" (12:6-8). The Chronicler notes that Shemaiah also kept a written record of Rehoboam's reign (12:15).

When Israel's kingdom split into two after Solomon died, King Rehoboam of Judah gathered a huge army to fight the northern tribes and force them back under his rule. But God sent the prophet Shemaiah with a message: do not go to war against your own relatives, because this split happened as part of God's plan. Rehoboam and his soldiers obeyed and went home instead of fighting. Later, when Rehoboam and Judah stopped following God, Shemaiah came again and warned that a foreign king named Shishak would invade as a consequence. Rehoboam and Judah's leaders humbled themselves and admitted that the LORD was right. Because they humbled themselves, God softened the punishment instead of destroying Jerusalem completely.

Thought for Today

Shemaiah's word from God stopped Rehoboam's army from attacking their own relatives in civil war. May we listen for God's voice before rushing into conflict with others.

References: 1 Kings 12:22; 2 Chronicles 11:2; 2 Chronicles 12:5-15
