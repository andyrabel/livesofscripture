Nimrod (M)

First named in Genesis 10:8-12

OT Primeval History

Nimrod appears in the Table of Nations as a grandson of Ham through Cush, described as 'a mighty one on the earth' and 'a mighty hunter before the LORD,' a phrase whose tone -- honor or irony -- interpreters read differently (Genesis 10:8-9). The beginning of his kingdom was Babel, Erech, Accad, and Calneh in the land of Shinar, from which he went to Assyria and built Nineveh and other cities (10:10-12). Nimrod is Scripture's first person described in terms of an empire -- not a family or tribe but consolidated political power across multiple cities and territories. His prominence in Shinar sets the geographical and thematic stage for Genesis 11's very next episode, the tower of Babel, where humanity in that same region says 'let us make for ourselves a name' and attempts to build a tower to the heavens rather than scatter and fill the earth as God had commanded (Genesis 9:1; 11:1-9). Genesis never states that Nimrod personally built the tower, but his kingdom-building ambition in the same place and among the same early post-flood population frames Babel as the natural outgrowth of the drive to self-glorifying power his story represents -- a warning, in the middle of Genesis's earliest chapters, that human ambition unmoored from trust in God tends toward pride and eventual judgment.

Nimrod was a powerful leader described in Genesis as a 'mighty hunter' and a mighty man on the earth. He built some of the very first great cities and kingdoms, including Babel, in the land of Shinar. Nimrod's story comes right before the famous story of the Tower of Babel, where the people of that same region tried to build a tower to make a name for themselves instead of trusting God. Nimrod's ambition to build a great human kingdom is a reminder that impressive human power and achievement are never a substitute for depending on God.

Thought for Today

Nimrod’s story teaches us about human ambition; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Genesis 10:8-12; 1 Chronicles 1:10; Micah 5:6
