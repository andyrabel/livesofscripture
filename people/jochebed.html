Jochebed (F)

First named in Exodus 2:1-10

OT Exodus/Wilderness

Jochebed gave birth to Moses under Pharaoh's standing order that every Hebrew baby boy be thrown into the Nile, and 'seeing that he was a fine child,' she hid him for three months — an act of faith the New Testament credits jointly to Moses' parents, who 'were not afraid of the king's edict' (Exodus 1:22-2:2; Hebrews 11:23). When she could no longer keep him hidden, she wove a papyrus basket, coated it with tar and pitch, and placed it among the reeds — aiming his exposure toward the very place Pharaoh's household drew its water, using the river Pharaoh had weaponized against Hebrew sons as the means of saving one instead (Exodus 2:3). She sent her daughter Miriam to watch from a distance. When Pharaoh's daughter found the basket, she had compassion on the child despite her father's decree, and Miriam offered to find a Hebrew nurse, fetching her own mother (Exodus 2:4-8). In one of Scripture's sharper ironies, Pharaoh's daughter then paid Jochebed wages to nurse her own son, and Jochebed raised Moses under the unwitting protection of the very household that had ordered his death, until he was old enough to be brought to Pharaoh's daughter to become, legally, her son (Exodus 2:9-10). Exodus 6:20 identifies her as Amram's father's sister as well as his wife, and mother not only of Moses but of Aaron and Miriam — one woman's quiet courage setting in motion Israel's entire deliverance story.

Jochebed was Moses' mother, and she showed incredible bravery and cleverness when Pharaoh ordered that all Hebrew baby boys be killed. Instead of giving up, she hid baby Moses as long as she could, then made a waterproof basket and placed him carefully in the river, trusting God with the outcome. Her plan worked out in an amazing way — Pharaoh's own daughter found and rescued Moses, and even ended up paying Jochebed to take care of her own baby! Jochebed's courage protected the boy who would grow up to become Israel's great deliverer. Her story shows how one parent's brave faith and quick thinking, even in a truly frightening situation, can be part of God's much bigger plan.

Thought for Today

Jochebed’s story warns us about faith under persecution; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: Exodus 2:1-10; Exodus 6:20; Numbers 26:59; Hebrews 11:23
