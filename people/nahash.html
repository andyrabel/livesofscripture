Nahash (M)

Name means “Snake; serpent” — Hebrew

First named in 1 Samuel 11:1-2

OT United Monarchy

Nahash, king of the Ammonites, besieged the Israelite town of Jabesh-gilead early in Saul's reign. When the town's elders sought terms of surrender, Nahash's demand was deliberately cruel: 'On this condition I will make it with you, that I will gouge out the right eye of every one of you, thus I will make it a reproach on all Israel' (1 Samuel 11:2). Word of the crisis reached Saul, who had just been anointed king, and it became the occasion for his first decisive royal act: he summoned Israel's tribes with a graphic call to arms, gathered a large army, and delivered Jabesh-gilead in a surprise attack that routed the Ammonites 'so that no two of them were left together' (11:3-11). This victory cemented popular support for Saul's kingship (11:12-15). Samuel later referenced Nahash's threat as part of Israel's history of God's deliverance when reviewing the nation's request for a king (12:12). Years later, in David's reign, it emerges that Nahash had shown David some unspecified kindness (2 Samuel 10:2), prompting David to send condolences and a delegation of respect to Nahash's son Hanun upon Nahash's death -- an overture Hanun humiliated, triggering a war between Israel and the Ammonites (10:1-19; 1 Chronicles 19:1-19).

Nahash was the king of Ammon, a nation near Israel. He surrounded an Israelite town called Jabesh-gilead and made a terrible demand: the people could only make peace with him if he could poke out the right eye of every person in the city. The frightened townspeople sent for help, and this crisis became the moment when Saul, freshly anointed as Israel's first king, rallied the tribes of Israel together and rescued the city, winning the people's full support as their new king. Interestingly, later in the Bible we learn that Nahash had shown some kindness to David, so David tried to return that kindness to Nahash's son after Nahash died.

Thought for Today

Nahash’s story warns us about cruelty and threat; may we look to Jesus, whose grace and truth never fail.

References: 1 Samuel 11:1-2; 1 Samuel 12:12; 2 Samuel 10:2; 2 Samuel 17:27; 1 Chronicles 19:1-2
